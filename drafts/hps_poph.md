### 03-10 · Biliary atresia: HPS and portopulmonary hypertension (synced 2026-11-01)
This is the main lesson for the card. Every number — TODO(verify) against the cited paper at build. Diagnostic thresholds are **adult-derived** (ILTS/ERS/ESC); listing thresholds are **US OPTN policy**; no pediatric definition exists — say so on the card ("Adult criteria; no pediatric validation"). Spell US English at build (pediatric, dyspnea, anemia, anesthesiologist, operating room).
- **Mechanism (slide 3, for Why):** the driver is portosystemic shunting, not loss of synthetic function — gut mediators bypass the liver (endothelin-1 → endothelial ET-B → eNOS NO; macrophage HO-1/CO; VEGF angiogenesis). HPS = pre-capillary/capillary dilatation (diffusion–perfusion defect + true shunts), basal → orthodeoxia. POPH = vasoconstriction + plexogenic arteriopathy → PVR ↑ → RV failure, syncope, sudden death. Occurs with normal albumin/INR, in non-cirrhotic PHT and congenital portosystemic shunts (closing the shunt helps); PELD/MELD did not predict HPS (Kim KY et al. Sci Rep 2021;11:4134). → The child at risk is the jaundice-free native-liver survivor with portal hypertension.
- **HPS definition (slide 4; ILTS, Krowka MJ et al. Transplantation 2016;100:1440–52; ERS, Rodriguez-Roisin R et al. Eur Respir J 2004;24:861–80):** liver disease or PHT + intrapulmonary vascular dilatation (contrast echo: left-heart bubbles ≥ 3 cardiac cycles after the right) + A–a gradient ≥ 15 mmHg on room air, sitting. Tc-MAA brain uptake > 6% supports (useful with lung disease). Severity by PaO2: ≥ 80 mild · 60–79 moderate · 50–59 severe · < 50 very severe.
- **POPH definition (slides 4, 10; ILTS 2025 update, DuBrock HM et al. Liver Transpl 2026;32:296–314, aligned with ESC/ERS 2022, Humbert M et al. Eur Heart J 2022):** PHT + right-heart catheter (RHC) mPAP > 20 mmHg, PAWP ≤ 15, PVR > 2 WU (ILTS 2016: mPAP > 25, PVR > 3). Severity mPAP 20–34 mild · 35–44 moderate · ≥ 45 severe. Echo trigger for RHC lowered: RVSP > 40 mmHg (was > 50), TR velocity > 2.8 m/s, or RV dilatation/dysfunction. (2016 PAWP < 15, 2025 ≤ 15.) Team proposal, not a lesson verdict: a `guideline_compare` row "ILTS 2016 vs 2025" (DIFFERS — earlier disease qualifies). Who to screen for POPH (slide 10): all LT and TIPS candidates; yearly echo on the waiting list is reasonable. Echo RV estimates less reliable in small children → pediatric cardiologist; look at septal flattening, RV size, pulmonary acceleration time.
- **Frequency and timing (slides 2, 5, 6) — Learn only, each figure with its own cohort:** Japanese BA registry HPS 1.9% (71), POPH 0.7% (27) of 3,777 (Okubo R et al. Pediatr Surg Int 2025;41:324); Osaka HPS 6.8%, POPH 3.4% of 88 (Ueno T 2017); Seoul HPS 8/120 = 6.7% (Kim 2021 — **resolves the 02-13 TODO: lesson now says "7% (8/120)", not 10/120**; still confirm in the paper); Toronto HPS 2/26 with cirrhosis/PHT on echo, 2/305 overall (Noli K et al. Pediatrics 2008;121:e522–7); Essen IPVD 40% of 45 cirrhotic children (Hoerning A et al. J Pediatr 2014;164:546–52); Toronto 17% had IPVD with normal blood gases (slide 5). HPS median onset 4 y (Osaka); POPH median 15 y (Osaka), 13.7 y (Denver, Condino AA et al. J Pediatr 2005;147:20–6); Denver: median 12 y from PHT diagnosis to POPH. → **Surveillance continues into adolescence** in every native-liver survivor with PHT.
- **BASM / polysplenia (slide 7):** polysplenia or interrupted IVC in 3 of 8 BA with HPS vs 1 of 112 without — the only independent risk factor (Kim 2021). → lower threshold for contrast echo; look for a congenital portosystemic shunt on Doppler/CT; map IVC, hepatic and portal veins before listing. Pearl: "BASM + unexplained hypoxemia = HPS until proven otherwise, even with normal liver tests."
- **Oximetry limits (slide 8):** ILTS — SpO2 < 96% at rest is a reasonable trigger for a blood gas; AASLD/AST/NASPGHAN 2026 — measure supine and upright; orthodeoxia ≥ 4% fall on standing (TODO(verify) source of the 4% figure). Essen: every IPVD-positive child had SpO2 > 98%; capillary gas sensitivity 94%, specificity 53% for raised A–a gradient. Two Essen children with moderate HPS died before transplant. Oximetry never screens for POPH. → **Threshold conflict to resolve at build:** lesson 02-13 attributes "< 97%" to Martinez 2026, which is the same AASLD/AST/NASPGHAN 2026 guideline that lesson 03-10 slide 16 says names "oximetry only" with no number — the two lessons contradict each other about one document. 03-10 cites ILTS < 96%. Check the AASLD/AST/NASPGHAN 2026 full text; if it gives no number, use ILTS < 96% and record in DECISIONS. → **Posture conflict to resolve at build:** slide 17 says "sitting and standing"; slide 8 and the 02-13/03-08 entries say "supine and upright" (AASLD/AST/NASPGHAN 2026); orthodeoxia is classically supine → upright. Resolve from the guideline text; record in DECISIONS. The other 03-10 entries (chol_0_3m, splenomegaly_ph, varix_bleed) point here.
- **Confirm HPS stepwise (slide 9):** suspect (clubbing, cyanosis, dyspnea, platypnea; SpO2 < 96% or fall on standing; any LT candidate) → arterial gas room air sitting (capillary gas = sensitive, less specific substitute) → contrast echo → MAA if lung disease → grade by PaO2; exclude congenital portosystemic shunt. Pitfalls: early bubbles (< 3 cycles) = intracardiac shunt (PFO common in infants); positive bubbles + normal gas = IPVD, not HPS → follow, don't list; anemia, pneumonia, atelectasis from ascites lower SpO2 for other reasons.
- **Transplant (slides 11, 12, 15):** HPS exception — PaO2 < 60 mmHg room air, shunt shown, no significant lung disease (OPTN policy — TODO(verify) current policy text). SRTR 124 children with HPS exception: 5-y survival 88.7% vs 93.7%; PaO2 < 50 89.4%, 50–59 92.1%, 60–69 76.5% (p = 0.13); 87.9% had MELD/PELD < 15 (Raza MH et al. Liver Transpl 2023;29:134–44). → "Severity is a reason to list promptly, not to decline." After LT: HPS resolved in all 15 Birmingham children (Warner S et al. Liver Transpl 2018;24:1271–9); MAA < 6% at ~7.7 mo (Seoul); oxygen may be needed for months; no proven medical therapy — keep SpO2 > 88% with oxygen (TODO(verify) source); tell transplant anesthesiologist/intensivist early (iNO, Trendelenburg, prone, ECMO). Hb ≥ 130 g/L linked to longer PICU stay; de novo HPS after living-donor LT and with graft-outflow problems (slide 12) → route to `post_lt`. OPTN HPS exception also requires documented portal hypertension (TODO(verify)).
- **POPH treatment and listing (slides 13–15; adult data, pediatric PH team):** stop non-selective beta-blockers (lower cardiac output) → band ligation; avoid TIPS. mPAP < 45: monotherapy (PDE5 inhibitor — preferred in advanced liver disease — or ERA); ≥ 45: upfront combination, including a prostacyclin analogue when severe or with RV failure. Under OPTN policy, repeat the catheter every 3 months while on the exception (slide 13). Bosentan — most hepatotoxic ERA, monitor LFTs. OPTN POPH exception: baseline mPAP ≥ 35 and PVR > 3 WU, treated to mPAP < 35 with PVR < 5, or mPAP 35–45 with PVR < 3 (TODO(verify) OPTN text). mPAP ≥ 45–50 or PVR > 5 WU or severe RV dysfunction → absolute contraindication to LT (ILTS 2025). Pediatric mortality: Denver 4/7, Osaka 2/3, Geneva 0/6 with upfront combination (Joye R et al. J Hepatol 2021;74:742–7). No drug doses on the card — refer to the PH team (§6: no dose without a source; record in DECISIONS at build).
- **Red flags for the card:** syncope or chest pain on exertion in a child with PHT (slide 20: "probable POPH"; team wording "POPH until a catheter says otherwise") — never to anesthesia or the operating room on echo estimates alone; warn the anesthesiologist in advance (slides 14, 18); SpO2 < 96% or fall on standing; new clubbing ("clubbing is never asthma"); cyanosis.
- **Surveillance frame (slide 17 — lesson: "pragmatic schedule built from timing data"; label "Expert framework"):** every visit — SpO2 sitting and standing, clubbing, cyanosis, exercise tolerance, syncope, spleen and platelets → SpO2 < 96%, fall on standing, new clubbing · PHT established — yearly contrast echo with RV pressure estimate + capillary/arterial gas → late bubbles; RVSP > 40 or TRV > 2.8 · BASM or portosystemic shunt — contrast echo at PHT diagnosis; Doppler/CT for shunts · adolescence — continue yearly echo; ask about syncope and chest pain · transplant evaluation — gas, contrast echo, echo RV pressure, MAA if lung disease, RHC if triggered. Write SpO2 (both postures) into every clinic letter — a trend 98 → 95% over two years is the earliest sign.
- **Guideline compare (slide 16):** screen LT candidates for HPS ALIGNED (ILTS / EASL 2016 vascular liver diseases, J Hepatol 2016;64:179–202 / AASLD-AST-NASPGHAN 2026); how to screen DIFFERS; echo trigger for RHC DIFFERS; NSBB/TIPS in POPH ALIGNED; surveillance of native-liver survivors GAP; pediatric thresholds MATERIAL (lesson verdict — at build apply the §5.2 rule: "no pediatric threshold exists" is a GAP by schema; lesson's MATERIAL reading → `note`; record in DECISIONS).
- **Traps (slide 18):** "good liver, low PELD"; "normal SpO2"; "asthma" label for dyspnea + clubbing; propranolol continued in POPH; the operating room without a catheter. Biggest: treating HPS/POPH as a pulmonary curiosity rather than a transplant decision — involve the transplant center at diagnosis.
