### 03-12 · Biliary atresia: transplantation (synced 2026-11-05)
BA is the commonest pediatric LT indication; this lesson supplies the early-complication and long-term context for the card. Every number — TODO(verify) against the cited paper at build. The companion **AASLD/AST/NASPGHAN pediatric LT post-transplant management guideline** (Liver Transpl, doi 10.1097/LVT.0000000000000939 — found by web search 2026-11-05, not cited by the lesson; TODO(verify) authors/year/full text) is the likely primary guideline for this card — check at build.
- **Hepatic artery thrombosis (HAT) — the red flag for the early window (slide 12; Ebel NH et al. J Pediatr 2020;226:195–201, SPLIT 1995–2016, 3,801 first LTs):** HAT within 90 days in 7.4%, highest risk < 1 year of age; after HAT, graft failure by 90 days aHR 11.9 (about half lost the graft), death by 90 days aHR 6.2; 20.7% of retransplanted children thrombosed again. Technical-variant grafts (split, reduced, living donor) had *lower* odds than whole grafts (OR 0.59, P < 0.001 — adult-caliber arterial stump, microscope anastomosis). Standard practice per lesson: daily hepatic-artery Doppler in the first week, low threshold for re-exploration. → Red flags / algorithm: "First weeks after LT + rising ALT or bile leak → urgent Doppler" (TODO(verify) wording against the post-LT guideline).
- **Prior Kasai → more biliary and infectious complications, same survival (slide 8; Dong H et al. J Clin Med 2025;14:3328, n = 475):** biliary complications 2.0% (no Kasai) / 6.6% (Kasai < 90 d) / 10.8% (> 90 d); infection 18.5 / 32.1 / 29.7%; graft loss 4.6 / 4.5 / 13.5% (Kasai not independent on multivariable analysis). Lemoine 2022 (J Clin Med 2022;11:3012): 3-y survival 92.9% vs 90.7%, P = 0.80. → Why: adhesions and Roux limb explain the biliary/bowel complications.
- **Splenic malformation and complex vascular anomalies (slides 13–14; Anderson CD et al. Am J Transplant 2008;8:1197–204, SPLIT, 944 BA LTs):** 6.7% had complex vascular anomalies; 1-y patient survival 83% vs 93%, graft survival not different; more reoperations, biliary leaks and bowel perforations; infection the leading cause of death; pre-LT portal vein thrombosis and preduodenal portal vein predicted death. Asplenia/functional hyposplenism → encapsulated-bacteria risk: vaccines and, where indicated, prophylaxis. → Don't miss: "Fever after LT in a child with BASM/asplenia — treat as sepsis with encapsulated organisms in mind" (team synthesis; TODO(verify) source before publishing).
- **Infection dominates mortality (slide 6; Utterson EC et al. J Pediatr 2005;147:180–5):** SPLIT 567 BA children transplanted, 3-y survival 88%; infection 37% of post-LT deaths; pre-LT growth < −2 SD predicted death and graft loss.
- **Graft type and long-term survival (slides 9–11):** UNOS 2010–2019 left lateral segment for BA (Dalzell C et al. Transpl Int 2022;35:10437): 5-y graft survival living donor 94% (n = 291) vs split 81% (n = 157), P = 0.004; patient 97% vs 92%, P = 0.055; **all recipients ≤ 10 kg (any diagnosis, not the BA subgroup)** 5-y graft survival 88% vs 76%, P = 0.011; unadjusted, no hazard ratios. Japan 1989–2015, 2,085 living-donor LTs for BA (Kasahara M et al. Am J Transplant 2018;18:659–68): graft survival 90.5 / 90.4 / 84.6 / 82.0 / 79.9% at 1 / 5 / 10 / 15 / 20 y; **recipients aged 12–17 had the worst long-term graft survival**. → Why.
- **Adolescence is where late grafts are lost (slides 11, 19, 21):** non-adherence, late rejection, transition. Pitfall: "Treating adolescents like toddlers." Case: adolescent 10 years after LT with missed tacrolimus levels → adherence plan, transition clinic. → In the late-window algorithm, erratic levels + rising ALT → think rejection from non-adherence first.
- **Vaccines:** live vaccines cannot be given after LT (immunosuppression) — check what was completed before transplant (up to 71% arrive incomplete per lesson — TODO(verify) source).

### 03-13 · Biliary atresia: the adult with BA (synced 2026-11-05)
- **Transition of LT recipients (slide 7; Vajro P et al. ESPGHAN/EASL, JPGN 2018;66:976–90; AASLD/AST long-term care guidance, Kelly DA et al. Liver Transpl 2013;19:798–825):** never transfer during rejection or graft dysfunction; joint clinic; screen adherence, mood, alcohol — late graft loss clusters in adolescents and young adults.

- **Adult LT for BA (slide 10; Cortes-Cerisuelo M et al. J Clin Med 2021;10:4969, King's, 34 adults, median age 24):** graft survival 97.1% (1 y) / 91.2% (10 y), patient 100% / 94%; spontaneous portosystemic shunts 73%, enlarged splenic artery 58%, splenic artery aneurysms 21%; median 460 min, bowel injury 9%, **biliary complications 21%**. Indications: synthetic failure 32%, recurrent cholangitis 29%, progressive jaundice 18%.

### 04-01 · Alagille syndrome I: genetics (synced 2026-11-07)
- **Related living donor for Alagille syndrome (slides 14–15):** the parent is the likeliest donor and the likeliest carrier. Kyoto series (Kasahara 2003): 20 living-related grafts; 5 parental donors excluded for bile duct paucity on biopsy, 1 for a hepatic vascular anomaly; 3 recipient deaths — 1 from a graft with unsuspected paucity, 1 surgical, 1 progressive pulmonary artery stenosis; 5-year patient survival 80.4%.
- Donor rule (expert opinion; GeneReviews 2024, Kasahara 2023 review): variant known → targeted test of donor; carrier → exclude; negative → still examine (face, echo, eye, spine, liver tests) because of mosaicism; variant unknown → full clinical screen and consider donor biopsy; CT/MR angiography of donor vessels; prefer an unrelated or deceased graft when uncertain. Stop rule (slide 15): no related donor proceeds without either a negative targeted test plus a normal examination (incl. echo and vascular imaging), or — variant unknown — a normal examination and a normal donor biopsy. No society guideline grades this (MATERIAL gap, slide 16).
