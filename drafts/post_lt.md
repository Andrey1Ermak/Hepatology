### 03-12 · Biliary atresia: transplantation (synced 2026-11-05)
BA is the commonest pediatric LT indication; this lesson supplies the early-complication and long-term context for the card. Every number — TODO(verify) against the cited paper at build. The companion **AASLD/AST/NASPGHAN pediatric LT post-transplant management guideline** (Liver Transpl, doi 10.1097/LVT.0000000000000939 — found by web search 2026-11-05, not cited by the lesson; TODO(verify) authors/year/full text) is the likely primary guideline for this card — check at build.
- **Hepatic artery thrombosis (HAT) — the red flag for the early window (slide 12; Ebel NH et al. J Pediatr 2020;226:195–201, SPLIT 1995–2016, 3,801 first LTs):** HAT within 90 days in 7.4%, highest risk < 1 year of age; after HAT, graft failure by 90 days aHR 11.9 (about half lost the graft), death by 90 days aHR 6.2; 20.7% of retransplanted children thrombosed again. Technical-variant grafts (split, reduced, living donor) had *lower* odds than whole grafts (OR 0.59, P < 0.001 — adult-caliber arterial stump, microscope anastomosis). Standard practice per lesson: daily hepatic-artery Doppler in the first week, low threshold for re-exploration. → Red flags / algorithm: "First weeks after LT + rising ALT or bile leak → urgent Doppler" (TODO(verify) wording against the post-LT guideline).
- **Prior Kasai → more biliary and infectious complications, same survival (slide 8; Dong H et al. J Clin Med 2025;14:3328, n = 475):** biliary complications 2.0% (no Kasai) / 6.6% (Kasai < 90 d) / 10.8% (> 90 d); infection 18.5 / 32.1 / 29.7%; graft loss 4.6 / 4.5 / 13.5% (Kasai not independent on multivariable analysis). Lemoine 2022 (J Clin Med 2022;11:3012): 3-y survival 92.9% vs 90.7%, P = 0.80. → Why: adhesions and Roux limb explain the biliary/bowel complications.
- **Splenic malformation and complex vascular anomalies (slides 13–14; Anderson CD et al. Am J Transplant 2008;8:1197–204, SPLIT, 944 BA LTs):** 6.7% had complex vascular anomalies; 1-y patient survival 83% vs 93%, graft survival not different; more reoperations, biliary leaks and bowel perforations; infection the leading cause of death; pre-LT portal vein thrombosis and preduodenal portal vein predicted death. Asplenia/functional hyposplenism → encapsulated-bacteria risk: vaccines and, where indicated, prophylaxis. → Don't miss: "Fever after LT in a child with BASM/asplenia — treat as sepsis with encapsulated organisms in mind" (team synthesis; TODO(verify) source before publishing).
- **Infection dominates mortality (slide 6; Utterson EC et al. J Pediatr 2005;147:180–5):** SPLIT 567 BA children transplanted, 3-y survival 88%; infection 37% of post-LT deaths; pre-LT growth < −2 SD predicted death and graft loss.
- **Graft type and long-term survival (slides 9–11):** UNOS 2010–2019 left lateral segment for BA (Dalzell C et al. Transpl Int 2022;35:10437): 5-y graft survival living donor 94% (n = 291) vs split 81% (n = 157), P = 0.004; patient 97% vs 92%, P = 0.055; **all recipients ≤ 10 kg (any diagnosis, not the BA subgroup)** 5-y graft survival 88% vs 76%, P = 0.011; unadjusted, no hazard ratios. Japan 1989–2015, 2,085 living-donor LTs for BA (Kasahara M et al. Am J Transplant 2018;18:659–68): graft survival 90.5 / 90.4 / 84.6 / 82.0 / 79.9% at 1 / 5 / 10 / 15 / 20 y; **recipients aged 12–17 had the worst long-term graft survival**. → Why.
- **Adolescence is where late grafts are lost (slides 11, 19, 21):** non-adherence, late rejection, transition. Pitfall: "Treating adolescents like toddlers." Case: adolescent 10 years after LT with missed tacrolimus levels → adherence plan, transition clinic. → In the late-window algorithm, erratic levels + rising ALT → think rejection from non-adherence first.
- **Vaccines:** live vaccines cannot be given after LT (immunosuppression) — check what was completed before transplant (up to 71% arrive incomplete per lesson — TODO(verify) source).

### 04-01 · Alagille syndrome I: genetics (synced 2026-11-07)
- **Related living donor for Alagille syndrome (slides 14–15):** the parent is the likeliest donor and the likeliest carrier. Kyoto series (Kasahara 2003): 20 living-related grafts; 5 parental donors excluded for bile duct paucity on biopsy, 1 for a hepatic vascular anomaly; 3 recipient deaths — 1 from a graft with unsuspected paucity, 1 surgical, 1 progressive pulmonary artery stenosis; 5-year patient survival 80.4%.
- Donor rule (expert opinion; GeneReviews 2024, Kasahara 2023 review): variant known → targeted test of donor; carrier → exclude; negative → still examine (face, echo, eye, spine, liver tests) because of mosaicism; variant unknown → full clinical screen and consider donor biopsy; CT/MR angiography of donor vessels; prefer an unrelated or deceased graft when uncertain. Stop rule (slide 15): no related donor proceeds without either a negative targeted test plus a normal examination (incl. echo and vascular imaging), or — variant unknown — a normal examination and a normal donor biopsy. No society guideline grades this (MATERIAL gap, slide 16).

### 04-02 · Alagille syndrome II: clinical criteria (synced 2026-11-08)
- **Never clear a related donor by classic criteria (Kamath 2003):** of 53 relatives carrying the proband's JAG1 variant, 47% did not meet classic criteria and 4% had no feature at all; penetrance ~96% with variable expressivity. Reinforces the 04-01 donor rule: variant test, not phenotype.
- **Before LT in AGS:** right ventricular pressure known (echo/cardiology; intracardiac disease halves 20-year survival — Emerick 1999); kidney (39% renal involvement, Kamath 2012) assessed before calcineurin inhibitors; brain MRI/MRA if never imaged (vascular events = 34% of deaths, Kamath 2004).

### 04-03 · Alagille syndrome III: the liver (synced 2026-11-09)
- **ALGS transplant burden (GALA, Vandriel 2023):** 27% by 5 y, 38% by 10 y, 50% by 18 y; bilirubin median at 6–12 mo ≥ 10 mg/dL → HR 15.6 for transplant (vs < 5). Middle band (5 to < 10) → transplant-centre review before decompensation (lesson synthesis; no society threshold).

### 04-04 · Alagille syndrome IV: the heart (synced 2026-11-10)
- **Pre-LT cardiac evaluation in ALGS (slides 7, 12, 16 — a working sequence from small single-centre series; no society has graded it):** echo with Doppler in all candidates (Squires 2014, 2-B — superseded by Martinez 2026, TODO(verify) 2026 wording); CT angiography of chest and abdomen when transplant is raised (Ruth 2019; lesson sequence); catheter if RV pressure > ½ systemic on echo, a significant gradient or complex anatomy (Tretter 2018 trigger, expert opinion); dobutamine stress 10 → 20 µg/kg/min, cardiac output rise > 40% read as adequate (Razavi 2001; Squires text, ungraded; never validated — Bordin 2018: 18 children, 3 non-responders all with normal resting output). **A rise < 40% is a reason for joint review, not an automatic exclusion** — the two children excluded on it died of their liver (Bordin 2018). **Resting RV pressure is a trigger for a catheter, not a veto:** it did not predict the operating-room course (Png 1999), the stress response (Razavi 2001) or cardiac death (Ovaert 2001: mean RVSP 55 mmHg in 10 catheterised, ~70% 1-y survival, no cardiac deaths); small selected series.
- **Fix before listing:** balloon/stent or surgical PA reconstruction before transplant when gradients are significant; catheter restenosis up to 60%; surgical reconstruction RV:systemic 0.88 → 0.40, durable at 4 y (Monge 2013); Stanford 51 ALGS PA reconstructions — 80% complete repair, 9 later transplanted (Luong 2021). TOF with pulmonary atresia + MAPCAs = highest-risk combination; cardiac plan years ahead of the liver plan (Mainwaring 2012).
- **Outcomes (SPLIT, Kamath 2012):** 1-y patient survival 87% ALGS (n=91) vs 96% BA; 5 y 86% vs 94.4%; most ALGS deaths within 30 days; no pre-LT factor predicted death; post-LT GFR < 90 in 22% vs 8%; early vascular complications 21%. Excess risk is peri-operative: caval clamping −15 mmHg systolic, reperfusion another −16 (Png 1999, 21 LTs) → joint cardiology–anesthesia plan (venovenous bypass or caval sparing, fluids, inotropes, ICU).
- **Living related donor:** mother with murmur and "slightly high" GGT → targeted test for the family variant before any donor work-up; first-degree relative donor: bile duct hypoplasia must be excluded, and a shared allele makes living donation generally inadvisable (Squires 2014, 2-B — TODO(verify) Martinez 2026). Reinforces the 04-01/04-02 donor rule.

### 04-05 · Alagille syndrome V: the vessels (synced 2026-11-11)
- **Pre-LT vascular work-up in ALGS — expert practice, no graded guideline (slide 17: DIFFERS; Squires 2014 does not specify imaging) (Ayoub 2023, review of published series):** brain MRA + triphasic abdominal CT angiography (coeliac, hepatic, SMA, renal), one sedation in a small child; aortic conduit for hepatic arterial inflow in 64% of 55 children; HAT 35% → 6% after a policy of planned aortic conduit; vascular thrombosis 25% children vs 8% adults with ALGS. Tell the surgeon before the operation. TODO(verify) all numbers in Ayoub 2023.
- **After LT:** hypertension in ALGS → image the renal arteries before blaming tacrolimus (RAS 6/12 on CT after LT, only 1 hypertensive — Pediatr Nephrol 2025 review, TODO(verify) citation). Measure BP in both arms (subclavian stenosis).
- **Post-LT neuro red flag (ALGS):** headache, seizure, focal deficit → emergency neuroimaging; aneurysm/moyamoya risk persists after the new liver. Transition: MRA on record and a named owner of the result; adult ruptures median 25 y, mostly vertebrobasilar, 40% fatal (5 reported adults, Ayoub 2023). Pregnancy: know vascular status first; with an aneurysm — assisted vaginal or cesarean delivery (expert reviews, slide 16). Antiplatelets for moyamoya only by joint decision (varices, coagulopathy).

### 04-06 · Alagille syndrome VI: kidney, skeleton, eyes and face (synced 2026-11-12)
- **Kidney before the liver in ALGS (three-organ assessment: heart, vessels, kidney):** creatinine overestimates GFR in cholestatic, sarcopenic children (low muscle; bilirubin interferes negatively with the Jaffe assay) → cystatin C or a measured GFR (iohexol) before listing; renal imaging including the renal arteries; nephrology review. Only Martinez 2026 / Squires 2014 ask for renal function before LT, nothing ALGS-specific (TODO(verify) 2026 wording).
- **Outcomes:** SPLIT (Kamath 2012, Liver Transpl): post-LT GFR < 90 in 22% of 91 ALGS vs 8% of BA (already in 04-04 entry). Brussels (Demaret 2017, **abstract only** — do not publish numbers without a full paper): mean eGFR ~94 after LT vs ~143 other indications; non-transplanted ALGS ~88, so LT itself did not lower it; abnormal renal US → eGFR ~57 vs ~98. → The ALGS kidney enters transplant handicapped; the post-LT gap = native disease + calcineurin inhibitors.
- **Renal-sparing plan made before LT if GFR already reduced:** lower calcineurin-inhibitor exposure — mycophenolate early; an mTOR inhibitor (everolimus) **only ≥ 30 days after LT** (US boxed warning: hepatic artery thrombosis; sirolimus not recommended de novo — ALGS already carries a high HAT risk). Lesson says "from the start"; expert practice — TODO(verify) source and labels before publishing. Combined liver–kidney transplant uncommon (Philadelphia: 1 kidney transplant for ESRD, 2 after LT).
- **Parent donor extrahepatic screen:** slit lamp, spine film, echo and renal ultrasound + targeted test for the family variant (a butterfly vertebra on the mother's old chest film may be the first sign she carries the disease; ≥ 1 eye sign in 36% of parents — Hingorani 1999). Reinforces the 04-01/04-02/04-04 donor rule.
- **After LT:** normal-gap (hyperchloremic) acidosis or falling bicarbonate in ALGS → urine pH/anion gap before blaming diarrhea (negative urine anion gap does not exclude proximal RTA); HCO3 and urinalysis every visit; yearly cystatin C eGFR and urine protein (expert schedule).

### 04-07 · Alagille syndrome VII: the pruritus ladder (synced 2026-11-13)
- Rifampin (used for cholestatic itch before LT) is a strong CYP3A4 inducer → tacrolimus levels fall sharply; induction wears off over ~1–2 weeks after stopping, so levels rise later — plan levels (lesson slide 11; timing = general pharmacology). Naltrexone blocks opioid analgesia → stop before planned surgery and tell the anesthesiologist; a deceased-donor call-in is unplanned → flag naltrexone on the listing summary.
- Transplant cures ALGS itch, but it is "a transplant for a symptom": persistent ItchRO ≥ 3 with sleep loss despite a scored ladder (incl. IBAT inhibitor) is the lesson's trigger for the transplant meeting — expert opinion.

### 04-08 · Alagille syndrome VIII: IBAT inhibitors (synced 2026-11-14)
- **Who reaches transplant:** GALA (2023) native liver survival 54% at 10 y and 40% at 18 y; 69% of ALGS transplants were for intractable itch. Sokol 2023: on maralixibat, none of the three responses at wk 48 — itch fall > 1 point, bilirubin < 6.5 mg/dL, bile acids < 200 µmol/L — → transplant assessment (6-y event-free survival of non-responders by each criterion 43–57%). Decompensation (variceal bleed, ascites, encephalopathy) ends IBAT therapy (label contraindication) and moves to the transplant decision.
- **Listing summary:** record IBAT-inhibitor response history (diary, bile acids) — a non-responder's switch between agents should not delay the assessment (lesson, expert opinion).

### 04-09 · Alagille syndrome IX: surgical biliary diversion (synced 2026-11-15)
- A child with prior PEBD, internal diversion or ileal exclusion arrives at transplant with altered anatomy: gallbladder used for a conduit or removed, jejunal/colonic conduit, stoma → tell the transplant surgeon; it changes later surgical planning (lesson slide 19).
- GALA (Vandriel 2023, abstract): diverted ALGS children had HR 2.5 for transplant or death — likely confounded by indication and severity (diverted for severe itch, median bilirubin 8.8; abstract, no adjustment published); diversion should never postpone a transplant assessment that heart, growth or bilirubin already demand.
- Transplant in progressive ALGS — standard of care, refer early (EASL 2024); cardiac and vascular work-up before listing (AASLD/AST/NASPGHAN 2014).

### 04-10 · Alagille syndrome X: natural history (synced 2026-11-16)
- GALA (Vandriel 2023): transplant by 18 y in 50.4%, median age 2.8 y, 72% before age 5. Indications (328 with data): cholestasis complications 72%, PHT 30% (later, median 4.2 y vs 2.5 y), > 1 indication 53%. Post-LT patient survival 92% at 5 y and 88% at 20 y; graft 88% and 83% (TODO(verify) vs paper).
- Median total bilirubin 6–12 mo ≥ 10 mg/dL → transplant HR 15.6; lesson synthesis: ≥ 10 → transplant-centre referral; 5–10 → closer review including an early transplant-centre introduction, as in the 04-03 entry and the lesson's own scenario and case (expert opinion, no guideline threshold). Early referral ≠ early transplant: time for cardiac/vascular work-up.
- Deaths: cardiac (median 1.1 y) and non-cardiac vascular (2.2 y) act before liver deaths (2.8 y) → heart and vessels decide candidacy as much as the liver.

### 04-12 · Alagille syndrome XII: other causes of ductopenia (synced 2026-11-18)
- **Chronic rejection — Banff 2016 (Demetris, Am J Transplant 2016;16:2816–35; slide 14):** Lesson: "at least two findings needed for either stage" — reviewer recalls Banff minimal criteria as any one of: senescent changes in most ducts, foam-cell arteriopathy, duct loss in > 50% of tracts → do not publish "≥ 2" until the full text is checked. Late: duct loss in ≥ 50% of portal tracts, arteriole loss > 25%, focal venular obliteration, bridging fibrosis, foam-cell arteriopathy. Banff counts *tracts without a duct*, not a ratio. Consider DSA and C4d. TODO(verify) against the Banff full text (open access?).
- **Pediatric chronic rejection (Tannuri 2016, Clinics 2016;71:216–20, São Paulo, 537 primary LTs):** 29 (5.4%); remission 10, re-LT 7, death 12; adverse outcome with ductopenia 10/11 vs 7/16 without — the only significant factor (RR 2.08). ### 05-01 · PFIC I: physiology of bile secretion (synced 2026-11-21)
- **BSEP deficiency after LT:** anti-BSEP antibodies can cause recurrent low-GGT cholestasis in the graft that mimics the disease (lesson slides 6, 17; TODO(verify) primary source — block 5 day 10 lesson).
- **FIC1 deficiency after LT:** gut, pancreas and ear are still FIC1-deficient → diarrhea often worsens and the graft becomes steatotic (expected, not surprising). Lesson synthesis: bile acid binder, consider biliary diversion of the graft (expert opinion; TODO(verify) — block 5 day 10).
- **MYO5B:** liver transplant can worsen microvillus inclusion disease diarrhea; cholestasis may appear after intestinal transplant (Gonzales 2017).

### 05-02 · PFIC II: FIC1 deficiency (ATP8B1) (synced 2026-11-22)
- **FIC1 after LT — VERIFIED headline (Bull 2018, Hepatol Commun 2:515; abstract, Europe PMC 29761168; 42 FIC1 + 60 BSEP, 57 transplants; published in ref_genetics 2026-11-22):** transplant resolved cholestasis in all groups; FIC1 recipients commonly developed hepatic steatosis, diarrhea and/or pancreatic disease and often continued poor growth; growth generally improved after LT in BSEP. Lesson numbers (from full text, TODO(verify) — not in abstract): diarrhea 81% vs 7%, graft steatosis 90% vs 6%, pancreatic disease 40% vs 0%, height > 3rd centile 35% vs 88%, deaths 3/24 vs 0/33.
- **Pittsburgh (Henkel 2021, Pediatr Transplant 25:e14108; abstract verified, Europe PMC 34339082):** 7 PFIC1, mean follow-up 10.9 y; patient and graft survival 100%; diarrhea in all 7 (3 persisted, 4 new); height z −2.98, weight z −2.63; steatosis common, no steatohepatitis or significant fibrosis. Contrast: Kyoto LDLT series (Miyagawa-Hayashino 2009) — steatohepatitis progressing to fibrosis and graft loss; India (Vasudevan 2023) — steatohepatitis 23%, resolved after total internal biliary diversion (TODO(verify) both). → Why/guideline_compare: progression is variable; protocol biopsy or elastography with steatosis quantification reasonable (expert).
- **Mechanism (slide 12, partly hypothesis):** normal graft delivers a full bile acid load into an FIC1-deficient gut → bile acid/secretory diarrhea → gut-derived signals return as graft steatosis; interrupting the enterohepatic circulation improves both. → Pitfall: reading graft steatosis in FIC1 as rejection, NASH or donor fat.
- **Management ladder (slide 15; case series + expert opinion):** quantify stools, fecal elastase, steatosis, growth at 3–6 mo → bile acid binder (cholestyramine/colesevelam; separate from tacrolimus — binding; watch vitamins) → off-label IBAT inhibitor (odevixibat 30–120 µg/kg/day in 9 boys after LT, steatosis better in 3 of 5 — Vogel 2025 JPGN 81:1410, TODO(verify) abstract; do not publish dose before verification) → surgical diversion for failures or steatohepatitis; some centers divert at the time of LT. Clonidine palliation; enzymes only for documented exocrine insufficiency. EASL 2024: medical or surgical EHC interruption "could be used" (lesson: LoE 5, weak — TODO(verify)). Case report 2026 (JPGN Rep, DOI 10.1002/jpr3.70236): IBAT inhibitor allowed reversal of post-LT diversion without recurrence of enteropathy (n = 1).
- **Pre-LT checklist for FIC1 (slide 16):** baseline audiogram, lipase, fecal elastase, stool output, growth, vitamins, bone age; discuss diversion at LT if diarrhea already severe. **Counseling line:** the new liver stops itch and jaundice; it does not fix gut, pancreas, ears or short stature, and diarrhea may get worse.
- **Traps:** post-LT abdominal pain attributed to the anastomosis → missed pancreatitis (check lipase); cholestyramine given with tacrolimus → low levels.

### 05-03 · PFIC III: BSEP deficiency (ABCB11) (synced 2026-11-23)
- **Antibody-induced BSEP deficiency (AIBD) — VERIFIED core (Jara 2009, NEJM 361:1359, abstract; EASL 2024 full text; published in ref_genetics 2026-11-23):** recipients with no native BSEP never became tolerant → anti-BSEP IgG against the donor pump → low-GGT cholestasis, itch, high bile acids that mimic the original disease, without rejection or biliary complication. Jara: 3 children, all remitted with intensified immunosuppression; high-titer antibodies inhibited transport in vitro. EASL 2024 (LoE 3, strong): diagnose by anti-BSEP antibodies in plasma or anti-canalicular IgG on immunofluorescence; adapt immunosuppression, rituximab + plasmapheresis when needed; retransplant if it fails (LoE 3, weak) — EASL: "not always with long-term success"; recurrence in the next graft per lesson — TODO(verify).
- **Numbers (lesson, TODO(verify) full texts):** prevalence ≈ 8% after LT for PFIC2 (up to 33% in screening series); pooled 13 cases — 5 retransplanted, 4 died, onset 9 mo–17 y (Quintero 2020, PGHN 23:174); Düsseldorf 7 cases — onset 3–108 mo, preceded by reduced immunosuppression in 4, viral infection in 2; antibodies bind extracellular loop 1 in 6 of 7 (Stindt 2016, Hepatology 63:524; Kubitz 2015). Masahata 2016 (Transplant Proc 48:3156, abstract verified): one LDLT case, no native BSEP. Rituximab 375 mg/m² + 10 apheresis sessions; Pneumocystis after rituximab → prophylaxis.
- **Prevention (slide 17; extrapolated, expert):** flag "BSEP3 / no native protein — AIBD risk" on the problem list; no immunosuppression withdrawal or minimization trials; itch + normal GGT after LT = AIBD until proven otherwise → anti-BSEP serology; recheck tests after EBV or other significant viral infection; adherence lapses in teens = an unplanned minimization trial. Pitfall: the patients the team most wants to wean are the ones with a "perfect" graft.
- **Explant:** sample carefully — incidental HCC in BSEP explants changes post-LT imaging and AFP follow-up (Knisely 2006, verified: 11 pediatric HCCs at 13–52 mo, 9 + 1 sibling with BSEP deficiency).
- **Card 34 → workup row / pathway out:** low-GGT cholestasis after LT for PFIC2 → anti-BSEP antibodies + biopsy (bland canalicular cholestasis, giant cells possible, no rejection). guideline_compare row: anti-BSEP after LT — MATERIAL (only EASL).

### 05-04 · PFIC IV: MDR3 deficiency (ABCB4) (synced 2026-11-24)
- **PFIC3 is the most transplant-curable PFIC (slide 10):** MDR3 is a liver protein; no recurrence mechanism like anti-BSEP, no intestinal disease like FIC1.
- **Heterozygous parental graft:** the living donor parent carries one ABCB4 variant → check donor liver tests and imaging; EASL 2024 — long-term UDCA for recipients of a heterozygous parental graft (LoE 4, strong; TODO(verify) wording).
- **Presentation:** splenomegaly is more common than jaundice at presentation (96% vs 62%; portal hypertension 69%) — Schatz 2018, abstract-verified. The lesson's "portal hypertension before jaundice" is not supported (its own medians: jaundice 2.9 y, PH ~6.5 y).
