### 04-12 · Alagille syndrome XII: other causes of ductopenia (synced 2026-11-18)
- **Ducts vanish after pediatric HSCT, probably from drugs (Maximova 2018, Biol Blood Marrow Transplant 2018;24:2250–8 — verified against the abstract 2026-11-18):** prospective, 131 biopsies, 50 HSCTs, 47 children (mean 9.7 y); ductopenia (ratio ≤ 0.5) in 113 of 131 biopsies (93%); ratio 0.63 → 0.16 while cholestasis scores improved; severe ductopenia (≤ 0.2) linked to prior chemotherapy (P = 0.04), notably thiotepa, not to GVHD. Long-term liver risk unknown.
- **Cholestasis after allogeneic HSCT — what to exclude before "GVHD" (slide 13, by timing):** SOS/VOD (usually first 3 weeks, but late onset occurs — EBMT 2018 pediatric criteria have no time limit, TODO(verify); weight gain, tender liver, ascites, refractory platelets); acute liver GVHD (after engraftment, with skin or gut GVHD); chronic liver GVHD (months; NIH 2014 consensus — Jagasia, BBMT 2015;21:389–401: abnormal liver tests are not distinctive; lesson wording "needs a diagnostic sign elsewhere or biopsy proof" — TODO(verify) exact wording in the open full text PMC4329079); drug/conditioning injury (azoles, PN, thiotepa and other alkylators); infection (adenovirus, HHV-6, HBV PCR) and iron overload (ferritin). 
- → Card: "Rising GGT months after HSCT, no skin or gut GVHD → Doppler ultrasound first, then biopsy (transjugular if platelets are low); review conditioning and azoles." Pitfall: calling every post-HSCT cholestasis GVHD.
