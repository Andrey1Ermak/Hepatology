### 03-03 · Biliary atresia: diagnosis (synced 2026-10-25)
- **Infant cholestasis US:** fasting 3–4 h (else every gallbladder looks small); a feed afterwards tests contraction; high-frequency probe, operator who looks for atresia on purpose. Look for gallbladder (absent/small/irregular, "ghost" triad — Tan Kendrick 2003), triangular cord, hepatic subcapsular flow and enlarged hepatic artery on color Doppler, porta cyst (intrahepatic ducts dilated or not), splenic malformation (polysplenia, situs, preduodenal portal vein, absent IVC). Pooled accuracy: cord 74%/97%, gallbladder 85%/92% (Zhou 2016); subcapsular flow 95%/92% (9-study meta-analysis, Dis Markers 2020:5262565 — TODO(verify) first author). A normal scan does not exclude atresia (Fawaz 2017, 1A); repeating it a week later rarely helps.
- **HIDA:** non-drainage sens 98.7%, spec 70.4% (Kianifar 2013, via Fawaz 2017) — drainage argues against atresia; non-drainage still needs a definitive test; **no phenobarbital priming** (5 days of delay, no proven gain — Fawaz 2017).
- **MRCP in infants:** 99%/36% — small ducts not seen; limited role for atresia (useful for choledochal cyst).
- **ERCP** 86–100%/87–94% — expert centers, general anesthesia; **percutaneous cholecystocholangiography** 100%/87% where expertise exists (Ge 2025).
- **Shear wave elastography** in infants: adjunct for atresia (Wagner 2022 meta-analysis, 11 studies, 1,307 infants: 83%/79%, AUC 0.91); also stages fibrosis before the Kasai (→ ref_elasto).
- Rule for the card: a test with high sensitivity and low specificity is useful only when negative.
