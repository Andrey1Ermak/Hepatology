### 03-09 · Biliary atresia: portal hypertension after the Kasai (synced 2026-10-31)
Biliary atresia is the model here; generalise to other cholestatic cirrhosis only where the source does. Every number — TODO(verify) against the cited paper at build.
- **Definitions (slide 4):** definite PHT = past PHT complication, OR spleen > 2 cm below the costal margin + platelets < 150 ×10⁹/L; possible PHT = only one of the two (ChiLDReN, Shneider 2012 — research definition, insensitive in infants). High-risk varices (HRV) = grade 3 esophageal, or grade 2 with red signs, and/or gastric varices along the cardia (Duché M et al. J Hepatol 2017;66:320–7) — present in 96% of children who bled vs 11% of never-bleeders. HRV score 0–5, high-risk 3–5 (Ackermann 2026). Adult CSPH (HVPG ≥ 10 mmHg or LSM ≥ 25 kPa, Baveno VII/AASLD 2024) — not validated in children. Read a paper's "PHT" as sign vs endoscopic finding vs event.
- **Mechanism (slide 3, for Why):** sinusoidal + presinusoidal resistance from ongoing biliary fibrosis, even in anicteric children → congestive splenomegaly/hypersplenism → collaterals (esophageal, gastric, Roux-loop) → events (bleed, ascites, HPS/POPH). No routine paediatric HVPG.
- **Infant problem (slides 7–8):** Paris, 139 children after Kasai (Duché 2010, Gastroenterology 2010;139:1952–60): varices in 70% (88 of 125 scoped because of PHT signs; median 13 mo); 5-y bleeding 59% with grade 3 varices, 17% grade 2, 12% none; 20% bled, median age 17 mo; 75% of bleeds before 2.5 y. Ackermann 2026 (Gastroenterology 2026;170:188–98) — **mixed-cause cohort**: 1,586 children with portal hypertension (403 with BA; 590 with serial endoscopy): HRV emerging by 5 y 36%, by 10 y 54% (serial-endoscopy subgroup); bleeding 75% with untreated HRV vs 3.8% without HRV; **platelets ≥ 150 with HRV in 62% of infants < 12 mo vs 2% > 10 y**; 32% of all 629 children with HRV had platelets ≥ 150. Authors' proposal (not a guideline): in BA < 12 months, treat grade 2 varices or GOV1 prophylactically. → Pitfall: "Normal platelets do not exclude high-risk varices in infants."
- **Natural history (slide 6; Bass 2022, ChiLDReN n = 869):** 5-y first-bleed incidence 9.4% from Kasai (PROBE), 8.0% if enrolled > 3 y (BASIC); bleeds spread across childhood (slide 9). Surveillance is lifelong: varices by 16 y → adult transplant HR 8.6 (King's, Jain V et al. J Hepatol 2019;71:71–7).
- **Non-invasive tests (slide 10):** good at "probably has varices", poor at excluding HRV. Platelets sens 0.71 / spec 0.83 (Cochrane, Colli 2017, CD008759); prediction rule (platelets, spleen z, albumin) derivation AUC 0.93 (Gana JC et al. JPGN 2010;50:188–93), validation AUC 0.80; platelet/spleen z-score ratio AUC 0.84 (Gana 2011, Gastroenterology, multicentre, n = 108); liver stiffness in BA 12.7 kPa, AUC 0.89; APRI 1.92, AUC 0.87 (Chongsrisawat 2011, BMC Gastroenterol 2011;11:41). Adult Baveno rule (LSM < 20 kPa + platelets > 150 → skip endoscopy) not validated in infants → guideline_compare MATERIAL. Use the tests to decide who is scoped sooner, not who is left alone. (→ ref_elasto, c_apri_gpr.)
- **Primary prophylaxis debate (slides 12–13):** no paediatric RCT of beta-blockers; one feasibility RCT of banding vs sham, n = 22, RR 0.28 (0.03–2.27), very low certainty (Cifuentes LI et al. Cochrane 2021;CD011561); Baveno VI paediatric: no consensus (Shneider BL et al. Hepatology 2016;63:1368–80). Nearly nobody bands small varices (exception under discussion: Ackermann's proposal for grade 2/GOV1 in BA infants < 12 mo, above); nearly everybody treats HRV when transplant is far away. → Write as "Expert practice; limited paediatric data". Card wording: "HRV and transplant not imminent → endoscopic prophylaxis in an experienced unit."
- **Tools by size (slide 14, APSPGHAN 2026 — TODO(verify)):** banding preferred from about 10 kg; < 10 kg: sclerotherapy or a non-selective beta-blocker case by case (Italian survey of 16 centres, 2025: 63% used sclerotherapy < 10 kg; all used propranolol as the beta-blocker of choice, mostly titrated to heart rate — Sansotta N et al. Children 2025;12:940). **Beta-blocker pearl (slide 14):** in a toddler it blunts the tachycardia that would signal a bleed and can mask hypoglycaemia — give families a written action plan. Cyanoacrylate for GOV2/isolated gastric varices, expert hands. No propranolol dose in the lesson — do not publish one without a source. Duché 2013 (Gastroenterology 2013;145:801–7; n = 36 primary prophylaxis): no breakthrough bleed, 3-y survival 97%; eradication took a mean 4.2 sessions, recurrence 37%. Duché 2017: 110/120 HRV treated endoscopically; eradicated 76 (relapse 20); 29 of 34 not eradicated were transplanted — prophylaxis is a bridge.
- **Hypersplenism (slide 16):** a marker, rarely a target; treat for bleeding or sport risk, not a number. Partial splenic embolisation (n = 12, platelets +235; pain 33%, fever 17%) or partial splenectomy (BA n = 15; 27% later LDLT). **Avoid total splenectomy** (infection, loss of a natural shunt, complicates transplant). Teen with platelets 60, no varices, sport → spleen guard, no splenectomy. guideline_compare: hypersplenism — GAP.
- **Prognosis (slide 11):** treated PHT marks a failing liver (Okubo 2025: 30-y native-liver survival 45.9% vs 88.4% without PHT). Plan transplant assessment alongside endoscopic therapy.
- **Surveillance frame (slide 18, "Expert framework"):** every visit spleen/platelets/albumin/bilirubin; first year endoscopy if signs of PHT or poor drainage, consider at 9–12 mo even with normal platelets; yearly elastography ± spleen stiffness where available, repeat endoscopy if varices or rising stiffness; after any bleed — banding, transplant assessment, HPS/POPH screen; adolescence — continue, plan transition.
- **Guideline compare (slide 17):** screening endoscopy — MATERIAL (adult: skip if LSM/platelets favourable; paediatric: NITs cannot replace endoscopy); primary prophylaxis — MATERIAL (adult NSBB/carvedilol or banding; paediatric: no consensus, banding ≥ 10 kg); secondary prophylaxis — DIFFERS; hypersplenism — GAP. AASLD 2024 states adults only.
- **Cases (slide 21):** 11 mo, cleared, spleen 3 cm, platelets 168 → endoscopy at 9–12 mo · 8 kg infant, grade 3 + red signs, TB 1 → sclerotherapy or beta-blocker, band at ≥ 10 kg · toddler, small grade 1, good synthesis → no prophylaxis, repeat endoscopy in 1–2 y · teen, platelets 60, no varices, sport → spleen guard.
