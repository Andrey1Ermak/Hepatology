### 03-05 · Biliary atresia: the Kasai operation (synced 2026-10-27)
- **How common (slides 2, 7, 20):** cholangitis after the Kasai in 30–60%; ~57% of 223 US infants had ≥ 1 episode in the first year (Kelley-Quon 2022, Western Pediatric Surgery Research Consortium, Pediatr Surg Int 2022;38:193–9, online 2021). Cholangitis is the most common complication.
- **Why (slide 7, for Why):** bacterial access to a poorly draining, fibrotic biliary tree — not simple reflux of bowel content; antireflux valves, stomas and long conduits did not reduce cholangitis. Gallbladder Kasai keeps the sphincter of Oddi (rationale, not proven benefit).
- **Fever + new jaundice after clearance (slides 19, 21):** cholangitis is the first diagnosis and is treated first — most recurrences are infection, not anastomotic; a redo Kasai is rarely the answer (only after good initial flow that stopped abruptly). → First contact: blood cultures + IV antibiotics; low threshold (high-volume pathway, slide 15). Antibiotic choice/duration and prophylaxis — not in this lesson (TODO(verify) source at build; lesson says only "prophylaxis" as part of the center protocol).
- **Context for the card:** bilirubin < 2 mg/dL by 3 months is the main prognostic readout (HR 0.08, Superina 2011); not cleared → transplant assessment (→ chol_0_3m).
- **Build note (clinical review 2026-10-27):** red flags must not require jaundice — fever alone after a Kasai, paler stools or a rising bilirubin = suspected cholangitis until proven otherwise. ### 03-06 · Biliary atresia: adjuvant therapy after the Kasai (synced 2026-10-28)
- **Prophylaxis does not exclude cholangitis (slides 19, 21):** fever after a Kasai = cholangitis until proven otherwise, even on oral prophylaxis → blood cultures, IV antibiotics. Case: fever at 6 weeks on TMP-SMX → cultures + IV antibiotics 10–14 days (lesson does not say whether 6 weeks is age or time after the Kasai — word the case as "6 weeks after the Kasai" at build, consistent with the TMP-SMX age limit). Red flag line for the card.
- **Duration (slide 14; Calinescu-Tuleasca 2022, J Clin Med 2022;11:494 — international Delphi, BARD network / ERN Rare-Liver; expert consensus):** suspected cholangitis — treat 10–14 days; confirmed (culture or imaging) — 14–21 days. Label as "Expert consensus (Delphi)". Empiric IV regimen and diagnostic criteria — still not in the lessons (TODO(verify) from the Delphi full text at build; TRACK-BA, prospective since 2025, is validating definitions).
- **Prophylaxis (slides 13–14):** Delphi supports oral prophylaxis for 6–12 months after the Kasai; no consensus on the agent — TMP-SMX most used (54% of respondents). Direct evidence weak: no placebo-controlled RCT of oral prophylaxis; Bu 2003 (J Pediatr Surg 2003;38:590–3; 19 infants after a first cholangitis, TMP-SMX or neomycin vs 18 historical controls — fewer recurrences, P = .042 and .011; secondary prevention); Brody 2025 (Acta Paediatr 2025;114:654–9; 72 infants, primary prophylaxis in 49%, infection rate not lower). Indian Delphi split 6–12 weeks vs > 12 weeks (Vutukuru 2024, J Indian Assoc Pediatr Surg 2024;29:271–6). Counterweights: resistance, C. difficile. Swiss national protocol 2015: fewer recurrences and transplants afterwards (Calinescu-Tuleasca & Wildhaber, Semin Pediatr Surg 2024;33:151471) — before/after, not randomized. Card: prophylaxis dose and minimum age — TODO(verify) source at build (TMP-SMX: US labeling contraindicates use < 2 months; sulfonamides displace bilirubin — relevant in young jaundiced infants); do not publish a TMP-SMX dose from memory.
- **Post-operative IV course (slide 13):** Chen 2021 (Pediatr Surg Int 2021;37:1089–97; RCT, 180 infants, 7 vs 14 days of IV antibiotics after the Kasai): 6-month cholangitis similar (P = .27; published abstract: 62% vs 70%, open-label, cefoperazone/sulbactam + ornidazole); authors: a 7-day course is sufficient. Early cholangitis 61% (7 days) vs 38% (14 days) per the lesson chapter — TODO(verify) against the full text before publishing.
- **Context:** cholangitis in 30–60% (slide 2; consistent with 03-05), roughly half in the first two years; each episode can end bile drainage. Steroids did not clearly reduce cholangitis (Yang 2022 RR 0.78, 0.60–1.01).
