### 03-05 · Biliary atresia: the Kasai operation (synced 2026-10-27)
- **How common (slides 2, 7, 20):** cholangitis after the Kasai in 30–60%; ~57% of 223 US infants had ≥ 1 episode in the first year (Kelley-Quon 2022, Western Pediatric Surgery Research Consortium, Pediatr Surg Int 2022;38:193–9, online 2021). Cholangitis is the most common complication.
- **Why (slide 7, for Why):** bacterial access to a poorly draining, fibrotic biliary tree — not simple reflux of bowel content; antireflux valves, stomas and long conduits did not reduce cholangitis. Gallbladder Kasai keeps the sphincter of Oddi (rationale, not proven benefit).
- **Fever + new jaundice after clearance (slides 19, 21):** cholangitis is the first diagnosis and is treated first — most recurrences are infection, not anastomotic; a redo Kasai is rarely the answer (only after good initial flow that stopped abruptly). → First contact: blood cultures + IV antibiotics; low threshold (high-volume pathway, slide 15). Antibiotic choice/duration and prophylaxis — not in this lesson (TODO(verify) source at build; lesson says only "prophylaxis" as part of the center protocol).
- **Context for the card:** bilirubin < 2 mg/dL by 3 months is the main prognostic readout (HR 0.08, Superina 2011); not cleared → transplant assessment (→ chol_0_3m).
- **Build note (clinical review 2026-10-27):** red flags must not require jaundice — fever alone after a Kasai, paler stools or a rising bilirubin = suspected cholangitis until proven otherwise. - **Duration (slide 14; Calinescu-Tuleasca 2022, J Clin Med 2022;11:494 — international Delphi, BARD network / ERN Rare-Liver; expert consensus):** suspected cholangitis — treat 10–14 days; confirmed (culture or imaging) — 14–21 days. Label as "Expert consensus (Delphi)". Empiric IV regimen and diagnostic criteria — still not in the lessons (TODO(verify) from the Delphi full text at build; TRACK-BA, prospective since 2025, is validating definitions).
- **Prophylaxis (slides 13–14):** Delphi supports oral prophylaxis for 6–12 months after the Kasai; no consensus on the agent — TMP-SMX most used (54% of respondents). Direct evidence weak: no placebo-controlled RCT of oral prophylaxis; Bu 2003 (J Pediatr Surg 2003;38:590–3; 19 infants after a first cholangitis, TMP-SMX or neomycin vs 18 historical controls — fewer recurrences, P = .042 and .011; secondary prevention); Brody 2025 (Acta Paediatr 2025;114:654–9; 72 infants, primary prophylaxis in 49%, infection rate not lower). Indian Delphi split 6–12 weeks vs > 12 weeks (Vutukuru 2024, J Indian Assoc Pediatr Surg 2024;29:271–6). Counterweights: resistance, C. difficile. ### 03-07 · Biliary atresia: cholangitis after the Kasai (synced 2026-10-29)
Core lesson for this card (block 3 plan link: day 7).
- **Definition — BARD Delphi 2022 (slide 6; Calinescu AM et al. J Clin Med 2022;11:494 — expert consensus):** list A (clinical): fever without another source and/or shivering · paler stools · new or increasing jaundice · abdominal discomfort (vomiting, poor feeding, irritability). List B (lab/imaging): raised WBC, CRP and/or procalcitonin · raised transaminases · raised GGT and/or bilirubin · bile lakes on imaging. **Suspected** = 1 A + 1 B; **confirmed** = 2 A + 2 B, or suspected + positive blood culture. Patterns: early (first month after the Kasai), relapsing (> 3 episodes in 6 months), intractable (antibiotics > 4 weeks, or ≥ 3 episodes < 1 month apart). Adult Tokyo criteria do not fit infants. → Red flags + algorithm entry; candidate chip_screen at build (suspected/confirmed), sourced to the Delphi only.
- **Fever is not required (slide 7; Madadi-Sanjani O et al. JPGN Rep 2024, doi:10.1002/jpr3.12071 — 59 cholangitis cases among 185 infants (32%), Hannover/Geneva, first year; confirm in the full text whether 59 is episodes or infants):** fever in 41%, raised bilirubin or GGT in 90%, inflammatory markers 70%, transaminases 68%, ≥ 1 B criterion 94.9%; positive blood culture 1/59, bile lakes 1/59. → Red flag wording: "After a Kasai: fever, paler stools, new or deeper jaundice, or rising bilirubin/GGT = cholangitis until proven otherwise. Fever is not required. Cultures + IV antibiotics now." GGT often rises before fever.
- **Epidemiology (slides 2, 4):** 22–93% across series (definition explains most of the spread); ≥ 50% expected, most in the first year, many by 2 months; recurrence common (76% of affected Korean infants, Baek 2020 JPGN 2020;70:171–7). Wider than 03-05/03-06 (30–60%); the spread is definition-driven — at build use "about half or more" rather than a single number.
- **Prognosis (slides 3, 5; for Why):** cholangitis is both marker and cause; partial drainage has the highest risk (Hong Kong, van Heurn 2003, J Pediatr 2003;142:566–71, OR 5.72) but well-draining infants are diagnosed more often (open conduit). One episode alone did not predict liver loss; recurrent (> 3 in 6 months, Liu F 2023) or ≥ 3 episodes (Harumatsu 2024, AUC 0.695) did; in infants with cystic lesions, cholangitis starting before 3 months of age vs after 6 months: 2-yr native-liver survival 43% vs 80%. Swiss protocol 2015: transplant rate 89% → 55% (before/after only).
- **First hour (slide 8) → workup:** blood culture before the first dose (ideally 2 sets) · CBC, CRP, procalcitonin · bilirubin (total + conjugated), GGT, ALT/AST · abdominal ultrasound with Doppler (bile lakes, ascites, portal flow, other source) · urinalysis/urine culture and line culture if a central line (alternatives: UTI, line sepsis, viral illness with transient cholestasis, Kasai failure). Liver tissue culture/PCR — selected cases only (Aziz 2025, yield ~53%). Neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio — prognostic hint only, do not publish as a decision tool.
- **Pitfall (slides 8, 19):** "The Kasai has failed" — a rising bilirubin after fever is cholangitis until proven otherwise; finish treating it before labelling the operation as failed (consistent with 03-05).
- **Microbiology (slide 9; Baek 2020):** blood-culture yield 9% (Korea), 15% overall / 26% severe (Wang 2023), 10–44% across series. Isolates: Enterococcus faecium 28%, E. coli 15%, Enterobacter cloacae 11%, Klebsiella pneumoniae 9%. Gram-negatives cefotaxime-susceptible in only 38%, nearly all meropenem-susceptible; gram-positives ampicillin 42%, vancomycin 100% (single-centre Korean data — label as such).
- **Empiric therapy (slides 10–11) — lesson calls it "a decision frame, not a guideline":** mild first episode, low local resistance → ceftriaxone or piperacillin–tazobactam (pip-tazo first choice of 70.9% in the BARD survey); moderate/severe or septic → meropenem; breakthrough on prophylaxis → different class; gram-positive cocci or no response → add vancomycin; no improvement at 72 h → escalate, repeat culture, repeat ultrasound (lakes, abscess, line). Narrow on culture. Wang 2023 (Int J Surg 2023, doi:10.1097/JS9.0000000000000682; 14-centre RCT, open-label): mild — cefoperazone–sulbactam = meropenem; severe — meropenem + IVIG shortened fever 52 vs 68 h; recurrence data inconsistent. IVIG = adjunct for severe/intractable, not routine. **Build:** present as "Expert opinion / local antibiogram"; no IV doses unless from a pediatric dosing reference — TODO(verify) at build; no local protocols. Cefoperazone–sulbactam is not available in the US — Learn layer only. TODO(verify) at build: ceftriaxone in a jaundiced young infant — check label limits (hyperbilirubinemic neonates, IV calcium) and biliary pseudolithiasis before listing it; slide 19 also warns a ceftriaxone habit fails where Klebsiella/enterococci dominate.
- **Duration (slide 12):** suspected 10–14 days, confirmed 14–21 days IV (BARD Delphi; 7–10 days judged too short) — same as 03-06. No data for early oral switch. Intractable: home IV 6–12 weeks reported (20-patient series — do not publish as a recommendation). No steroids for cholangitis (no evidence; Japanese BA guideline 2021, Ando H et al. J Hepatobiliary Pancreat Sci 2021;28:55–61, makes no steroid recommendation). Intractable cholangitis + failing drainage → transplant assessment. Japanese BA guideline 2nd edition appeared in 2026 (per the lesson chapter) — check for a cholangitis/steroid statement at build (§6).
- **Prophylaxis (slides 13–15):** meta-analyses null — Decharun 2016 (Clin Pediatr 2016;55:66–72; 4 studies, 329 infants, too weak); Alatas 2023 (JPGN 2023;77:648–54; 6 studies, 714 infants, OR 1.15, 0.59–2.25, I² 68%); Chen D & Chen G 2026 (Front Pediatr 2026; cholangitis 56.9% vs 56.6%, OR 0.88, 0.41–1.92; native-liver survival OR 2.15 from 2 studies only — "hypothetical"). Brody 2025: on primary prophylaxis the first episode came earlier (median 77 vs 239 days, P = .016). RCT NCT05925309 (Shanghai, TMP-SMX alternating with cefaclor, day 15–month 6, n = 356) expected ~2029. Bu 2003 regimens (secondary prevention): trimethoprim 4 mg/kg/day; neomycin 25 mg/kg/day 4 days a week. **Build:** keep prophylaxis as Learn-layer text and guideline_compare; do not publish a dose in the Duty layer from a 9–10-infant trial without a pediatric dosing reference (TMP-SMX labeling < 2 months still applies — see 03-06). Practical: one agent, a written stop date (review at 12 months), breakthrough → different class; prophylaxis never replaces cultures + full IV course.
- **Post-operative IV course (slide 13):** Chen 2021 early cholangitis 61% (7 days) vs 38% (14 days), P = .02; 6-month cholangitis and native-liver survival similar. The 03-06 Inbox recorded the published abstract as 62% vs 70% at 6 months — still TODO(verify) against the full text; publish only the outcome confirmed there. Pang 2019 (Surg Infect 2019;20:231–5): imipenem vs cephalosporin + metronidazole early cholangitis 9.9% vs 59.2% — retrospective, do not publish as advice.
- **Bile lakes (slides 16–17):** 6–26% after the Kasai (Bu 2002, J Pediatr Surg 2002;37:1183–7: any cyst 25.3%, multiple 10.4%; 93.8% of multiple-cyst infants had prior cholangitis). Single lake near the porta = often a pseudocyst → prolonged IV antibiotics, consider drainage (percutaneous: 6/7 resolved but biliary peritonitis 3/7 — Pediatr Surg Int 2024, doi:10.1007/s00383-024-05857-z). Multiple/complex true-duct dilatation → recurrence, worse prognosis → transplant assessment if recurrent cholangitis or failing drainage. Bile-lake jejunostomy (Ginström 2019, JPGN 2019;69:488–94; n = 6). Ultrasound at every episode. All tiny series → "centre with hepatobiliary surgery and transplant". Optional link → duct_dilation for the differential of intrahepatic cysts (not in the §4.5 map for 33; decide at build, record in DECISIONS). No atresia-specific biloma data.

- **Pearls/pitfalls (slides 19–20):** "No fever, so not cholangitis" · "Culture negative, so viral" · "The Kasai has failed" · "She is on prophylaxis" (breakthrough common, change class) · "Oral after 3 days" (consensus 10–21 days IV) · a ceftriaxone habit where Klebsiella/enterococci dominate.
