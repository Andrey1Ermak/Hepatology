### 02-11 · Syndromic cholestasis (synced 2026-10-19)
- Dysmorphism plus cholestasis: run a syndrome workup **alongside** the standard algorithm, never instead of it. Acholic stools still mean a cholangiogram early enough to allow a Kasai before about 60 days of age, whatever the face looks like (lesson 02-11 slide 18 and chapter; keep in line with the 30/45/60 Kasai clock — the day-60 figure is the lesson's, not Fawaz 2017).
- **Safety, day 1:** contractures or renal Fanconi → suspect ARC → **no percutaneous liver (or kidney) biopsy** (7/16 biopsied children had life-threatening hemorrhage, normal platelet count in 9/11 — Gissen 2006). Blasts on the film → hematology now.
- Day-1 blood in a dysmorphic cholestatic infant: GGT, glucose with insulin, TSH, cortisol, blood film for blasts, plasma 7-DHC, VLCFA, urine for Fanconi (lesson slide 18). Plan biopsy and Kasai decisions with the genome result still pending.
- Bedside, day 1: face, hands, feet, skin, genitals, joints; stool color; murmur; spleen. Contractures, Fanconi, skin, glucose and the blood film are part of the liver exam.
- Down syndrome: cholestasis in ~4% of newborns (8/206, Arnell 2012); all 8 had congenital heart disease; severe cholestasis only with TAM. Send a **blood film for blasts** in every cholestatic DS infant; blasts > 10% + hepatomegaly + ascites = TAM until proven otherwise → hematology same day (Roberts 2013). Also TSH/free T4 (congenital hypothyroidism ~1% in DS).
- TAM: low-dose cytarabine cut early death 33% → 12% and liver fibrosis 7/104 → 0/102 (Flasche 2018, TMD Prevention 2007). Treats the liver, not the clone (ML-DS still ~25%).
- No proven excess of biliary atresia in DS: run the atresia pathway as in any infant (5-week-old DS, pale stools, GGT 450 → ultrasound, then cholangiogram).
- Hypoglycemia + cholestasis is not only hypopituitarism: critical sample with insulin, beta-hydroxybutyrate, free fatty acids. Kabuki (KMT2D, KDM6A): hyperinsulinism ~4%, diazoxide-responsive 92%, often diagnosed before the syndrome (Rosenfeld 2024).
- Trisomy 18: median survival 9 days (Nelson 2016); the liver is rarely the decision → goals-of-care discussion.
- BASM: 10.2% of 548 atresias, native-liver survival 46% at 5 y (Davenport 2006); maternal diabetes is a clue.
- Genetics: a cholestasis panel is not a syndrome test. Dysmorphic infant → rapid aneuploidy test/karyotype/microarray, then trio genome or exome with copy-number analysis (sequence = lesson slide 18; ACMG 2021 only says exome/genome first- or second-tier in congenital anomalies). Panel yield 12–26%, rapid genome in sick infants 46–57% (slide 17).
- Guideline compare candidates: syndromes in differential (NASPGHAN/ESPGHAN 2017 Table 1 vs EASL 2024 — DIFFERS); reanalysis at least every 3 years (EASL only — DIFFERS); karyotype/microarray (GAP); TAM, SLOS not in liver guidelines (GAP). EASL 2024 = Verkade HJ et al. J Hepatol 2024;81:303–25 → add to guidelines.json on day 3+.
- Case candidates (slide 22): DS day 3 with blasts 30% → TAM; 6-week-old with contractures, GGT 25, glycosuria → ARC, no biopsy.

### 02-12 · Genetics in the infant (synced 2026-10-20)
- **Core rule (lesson slides 12–13, 18):** genetics runs **beside** the cholangiogram, never ahead of it. A result can remove the reason to operate; it can never supply a reason to wait. Acholic stools → cholangiogram on the atresia timetable, whatever is pending.
- Parallel pathway (slide 12, built on Fawaz 2017): day 0 — fractionated bilirubin, stool color, GGT, ultrasound, exclude sepsis/galactosemia/hypothyroidism/hypopituitarism; day 0–1 — same-day sample for rapid trio genome or broad panel **with del/dup**, alpha-1 antitrypsin Pi type + level, sweat test or CFTR; day 1–3 — Alagille screen (echo, spine film, eye exam for posterior embryotoxon) **before** the cholangiogram (Fawaz 2017: pre-op ALGS and CF testing helps interpret the cholangiogram); day 3–7 — cholangiogram on the atresia timetable: neither the Alagille screen nor a pending result may push the date.
- Turnaround (slide 3): rapid exome/genome about 2–6 days; cholestasis panel about 3 weeks (median 21 d, Karpen 2021). Only a rapid test can return before the cholangiogram.
- **Alagille is the dangerous mimic** (slides 10–11): Alagille + Kasai → transplant 47% vs 14%, death 32% vs 3% (Kaye 2010, n = 19 vs 36; association, not proven cause); meta-analysis OR 6.46 for transplant (Fujishiro 2018). 9/14 Alagille infants explored surgically (Zhou 2025). Normal gallbladder on ultrasound argues for Alagille (abnormal 64% vs 96% in BA) but does not exclude atresia — with acholic stools the cholangiogram stays on schedule; GGT overlaps (median 305 vs 515 U/L); cardiac anomaly 79% vs 25% → **echo before the cholangiogram** in any high-GGT infant. Early biopsy may show ductular proliferation, not paucity; HIDA non-excretion also occurs with paucity.
- What a result does before surgery (slides 12–13): a result cancels surgery **only if it explains the phenotype**; persistent acholic stools → cholangiogram anyway. JAG1/NOTCH2 pathogenic + patent, hypoplastic ducts → argues against Kasai, cholangiogram only if atresia still suspected; biallelic ABCC2 (neonatal Dubin–Johnson: high conjugated bilirubin, near-normal transaminases) → no surgery; biallelic SLC25A13 (NICCD) → no surgery, lactose-free MCT formula; biallelic ATP8B1/ABCB11/TJP2/USP53 (phased with parents) + low GGT → no surgery; two CFTR variants → usually no surgery, but inspissated bile may still need drainage; one ABCB4/ABCB11 variant, ADD3/PKD1L1 risk allele or VUS → **no change**, proceed.
- No biliary atresia gene test exists (slide 14): susceptibility findings are research information; BASM/ciliopathy findings mean screening heart, kidneys, laterality.
- Ask for copy-number (del/dup): whole-gene JAG1 deletions ~6.4%, NOTCH2 3.6% of probands (Vandriel 2025) — sequencing-only tests miss them.
- Pi type and level belong in tier 1 (SERPINA1 2nd commonest definitive diagnosis in US panels, Hoskins 2026; not on every panel; level alone insufficient — Fawaz 2017).
- Guideline compare candidates (slide 16): when to test (ALIGNED), which test (Fawaz panel/exome, ACMG exome/genome first- or second-tier for congenital anomalies vs EASL panel/WES, WGS if negative — DIFFERS), rapid genome in ICU (GAP; NHS R14 only), alpha-1 antitrypsin Pi type + level in tier 1 (ALIGNED), Alagille before surgery (Fawaz yes, EASL silent — DIFFERS), genetics vs cholangiogram timing (Fawaz: expedite the atresia workup, especially after 6 weeks; no guideline states a pending result must not delay — MATERIAL), reanalysis (ACMG no interval vs EASL ≥ every 3 y — DIFFERS).
- Case candidates (slide 19): 5-week-old, pale stools, GGT 480, small gallbladder → atresia pathway now + same-day rapid trio genome; 6-week-old, murmur, butterfly vertebra, GGT 300, gallbladder seen → echo, eye exam, JAG1/NOTCH2 with del/dup, cholangiogram only if still needed; 4-week-old East Asian, stools pigmented, conjugated bilirubin 8 mg/dL, normal ALT → ABCC2/SLC25A13, citrulline and panel before any surgery.
- Note for card build: state genetic yield as phenotype-dependent (4–71%) and use GEMINI (genome 49% vs panel 27%) for panel vs genome — supersedes the 02-11 "12–26% / 46–57%" phrasing.

### 02-13 · Supportive care in the cholestatic infant (synced 2026-10-21)
- **Core idea (slide 2):** treat the cholestasis, not only the cause. From week 1, whatever the diagnosis, every cholestatic infant needs the same supportive program: nutrition, fat-soluble vitamins, itch control, surveillance. The diagnostic guideline (Fawaz 2017) stops at the diagnosis; supportive care rests on the joint NASPGHAN/ESPGHAN nutrition paper (Mouzaki M et al. JPGN 2019;69:498–511) → add to guidelines.json at card build.
- **Urine bile acids before the first dose of UDCA** (Fawaz 2017 — its only UDCA statement): bile acid synthesis defects are treatable with cholic acid, and UDCA blurs the mass-spectrometry profile. Order is fixed: send urine, then start the drug.
- UDCA dose 10–20 mg/kg/day; do not escalate for a stubborn bilirubin (adult PSC trial at 28–30 mg/kg/day: primary endpoint 39% vs 26%, serious AE 63% vs 37% — Lindor 2009; adult data, different disease). Evidence after Kasai is biochemical only: liver tests worsened in 13/16 off drug (mean ALT 1.4 → 3.0 × ULN, GGT 4.2 → 8.0 × ULN), all improved on restart (Willot 2008); cohort dose 25 mg/kg/day (range 20–36), above the advised 10–20 and overlapping the adult harm range; no RCT, no survival endpoint.
- **Vitamin K, day 1 (slide 10):** INR on day 1 in every jaundiced infant; parenteral vitamin K if prolonged; correction within hours → deficiency, not synthetic failure (→ inr_prolonged). **Trap:** breast-fed infant with no prophylaxis or a single oral dose at birth — intracranial bleed can be the first sign of biliary atresia or AATD (19 UK cases with liver disease, McNinch 2007).
- Fat-soluble vitamins after diagnosis: a combined TPGS multivitamin is a start, not a plan. Bilirubin ≥ 2 mg/dL at 6 months after Kasai → 0/23 sufficient in all of A, D, E, K (ChiLDREN, Shneider 2012). Once bilirubin stays ≥ 2: dose each vitamin separately, measure each (doses and markers → c_vitamins).
- Nutrition targets (Mouzaki 2019): energy 130% of age requirement; protein 130–150% (do not restrict; any restriction for severe encephalopathy ≤ 2–3 days); fat 30–50% of calories, MCT:LCT start 30:70, MCT < 80% of fat; linoleic acid ≥ 3% of calories. Tube feed if energy goals unmet > 2 weeks or growth poor 2–4 weeks; avoid gastrostomy with portal hypertension. Breast milk alone rarely meets targets — fortify with MCT-rich formula.
- **Weight lies (slide 13):** liver, spleen and ascites add weight. Measure length, mid-upper arm circumference (MUAC) and triceps skinfold every 2–4 weeks; head circumference every visit. Rising weight z-score with a growing spleen is not catch-up growth.
- Growth predicts outcome: weight z −2.1 vs −1.2 at 6 months after Kasai separated transplant/death from native-liver survival (DeRusso 2007, n = 100). Growth faltering after Kasai → tube feeding **and** transplant referral in parallel.
- Surveillance (slide 17): spleen and platelets every visit; pulse oximetry upright and supine (HPS, < 97% → investigate; → hps_poph); accelerated vaccines, MMR and varicella from 6 months, before transplant (Martinez 2026 AASLD/AST/NASPGHAN transplant evaluation guideline — TODO(verify) citation and the 6-month MMR/varicella statement before publishing).
- IBAT inhibitors after Kasai: no benefit (EMBARK missed bilirubin endpoint; BOLD missed native-liver survival, company report 2026 — TODO(verify)). Do not start outside a trial.
- Guideline compare candidates (slide 18): UDCA in cholestatic infants (Fawaz: screen first; EASL: not first line — ALIGNED); UDCA after Kasai (no graded recommendation anywhere — GAP); vitamin doses/markers and energy/protein/MCT (same joint paper — ALIGNED); transplant evaluation (2026 North American guideline, no European pediatric equivalent — MATERIAL).
- Case candidates (slide 21): 6-week-old, cause unknown, team wants UDCA → urine bile acids first; 4 months after Kasai, bilirubin 3.2, on ADEK drops only → measure A, D, E and INR, add separate vitamin D, TPGS and daily vitamin K, recheck in 4–6 weeks; breast-fed 7-week-old, single oral vitamin K at birth, irritable, INR 6 → treat as VKDB: IV vitamin K, head imaging, cholestasis workup; post-Kasai infant gaining weight, spleen growing, MUAC falling → NG MCT-rich feeds + transplant referral.
