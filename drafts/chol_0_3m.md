### 02-11 · Syndromic cholestasis (synced 2026-10-19)
- Dysmorphism plus cholestasis: run a syndrome workup **alongside** the standard algorithm, never instead of it. Acholic stools still mean a cholangiogram early enough to allow a Kasai before about 60 days of age, whatever the face looks like (lesson 02-11 slide 18 and chapter; keep in line with the 30/45/60 Kasai clock — the day-60 figure is the lesson's, not Fawaz 2017).
- **Safety, day 1:** contractures or renal Fanconi → suspect ARC → **no percutaneous liver (or kidney) biopsy** (7/16 biopsied children had life-threatening hemorrhage, normal platelet count in 9/11 — Gissen 2006). Blasts on the film → hematology now.
- Day-1 blood in a dysmorphic cholestatic infant: GGT, glucose with insulin, TSH, cortisol, blood film for blasts, plasma 7-DHC, VLCFA, urine for Fanconi (lesson slide 18). Plan biopsy and Kasai decisions with the genome result still pending.
- Bedside, day 1: face, hands, feet, skin, genitals, joints; stool color; murmur; spleen. Contractures, Fanconi, skin, glucose and the blood film are part of the liver exam.
- Down syndrome: cholestasis in ~4% of newborns (8/206, Arnell 2012); all 8 had congenital heart disease; severe cholestasis only with TAM. Send a **blood film for blasts** in every cholestatic DS infant; blasts > 10% + hepatomegaly + ascites = TAM until proven otherwise → hematology same day (Roberts 2013). Also TSH/free T4 (congenital hypothyroidism ~1% in DS).
- TAM: low-dose cytarabine cut early death 33% → 12% and liver fibrosis 7/104 → 0/102 (Flasche 2018, TMD Prevention 2007). Treats the liver, not the clone (ML-DS still ~25%).
- No proven excess of biliary atresia in DS: run the atresia pathway as in any infant (5-week-old DS, pale stools, GGT 450 → ultrasound, then cholangiogram).
- Hypoglycemia + cholestasis is not only hypopituitarism: critical sample with insulin, beta-hydroxybutyrate, free fatty acids. Kabuki (KMT2D, KDM6A): hyperinsulinism ~4%, diazoxide-responsive 92%, often diagnosed before the syndrome (Rosenfeld 2024).
- Trisomy 18: median survival 9 days (Nelson 2016); the liver is rarely the decision → goals-of-care discussion.
- BASM: 10.2% of 548 atresias, native-liver survival 46% at 5 y (Davenport 2006); maternal diabetes is a clue.
- Genetics: a cholestasis panel is not a syndrome test. Dysmorphic infant → rapid aneuploidy test/karyotype/microarray, then trio genome or exome with copy-number analysis (sequence = lesson slide 18; ACMG 2021 only says exome/genome first- or second-tier in congenital anomalies). Panel yield 12–26%, rapid genome in sick infants 46–57% (slide 17).
- Guideline compare candidates: syndromes in differential (NASPGHAN/ESPGHAN 2017 Table 1 vs EASL 2024 — DIFFERS); reanalysis at least every 3 years (EASL only — DIFFERS); karyotype/microarray (GAP); TAM, SLOS not in liver guidelines (GAP). EASL 2024 = Verkade HJ et al. J Hepatol 2024;81:303–25 → add to guidelines.json on day 3+.
- Case candidates (slide 22): DS day 3 with blasts 30% → TAM; 6-week-old with contractures, GGT 25, glycosuria → ARC, no biopsy.
