### 02-11 · Syndromic cholestasis (synced 2026-10-19)
- Dysmorphism plus cholestasis: run a syndrome workup **alongside** the standard algorithm, never instead of it. Acholic stools still mean a cholangiogram early enough to allow a Kasai before about 60 days of age, whatever the face looks like (lesson 02-11 slide 18 and chapter; keep in line with the 30/45/60 Kasai clock — the day-60 figure is the lesson's, not Fawaz 2017).
- **Safety, day 1:** contractures or renal Fanconi → suspect ARC → **no percutaneous liver (or kidney) biopsy** (7/16 biopsied children had life-threatening hemorrhage, normal platelet count in 9/11 — Gissen 2006). Blasts on the film → hematology now.
- Day-1 blood in a dysmorphic cholestatic infant: GGT, glucose with insulin, TSH, cortisol, blood film for blasts, plasma 7-DHC, VLCFA, urine for Fanconi (lesson slide 18). Plan biopsy and Kasai decisions with the genome result still pending.
- Bedside, day 1: face, hands, feet, skin, genitals, joints; stool color; murmur; spleen. Contractures, Fanconi, skin, glucose and the blood film are part of the liver exam.
- Down syndrome: cholestasis in ~4% of newborns (8/206, Arnell 2012); all 8 had congenital heart disease; severe cholestasis only with TAM. Send a **blood film for blasts** in every cholestatic DS infant; blasts > 10% + hepatomegaly + ascites = TAM until proven otherwise → hematology same day (Roberts 2013). Also TSH/free T4 (congenital hypothyroidism ~1% in DS).
- TAM: low-dose cytarabine cut early death 33% → 12% and liver fibrosis 7/104 → 0/102 (Flasche 2018, TMD Prevention 2007). Treats the liver, not the clone (ML-DS still ~25%).
- No proven excess of biliary atresia in DS: run the atresia pathway as in any infant (5-week-old DS, pale stools, GGT 450 → ultrasound, then cholangiogram).
- Hypoglycemia + cholestasis is not only hypopituitarism: critical sample with insulin, beta-hydroxybutyrate, free fatty acids. Kabuki (KMT2D, KDM6A): hyperinsulinism ~4%, diazoxide-responsive 92%, often diagnosed before the syndrome (Rosenfeld 2024).
- Trisomy 18: median survival 9 days (Nelson 2016); the liver is rarely the decision → goals-of-care discussion.
- BASM: 10.2% of 548 atresias, native-liver survival 46% at 5 y (Davenport 2006); maternal diabetes is a clue.
- Genetics: a cholestasis panel is not a syndrome test. Dysmorphic infant → rapid aneuploidy test/karyotype/microarray, then trio genome or exome with copy-number analysis (sequence = lesson slide 18; ACMG 2021 only says exome/genome first- or second-tier in congenital anomalies). Panel yield 12–26%, rapid genome in sick infants 46–57% (slide 17).
- Guideline compare candidates: syndromes in differential (NASPGHAN/ESPGHAN 2017 Table 1 vs EASL 2024 — DIFFERS); reanalysis at least every 3 years (EASL only — DIFFERS); karyotype/microarray (GAP); TAM, SLOS not in liver guidelines (GAP). EASL 2024 = Verkade HJ et al. J Hepatol 2024;81:303–25 → add to guidelines.json on day 3+.
- Case candidates (slide 22): DS day 3 with blasts 30% → TAM; 6-week-old with contractures, GGT 25, glycosuria → ARC, no biopsy.

### 02-12 · Genetics in the infant (synced 2026-10-20)
- **Core rule (lesson slides 12–13, 18):** genetics runs **beside** the cholangiogram, never ahead of it. A result can remove the reason to operate; it can never supply a reason to wait. Acholic stools → cholangiogram on the atresia timetable, whatever is pending.
- Parallel pathway (slide 12, built on Fawaz 2017): day 0 — fractionated bilirubin, stool color, GGT, ultrasound, exclude sepsis/galactosemia/hypothyroidism/hypopituitarism; day 0–1 — same-day sample for rapid trio genome or broad panel **with del/dup**, alpha-1 antitrypsin Pi type + level, sweat test or CFTR; day 1–3 — Alagille screen (echo, spine film, eye exam for posterior embryotoxon) **before** the cholangiogram (Fawaz 2017: pre-op ALGS and CF testing helps interpret the cholangiogram); day 3–7 — cholangiogram on the atresia timetable: neither the Alagille screen nor a pending result may push the date.
- Turnaround (slide 3): rapid exome/genome about 2–6 days; cholestasis panel about 3 weeks (median 21 d, Karpen 2021). Only a rapid test can return before the cholangiogram.
- **Alagille is the dangerous mimic** (slides 10–11): Alagille + Kasai → transplant 47% vs 14%, death 32% vs 3% (Kaye 2010, n = 19 vs 36; association, not proven cause); meta-analysis OR 6.46 for transplant (Fujishiro 2018). 9/14 Alagille infants explored surgically (Zhou 2025). Normal gallbladder on ultrasound argues for Alagille (abnormal 64% vs 96% in BA) but does not exclude atresia — with acholic stools the cholangiogram stays on schedule; GGT overlaps (median 305 vs 515 U/L); cardiac anomaly 79% vs 25% → **echo before the cholangiogram** in any high-GGT infant. Early biopsy may show ductular proliferation, not paucity; HIDA non-excretion also occurs with paucity.
- What a result does before surgery (slides 12–13): a result cancels surgery **only if it explains the phenotype**; persistent acholic stools → cholangiogram anyway. JAG1/NOTCH2 pathogenic + patent, hypoplastic ducts → argues against Kasai, cholangiogram only if atresia still suspected; biallelic ABCC2 (neonatal Dubin–Johnson: high conjugated bilirubin, near-normal transaminases) → no surgery; biallelic SLC25A13 (NICCD) → no surgery, lactose-free MCT formula; biallelic ATP8B1/ABCB11/TJP2/USP53 (phased with parents) + low GGT → no surgery; two CFTR variants → usually no surgery, but inspissated bile may still need drainage; one ABCB4/ABCB11 variant, ADD3/PKD1L1 risk allele or VUS → **no change**, proceed.
- No biliary atresia gene test exists (slide 14): susceptibility findings are research information; BASM/ciliopathy findings mean screening heart, kidneys, laterality.
- Ask for copy-number (del/dup): whole-gene JAG1 deletions ~6.4%, NOTCH2 3.6% of probands (Vandriel 2025) — sequencing-only tests miss them.
- Pi type and level belong in tier 1 (SERPINA1 2nd commonest definitive diagnosis in US panels, Hoskins 2026; not on every panel; level alone insufficient — Fawaz 2017).
- Guideline compare candidates (slide 16): when to test (ALIGNED), which test (Fawaz panel/exome, ACMG exome/genome first- or second-tier for congenital anomalies vs EASL panel/WES, WGS if negative — DIFFERS), rapid genome in ICU (GAP; NHS R14 only), alpha-1 antitrypsin Pi type + level in tier 1 (ALIGNED), Alagille before surgery (Fawaz yes, EASL silent — DIFFERS), genetics vs cholangiogram timing (Fawaz: expedite the atresia workup, especially after 6 weeks; no guideline states a pending result must not delay — MATERIAL), reanalysis (ACMG no interval vs EASL ≥ every 3 y — DIFFERS).
- Case candidates (slide 19): 5-week-old, pale stools, GGT 480, small gallbladder → atresia pathway now + same-day rapid trio genome; 6-week-old, murmur, butterfly vertebra, GGT 300, gallbladder seen → echo, eye exam, JAG1/NOTCH2 with del/dup, cholangiogram only if still needed; 4-week-old East Asian, stools pigmented, conjugated bilirubin 8 mg/dL, normal ALT → ABCC2/SLC25A13, citrulline and panel before any surgery.
- Note for card build: state genetic yield as phenotype-dependent (4–71%) and use GEMINI (genome 49% vs panel 27%) for panel vs genome — supersedes the 02-11 "12–26% / 46–57%" phrasing.

### 02-13 · Supportive care in the cholestatic infant (synced 2026-10-21)
- **Core idea (slide 2):** treat the cholestasis, not only the cause. From week 1, whatever the diagnosis, every cholestatic infant needs the same supportive program: nutrition, fat-soluble vitamins, itch control, surveillance. The diagnostic guideline (Fawaz 2017) stops at the diagnosis; supportive care rests on the joint NASPGHAN/ESPGHAN nutrition paper (Mouzaki M et al. JPGN 2019;69:498–511) → add to guidelines.json at card build.
- **Urine bile acids before the first dose of UDCA** (Fawaz 2017 — its only UDCA statement): bile acid synthesis defects are treatable with cholic acid, and UDCA blurs the mass-spectrometry profile. Order is fixed: send urine, then start the drug.
- UDCA dose 10–20 mg/kg/day; do not escalate for a stubborn bilirubin (adult PSC trial at 28–30 mg/kg/day: primary endpoint 39% vs 26%, serious AE 63% vs 37% — Lindor 2009; adult data, different disease). Evidence after Kasai is biochemical only: liver tests worsened in 13/16 off drug (mean ALT 1.4 → 3.0 × ULN, GGT 4.2 → 8.0 × ULN), all improved on restart (Willot 2008); cohort dose 25 mg/kg/day (range 20–36), above the advised 10–20 and overlapping the adult harm range; no RCT, no survival endpoint.
- **Vitamin K, day 1 (slide 10):** INR on day 1 in every jaundiced infant; parenteral vitamin K if prolonged; correction within hours → deficiency, not synthetic failure (→ inr_prolonged). **Trap:** breast-fed infant with no prophylaxis or a single oral dose at birth — intracranial bleed can be the first sign of biliary atresia or AATD (19 UK cases with liver disease, McNinch 2007).
- Fat-soluble vitamins after diagnosis: a combined TPGS multivitamin is a start, not a plan. Bilirubin ≥ 2 mg/dL at 6 months after Kasai → 0/23 sufficient in all of A, D, E, K (ChiLDREN, Shneider 2012). Once bilirubin stays ≥ 2: dose each vitamin separately, measure each (doses and markers → c_vitamins).
- Nutrition targets (Mouzaki 2019): energy 130% of age requirement; protein 130–150% (do not restrict; any restriction for severe encephalopathy ≤ 2–3 days); fat 30–50% of calories, MCT:LCT start 30:70, MCT < 80% of fat; linoleic acid ≥ 3% of calories. TODO(verify) MCT share against Mouzaki 2019 full text (lesson slide 2 says "MCT 30–50% of fat", which conflicts with slide 11 — do not copy slide 2). Tube feed if energy goals unmet > 2 weeks or growth poor 2–4 weeks; avoid gastrostomy with portal hypertension. Breast milk alone rarely meets targets — fortify with MCT-rich formula.
- **Weight lies (slide 13):** liver, spleen and ascites add weight. Measure length, mid-upper arm circumference (MUAC) and triceps skinfold every 2–4 weeks; head circumference every visit. Rising weight z-score with a growing spleen is not catch-up growth.
- Growth predicts outcome: weight z −2.1 vs −1.2 at 6 months after Kasai separated transplant/death from native-liver survival (DeRusso 2007, n = 100). Growth faltering after Kasai → tube feeding **and** transplant referral in parallel.
- Surveillance (slide 17): spleen and platelets every visit; pulse oximetry upright and supine (HPS, < 97% → investigate; → hps_poph); accelerated vaccines, MMR and varicella from 6 months, before transplant (Martinez 2026 AASLD/AST/NASPGHAN transplant evaluation guideline — TODO(verify) citation and the 6-month MMR/varicella statement before publishing).
- IBAT inhibitors after Kasai: no benefit (EMBARK missed bilirubin endpoint; BOLD missed native-liver survival, company report 2026 — TODO(verify)). Do not start outside a trial.
- Guideline compare candidates (slide 18): UDCA in cholestatic infants (Fawaz: screen first; EASL: not first line — ALIGNED); UDCA after Kasai (no graded recommendation anywhere — GAP); vitamin doses/markers and energy/protein/MCT (same joint paper — ALIGNED); transplant evaluation (2026 North American guideline, no European pediatric equivalent — MATERIAL).
- Case candidates (slide 21): 6-week-old, cause unknown, team wants UDCA → urine bile acids first; 4 months after Kasai, bilirubin 3.2, on ADEK drops only → measure A, D, E and INR, add separate vitamin D, TPGS and daily vitamin K, recheck in 4–6 weeks; breast-fed 7-week-old, single oral vitamin K at birth, irritable, INR 6 → treat as VKDB: IV vitamin K, head imaging, cholestasis workup; post-Kasai infant gaining weight, spleen growing, MUAC falling → NG MCT-rich feeds + transplant referral.

### 02-14 · Block 2 synthesis (synced 2026-10-22)
- **Organizing idea for the card (slide 2):** one sign, many clocks — hours (sepsis, galactosemia, adrenal/pituitary failure), weeks (biliary atresia), months (nutrition, vitamins, growth). Order the workup by the fastest clock, not the commonest disease → Red flags and the day-1 list come **before** the atresia section.
- **Day-1 list before the atresia workup (slides 10, 19):** urine culture, urine reducing substances, glucose, INR, TSH **with free T4**, cortisol, parenteral vitamin K.
- Seven mechanisms → first action (slide 3), usable as the skeleton of the Algorithm / Don't miss sections: obstruction (pale stool, high GGT, firm liver → fasting US, then biopsy or cholangiogram); inflammatory signaling (sepsis, E. coli UTI, CMV, syphilis, HSV; fever may be absent → urine culture; acyclovir if liver failure, before the HSV PCR); hormone deficiency (hypoglycemia, micropenis, midline defects → cortisol + free T4 now, hydrocortisone before levothyroxine); toxic metabolite (galactosemia, tyrosinemia, citrin, HFI after weaning; coagulopathy out of proportion → stop lactose, succinylacetone, GALT before transfusion); transport/synthesis (PFIC, Alagille, BASD, A1AT → urine bile acids before UDCA, genome); infiltration/alloimmune (HLH, NPC, Wolman, GALD; splenomegaly, ferritin, liver failure at birth → HLH criteria, lip biopsy/MRI for extrahepatic iron, IVIG + exchange); iatrogenic/preterm (PN, NEC, surgery → enteral feeds, lipid change, **still exclude atresia**).
- **Galactosemia (slide 11, Levy 1977):** gram-negative sepsis + cholestasis + coagulopathy = galactosemia until proven otherwise; 4/10 galactosemic infants had E. coli infection, 3 died. Stop lactose on suspicion, before results. **Red-cell GALT before any transfusion** (donor cells → falsely normal for months). Tyrosinemia: **succinylacetone, not tyrosine**.
- **Hypopituitarism (slide 12, Braslavsky 2011):** hypoglycemia in 14/16 hypopituitary cholestatic infants — best bedside clue; GGT normal in about half (mimics PFIC); normal TSH does not exclude central hypothyroidism → free T4; random cortisol < 5.6 µg/dL specific, normal value excludes nothing — TODO(verify) the 5.6 µg/dL cut-off against Braslavsky 2011 before publishing. Hydrocortisone before levothyroxine; pituitary MRI; jaundice clears in 2–6 months; adrenal crisis is the threat to life.
- **Kasai clock, evidence (slide 6):** native-liver survival at 4 y 49% (Kasai ≤ 30 d) / 36% (31–90 d) / 23% (> 90 d), Canada, n = 349 (Schreiber 2007). Texas two-stage direct bilirubin screen (Harpavat 2020 JAMA): 7/7 atresia among 124,385 newborns, specificity 99.9%, PPV 5.9%, sensitivity lower 95% bound ~56%; mean Kasai 56 → 36 d (P = 0.004); Kasai ≤ 30 d 12.5% → 57.9%; normal bilirubin by 90 d 41.7% → 78.9%; 1-y transplant-free 70.8% → 94.7% (P = 0.06, trend only).
- **Kasai target — conflict to resolve at card build:** slide 2 says "Kasai before 30–45 days", case 1 slide "before day 45 at the latest" (chapter: "well before the end of the sixth week"), 02-11 "before about 60 days"; AAP 2025 says before 30 days; European documents give no number (UK median ~50 d). Use the c_kasai_clock 30/45/60 bands, show the difference in guideline_compare (MATERIAL), record the wording in DECISIONS.
- **Etiology (slide 8, Gottesman 2015, n = 1,692):** idiopathic neonatal hepatitis 26.0%, BA 25.9%, infection 11.5%, PN 6.4%, metabolic 4.4%, A1AT 4.1%, hypopituitarism/hypothyroidism 2.0%. Causes that harm within days (infection, metabolic, endocrine) ≈ 1 in 6 (slide title; slide text says "about 18%"). Idiopathic share shrinks with genetics (≤ ~14% unnamed with modern algorithms).
- **Alagille before the cholangiogram (case 4):** acholic stools, non-excreting HIDA and ductular reaction on biopsy all occur in Alagille. Echo, spine film, eye exam first; cholangiogram only if the duct is still in doubt; **no portoenterostomy if the duct is patent**. "A genetic result may cancel a Kasai; a pending result may never delay a cholangiogram." (Consistent with 02-12 Inbox.)
- **Atresia from birth (case 1, Harpavat 2011):** a day-2 direct bilirubin above the lab range is the first signal; stools can be pigmented early in atresia — a normal stool card does not exclude it. Breast milk and thriving explain nothing once direct is > 1.0 mg/dL.
- Infection pearls (slide 15): moderate–severe congenital CMV → valganciclovir 6 months (Kimberlin 2015), never a reason to delay a cholangiogram; neonatal liver failure → acyclovir before the PCR; INR out of proportion → tyrosinemia or GALD.
- Infiltration (slide 16): splenomegaly in a cholestatic infant → storage disease or HLH; ferritin > 10,000 ~90% sensitive for HLH (Allen CE, Pediatr Blood Cancer 2008 — TODO(verify); Henter 2007 HLH-2004 uses ferritin ≥ 500). In a neonate, very high ferritin + liver failure at birth also fits GALD.
- Family (slide 19): after GALD → obstetrics: weekly antenatal IVIG from mid-pregnancy, recurrence ~45% → ~5% (lesson cites Whitington 2014; primary protocol likely Whitington & Kelly, Pediatrics 2008;121:e1615, IVIG from 14–18 weeks' gestation — TODO(verify) start week and citation); familial idiopathic hepatitis → genetics; sporadic INH with pigmented stools usually recovers.
- Supportive-care summary (slide 2): vitamins A, D, E, K by level; energy 130%, MCT; itch ladder; growth by arm circumference (details in 02-13 Inbox).
- **Guideline compare rows (slide 5) — card 2 owns:** target age at Kasai (Fawaz: as early as possible / AAP 2025: before 30 d / Europe: no number, UK median ~50 d — MATERIAL); HIDA (Fawaz: excretion helps exclude / AAP: not addressed / SIGENP 2022: not standard — DIFFERS). Rows on abnormal value, timing, stool color, universal screen → card 1 (neo_jaundice); preterm → card 29.
- Gaps (slide 17) for "Limited data" wording: UDCA after Kasai never placebo-tested; giant-cell hepatitis with autoimmune hemolysis — no guideline, mortality 10–45%; no guideline says antenatal IVIG after GALD; no guideline says a VUS/pending result must not delay the cholangiogram.

Self-test Qs (slide 18) map onto these.

### 03-01 · Biliary atresia: epidemiology and phenotypes (synced 2026-10-23)
- **Atresia is present at birth (slide 3, Harpavat 2011):** conjugated bilirubin is already raised in the first 48 h in infants later diagnosed — act on stool color and conjugated bilirubin, never on the calendar, season or ancestry (consistent with 02-14 Inbox).
- **Phenotype, not one disease (slides 2, 8):** isolated (67–89%), splenic malformation (BASM, ~10% in the West), cystic (5–22% by definition), CMV IgM-positive (5–10% Europe), other syndromic (cat-eye, cardiac, duodenal atresia). Fawaz 2017 split: non-syndromic 84% / other malformation 6% / laterality 10%. Why section: name the phenotype in every case — it changes counseling and outcome benchmarks.
- **Before the Kasai, in every infant with atresia (slide 15):** echocardiogram and ultrasound mapping of portal vein and IVC in the same admission (plan anesthesia for the heart); send karyotype or microarray if dysmorphic — **a pending result never delays the cholangiogram or the Kasai** (consistent with 02-12). → candidate items for the workup "if atresia suspected" group. Slide 15 cites Fawaz 2017, Aldeiri 2020, Allotey 2008, Davenport 2025 — TODO(verify) whether Fawaz 2017 recommends routine echo; until confirmed, mark "Expert opinion".
- **BASM clues (slide 10, Davenport 2006, n = 548):** BASM in 56/548 (10.2%); situs inversus 37% (21/56), cardiac anomaly 45% (25/56), polysplenia or asplenia, preduodenal portal vein, absent intrahepatic IVC, malrotation; maternal diabetes 12.5% vs 1.2%. Native-liver survival 46% at 5 y, 32% at 10 y. Algorithm row: conjugated hyperbilirubinemia or pale stools + laterality defect (dextrocardia, midline liver) → suspect BASM: echo and vein mapping in parallel, plan surgery with cardiology; the cholangiogram stays on the atresia clock.
- **CMV never delays the cholangiogram (slides 12, 18):** CMV can coexist with atresia; pale stools + positive CMV → cholangiogram anyway. Send serum CMV IgM **and** blood or urine PCR at presentation and record both; antivirals after Kasai only within a protocol (Parolini 2019, single-centre, historical controls). Pitfall for the pearls block: "CMV positive, so hepatitis."
- **CMV prognosis (slide 13, Front Pediatr 2021;9:710450, 9 studies, n = 694):** clearance OR 0.47 (0.32–0.69); within 6 mo OR 0.31; within 3 mo OR 0.68 (0.35–1.33), not significant; native-liver loss at 2 y HR 1.59 (0.66–3.81) — not significant; cholangitis not significant (2 studies). Overall clearance from 8 of 9 studies; 42% CMV-positive → selection bias. Wording: "lower clearance; survival effect unproven".
- **Cystic atresia (slide 14):** small hilar cyst + pale stools and raised conjugated bilirubin (chapter wording; either alone → same-day hepatology/surgical review, Expert opinion) = cystic atresia → urgent cholangiogram and Kasai on the atresia clock, **not** elective choledochal cyst surgery. Clues: smaller cyst, small/irregular gallbladder, no intrahepatic duct dilation. Clearance 79% (type I cyst) vs 60% (type III), Japanese registry (Okubo 2025).
- Risk factors for the Why section (slide 6, Cavallo 2022 Texas registry, 305 cases / 4.69 M births): preterm < 32 wk aPR 3.85, maternal diabetes aPR 2.34, female aPR 1.68, preterm 32–37 wk aPR 1.64, Black mother aPR 1.54. Seasonality: not reproducible in large datasets — never use it (slide 7).
- Epidemiology for Why (slides 4, 19): cholestasis ~1 in 2,500 term infants, atresia 25–40% of it (Fawaz 2017); atresia ~1 in 17,000 England and Wales (Livesey 2009), 1 in 14,000 Sweden, ~1 in 6,800 Taiwan, ~1 in 3,100 French Polynesia; ~14-fold range. Ancestry shifts the prior, never excludes.
- Japanese registry (slide 9, Okubo 2025, n = 3,911 typed): type III 86.8%; mean Kasai age 65.2 d (70.6 → 60.6 d, 2011–2023); clearance 57.8% → 64.2%; **vitamin K deficiency bleeding before the Kasai in 8.8%** → supports parenteral vitamin K in the day-1 list (already in 02-14 Inbox).
- **Guideline compare rows (slide 17):** incidence — DIFFERS; classification (3 groups vs 5 phenotypes) — DIFFERS; BASM clues — ALIGNED; role of CMV — MATERIAL (Fawaz: causal role unproven; King's: distinct prognostic subgroup); CMV test (Fawaz: urine PCR, IgM of limited value / King's: serum IgM) — DIFFERS; cystic atresia — ALIGNED; antivirals — GAP; ethnicity and season — GAP. Material gap: no guideline defines, tests for or treats CMV-associated atresia.
- **Cases (slide 20):** (1) antenatal 1.5 cm hilar cyst, pale stools and conjugated bilirubin 2.4 mg/dL at 2 weeks → cystic atresia, urgent cholangiogram and Kasai (chapter says "about 2.5"; use the deck value 2.4); (2) jaundiced 5-week-old, dextrocardia, midline liver → BASM: echo, map portal vein and IVC, plan with cardiology; (3) 7-week-old, pale stools, CMV IgM positive, spleen 4 cm below the costal margin → cholangiogram; record as CMV-associated; antivirals only in protocol.

### 03-02 · Biliary atresia: pathogenesis (synced 2026-10-24)
- **Why section — multi-hit model (slides 2–3, Asai 2015):** susceptible developing duct (ciliary/laterality variants, low duct glutathione) → prenatal or perinatal epithelial insult (virus or toxin) → immune amplification (Th1/IFN-γ, NK cells via NKG2D, B cells; weak regulatory T cells) → obliteration and fibrosis. No single hypothesis explains every phenotype; by the time jaundice is noticed the early steps are complete — partly why adjuvants given after the Kasai have failed (slide 2).
- **Present at birth (slide 4, Harpavat 2011: 61 infants, newborn values in about half):** mean direct bilirubin in the first days 1.4 vs 0.19 mg/dL (P < .0001); most had direct/total ≤ 0.2. Consistent with 02-14/03-01 Inbox: use the absolute value, never the ratio.
- **Adjuvant steroids — not routine (slides 10–11):** START (Bezerra, JAMA 2014;311:1750–9, n = 140; trial regimen, not a recommendation: IV methylprednisolone 4 mg/kg/day × 2 wk, then oral prednisolone 2 mg/kg/day × 2 wk, taper, 13 wk total): bile drainage at 6 mo (total bilirubin < 1.5 mg/dL) 58.6% vs 48.6%, adjusted difference 8.7% (95% CI −10.4 to 27.7), P = .43; transplant-free survival at 24 mo 58.7% vs 59.4%; serious adverse events by day 30 37.2% vs 19.0% (earlier, not more). King's 2007 (Davenport, Hepatology 2007;46:1821–7, n = 73, low dose): transplant at 12 mo 26% vs 35%, NS. Cochrane (Tyraskis 2018, CD008735): no effect on death, transplant or clearance, low certainty. Pearl/pitfall: "Give steroids, it is immune" → not outside a protocol. Dose is for the Why/case text only — **do not** put a steroid regimen in the workup.
- **IVIG (PRIME, Mack JPGN 2019;68:495–501, phase I/IIa, n = 29, 1 g/kg × 3):** no bilirubin benefit; 1-y native-liver survival 58.6% vs 70.5% historical (NS). Rituximab — mechanistic pilot only (Wang, Cell 2020). Antivirals in CMV-positive — non-randomized signals only (consistent with 03-01). N-acetylcysteine after Kasai — lesson says phase 2 ongoing; ClinicalTrials.gov NCT03499249 shows it completed 2024-03 (IV NAC 150 mg/kg/day × 7 days, end point bile acids ≤ 10 µmol/L by 24 wk) — review published results at card build; no clinical use.
- **CMV gradient (slide 7, Fischler, J Clin Med 2022;11:945, 4 European centers):** CMV-positive at Kasai 0% (0/14) under 30 days, 17% (53/308) at 31–70 days, 33% (78/234) over 70 days — fits postnatal acquisition; CMV-positive infants clear jaundice less often (London 37% vs 71%). China, n = 1,276 type III atresia (Zhao, Front Pediatr 2025;13:1577113): 31.3% CMV-positive, older at Kasai (median 68 vs 57 d). Adds to the 03-01 CMV pitfall: a positive CMV test in a 7-week-old with pale stools → cholangiogram anyway.
- **Genetics (slides 14–15):** risk loci, not a Mendelian disease — ADD3 locus (GWAS), GPC1 deletions, rare biallelic PKD1L1 in the splenic-malformation phenotype; 19/19 twin pairs discordant, including monozygotic (Pediatr Surg Int 2020). Counseling line for Why: sibling recurrence is rare; no maternal or pregnancy exposure is proven in humans (biliatresone: animal and in-vitro models only; no human exposure shown).
- **MMP-7 belongs to the injury step (slide 16, Lertudomphonwanit, Sci Transl Med 2017;9:eaan8462):** with GGT, AUC 0.94, sensitivity 97%, specificity 94% in validation; at a 26% prior PPV 85%, NPV 99%. Assay-specific cut-offs come in lesson 03-03 — **do not** publish an MMP-7 cut-off from this lesson.

No AASLD/EASL document addresses atresia pathogenesis.

### 03-03 · Biliary atresia: diagnosis (synced 2026-10-25)
- **Organizing idea (slides 2, 16):** no pre-operative test proves atresia; the intraoperative cholangiogram with remnant histology does (Fawaz 2017, 1A; Ge 2025 Chinese guideline agrees). Every other test estimates probability → move high-probability infants to the OR fast, keep low-probability ones away. **Run labs and ultrasound in parallel, never in series** — serial testing turns a 40-day infant into a 70-day Kasai.
- **Algorithm skeleton (slide 16):** (1) suspect — direct bilirubin > 1.0 mg/dL at any age or pale stools (stool card); day-1 exclusions (sepsis, galactosemia, hypothyroidism, hypopituitarism — consistent with 02-14); (2) labs — GGT, A1AT level **and** phenotype, INR, MMP-7 on a validated platform; (3) fasting US — gallbladder, triangular cord, subcapsular flow, cyst, splenic malformation; (4) decide — high probability → cholangiogram; intermediate → expert biopsy; clearly low MMP-7 **after the first month** with evidence of bile flow → other workup (pale stools override a low MMP-7); (5) cholangiogram + Kasai in one session, consented for both; genetics in parallel. **Vitamin K and INR before any invasive step.**
- **Mimics of a non-filling cholangiogram (slide 15):** send A1AT phenotype, sweat test or CFTR genetics, and look for Alagille features (echo, spine, eyes — consistent with 02-14) **in parallel, at presentation; a pending result never delays the cholangiogram** (slide 16: steps run in parallel) — a hypoplastic but patent tree in Alagille, CF or severe intrahepatic cholestasis can fail to fill and mimic atresia. Laparoscopic cholangiogram when probability is moderate (avoids laparotomy).
- **GGT (slides 4–5):** high in most atresia but separates poorly — AUC 0.72 (Yang 2018, cut-off 314 U/L, 64%/72%), 0.81 (Pandurangi 2024 ChiLDReN, 267.5 U/L, 86.6%/77.4%); pooled 80%/79% (He 2021). Also high in Alagille, A1AT, PFIC3, neonatal sclerosing cholangitis (often > 800). **Low GGT points away from atresia** (→ chol_low_ggt); atresia with low GGT exists but is uncommon. Age-specific cut-offs (Shen 2020, Beijing, n = 282, single center): ≤ 60 d 191 U/L (89%/92%), 61–90 d 213 (85%/81%), 91–120 d 282 (100%/93%, only 7 BA). Do **not** publish a GGT cut-off as a rule — show as "thresholds near 200 U/L in the first 2 months; interpret against age" (single center, lab ranges differ).
- **MMP-7 (slides 6–10) — rule-out test:** median 121 vs 11.5 vs 2.9 ng/mL (BA / other cholestasis / healthy; Yang 2018). ChiLDReN (Pandurangi 2024, n = 399, 201 BA): AUC 0.90 (biopsy 0.84, GGT 0.81, stool color 0.68); at 52.8 ng/mL (single-plex) sens 94.0%, spec 77.8%, PPV 64.5%, NPV 96.8%. Meta-analyses: He 2021 96%/91%; Wang 2025 (17 studies, 2,836 serum samples) 93%/85%, AUC 0.96, LR− 0.08 (30% pre-test → ~3%).
- **The cut-off belongs to the assay (slide 7):** 52.85 ng/mL ELISA (Yang); 52.8 single-plex vs 18.2 TR-FRET in the same infants (Pandurangi); 1.43 ng/mL with another kit (Wu 2019); stored samples 69 ng/mL, sens 68% (Aleiri 2023). Ge 2025: grade 2C, **no cut-off endorsed**. → Card wording: "Use only the cut-off validated for your lab's assay; ask which platform it runs." **Publish no MMP-7 number as a threshold** — numbers only in Why/pearls as study results with the assay named (record in DECISIONS at build).
- **MMP-7 limits (slide 10):** lower in infants < 30 days (Wu 2019) → repeat if suspicion persists; above cut-off in choledochal cyst (7/8) and A1AT deficiency (8/17) and some indeterminate cholestasis (13/98) in ChiLDReN; ~6% of BA below cut-off; stored/hemolyzed samples read low. **A low MMP-7 never overrides pale stools.** Send-out test (days) → in parallel, never wait for it to book the US. Post-Kasai MMP-7 as prognostic marker — not validated.
- **Ultrasound (slides 11–12):** first line, fasting 3–4 h (else every gallbladder looks small); triangular cord 74%/97%, gallbladder abnormality 85%/92%, both 95%/89% (Zhou 2016, 23 studies); hepatic subcapsular flow 95%/92% pooled (9-study meta-analysis, Dis Markers 2020:5262565; Lee 2009 alone: present in 29/29 BA, spec 86%). Gallbladder "ghost" triad (Tan Kendrick 2003). Normal US does not exclude atresia (Fawaz 1A). **Do not repeat a normal US a week later — escalate.** Porta cyst: non-dilated intrahepatic ducts → cystic atresia; dilated → choledochal cyst (→ duct_dilation). BASM signs (polysplenia, situs, preduodenal portal vein, absent IVC) → tell the surgeon.
- **Other imaging (slide 13):** HIDA — non-drainage sens 98.7%, spec 70.4% (Kianifar 2013, via Fawaz 2017); excretion argues against atresia; never stand-alone; **phenobarbital priming (5 days) delays the Kasai with no proven gain — avoid**. MRCP 99%/36% — limited in infants. ERCP 86–100%/87–94% — expert centers only. Percutaneous cholecystocholangiography 100%/87% where expertise exists (Ge 2025). SWE adjunct (Wagner 2022: 83%/79%, AUC 0.91). Rule: high-sensitivity/low-specificity tests are useful only when negative.
- **Biopsy (slide 14):** obstructive pattern (ductular proliferation, bile plugs, portal edema/fibrosis, preserved lobule) vs hepatitic (lobular disarray, giant cells, portal inflammation); giant cells in 20–50% of atresia; A1AT, Alagille, PFIC3, CF, PN mimic. Correct in 90–95% with experienced pediatric pathologists (Fawaz, 1B "most supportive test"); ChiLDReN AUC 0.84. Complications ~1.7%, higher in small infants (TODO(verify) source — lesson gives none) → vitamin K + INR first. **Early (≈ 3 wk) biopsy can look hepatitic** → stools still pale → repeat or go to cholangiogram.
- **Pitfalls for the pearls block (slide 18):** "Normal US, so not atresia"; "MMP-7 high, book the Kasai" (cyst, A1AT); "Biopsy at 3 weeks was hepatitis"; "Let's prime and scan"; published MMP-7 cut-off on a different kit.
- **Guideline compare rows (slide 17), Fawaz 2017 vs Ge 2025 / current evidence:** bilirubin trigger — row owned by card 1 (neo_jaundice; lesson rates ALIGNED, but Ge's "> 20% of total" conflicts with the "never the ratio" line — one verdict decided at card 1 build, recorded in DECISIONS); GGT — DIFFERS (Ge: consider > 100, high suspicion > 300 U/L — sens 81.5%, spec 72.1%, not a validated rule); **MMP-7 — MATERIAL** (Fawaz silent; Ge 2C; ChiLDReN AUC 0.90); US — ALIGNED; scintigraphy — ALIGNED; elastography — DIFFERS (only Ge, 2B); biopsy — DIFFERS (Fawaz 1B most supportive / Ge 2B might help); cholangiogram — ALIGNED (1A both). No AASLD/EASL atresia diagnosis guideline.
- **Cases (slide 20):** (1) 6 wk, pale stools, GGT 410, MMP-7 150 ng/mL on a validated assay, absent gallbladder → vitamin K, cholangiogram + Kasai this week, no biopsy; (2) 5 wk, GGT 90, normal contracting gallbladder, MMP-7 below cut-off → atresia unlikely; PFIC, bile acid synthesis, genetics (→ chol_low_ggt); (3) 3 wk, direct bilirubin 2.1, borderline MMP-7, hepatitic biopsy → do not close; repeat MMP-7 or biopsy in 1–2 wk, watch stools; (4) 8 wk, high MMP-7, porta cyst with dilated intrahepatic ducts → choledochal cyst more likely: MRCP or cholangiogram, not a Kasai by default; (5) high MMP-7, PiZZ, pigmented stools → A1AT deficiency, avoid laparotomy; (6) polysplenia + situs inversus, rising direct bilirubin → BASM likely; cholangiogram; warn about vascular anatomy (overlaps 03-01 case 2 — keep one).
- Sources to register at card build (verify citations and newer versions): Kianifar 2013 (Pediatr Radiol 2013, via Fawaz 2017 — TODO(verify)); subcapsular flow meta-analysis (Dis Markers 2020:5262565 — TODO(verify) first author); Ge 2025 (Hepatobiliary Surg Nutr 2025 — TODO(verify) volume/pages); Pandurangi 2024 (Hepatology 2024;80:152–62); Yang 2018 (Hepatology 2018;68:2069–77); Wu 2019 (J Pediatr 2019;208:30–7); Aleiri 2023 (JPGN 2023;77:97–102); He 2021 (Sci Rep 2021;11:11692); Wang 2025 (Front Pharmacol 2025;16:1581053); Zhou 2016 (AJR 2016;206:W73–82); Lee 2009 (Radiology 2009;252:282–9); Shen 2020 (BMC Pediatr 2020;20:276); Wagner 2022 (World J Gastroenterol 2022;28:4726–40); Russo 2016 (Am J Surg Pathol 2016;40:1601–15); Tan Kendrick 2003 (Pediatr Radiol 2003;33:311–5).

### 03-04 · Biliary atresia: timing of the Kasai (synced 2026-10-26)
- Wording note: the 02-11 line "Kasai before about 60 days" must be rewritten to the gradient framing at build (no deadline reading).
- **Organizing idea (slides 2–3):** native-liver survival falls **continuously** with age at the Kasai — no safe age to delay, no age at which it becomes futile. 30/45/60 days are audit conventions, not biological switches (progressive fibro-obliterative cholangiopathy; ductules at the porta are lost week by week). Age at Kasai is set mostly **before** the surgeon: recognition, referral, speed of work-up.
- **Evidence for Why (slides 4–6):** France 1986–2015 (Fanna 2019, JPGN 2019;69:416–24, 1,340 operated): native liver at 25 y 38% (1st month) / 27% / 22% / 19% (after 3rd month); median Kasai 59 d, unchanged in 30 y. Canada (Schreiber 2007, n = 349): 4-y native liver 49% / 36% / 23% (≤ 30 / 31–90 / > 90 d — already in 02-14 entry). Serinet 2009 (n = 743): falls with each 15-day band; Kasai for all before 46 d would spare ~5.7% of French pediatric transplants. Meta-analyses: Hoshino 2023 (Eur J Pediatr 2023;182:2693–704) ≤ 30 vs ≥ 31 d RR 1.22 (1.13–1.31), ≤ 30 vs 31–60 RR 1.13, later Kasai HR 2.12 for transplant; Yang 2022 (Front Surg 2022;9:924506, 14 cohorts, 3,276) ≤ 60 vs 61–90 OR 1.41 for native-liver survival, clearance not different between ≤ 60 and 61–90 d. All observational — the **size**, not the existence, of the effect is uncertain.
- **Age is a proxy (slide 7):** infants with other major anomalies, mainly the syndromic (BASM) form, clear less (43% vs 57%, Davenport 2011); cystic atresia presents early and does well; types 1–2 drain better; center volume shifts the curve. Use age to set **urgency**, not to predict the individual outcome.
- **The older infant (slides 8, 18, 20):** offer a Kasai beyond 90 days when the liver is not decompensated (no ascites, INR corrects with vitamin K, no established cirrhosis); **transplant assessment first** with ascites, coagulopathy not corrected by vitamin K, established cirrhosis. "Judge the liver, not the calendar"; discuss with a transplant center. Source: expert practice (Zhan 2018 review, World J Pediatr Surg 2018;1:e000002) + cohorts — no guideline (GAP). → Algorithm out for > 90 d / decompensated; overlaps chol_3_12m.
- **Workup clock (slide 16) — for algorithm/workup:** direct bilirubin ≥ 1 mg/dL → **same-week** hepatology, vitamin K, parallel work-up (US, GGT, MMP-7, A1AT level + phenotype; biopsy if unclear), cholangiogram + Kasai under one anesthetic in an experienced center. Never "recheck in two weeks". Consistent with 03-03 skeleton. **Conflict to resolve at build (DECISIONS):** the lesson's "same-week" contact (slides 16, 20; chapter: "next Wednesday is too late… this week") vs earlier entries — pale stool is a same-day referral (neo_jaundice 02-14), cystic atresia same-day review (03-02). Proposed rule: pale/acholic stools or a rising direct bilirubin → same-day contact; isolated direct ≥ 1 mg/dL with pigmented stools → contact within days, this week at the latest, never "next week". NB threshold wording: lesson/AAP "≥ 1 mg/dL" vs Fawaz "> 1.0 mg/dL" — card 1 owns the row; use one wording across cards.
- **Centre volume (slides 13–15):** UK/Ireland 1993–95 (McKiernan 2000, Lancet 2000;355:25–9): 5-y native liver 61.3% in centers > 5 cases/yr vs 13.7% in smaller; after adjusting for center, age no longer predicted survival in that fragmented system. England and Wales after centralization in 1999 (3 centers): 1999–2009, 443 infants, native liver 46% at 5 y / 40% at 10 y, median Kasai 54 d, patient survival 90% at 5 y (Davenport 2011). Card wording (no local hospitals, §6): "Kasai in an experienced, high-volume center; transfer is worth a delay of days, not weeks" (slide 20, expert opinion).
- **Real-world clock (slide 9):** US 46 children's hospitals 2016–2019 (PHIS, J Pediatr Surg 2021, n = 470): median 57 d (IQR 42–72); hospital medians 38–76 d; 45.1% at ≥ 60 d. TODO(verify) first author of the PHIS paper before citing.
- **Pitfalls (slide 18):** "breastfed — recheck in two weeks"; "stools look yellow" (parents misread, pale can be intermittent — look yourself); "total bilirubin is falling" (physiological fall hides a rising direct fraction; a prior high direct value counts); "too old for a Kasai"; "60 days has passed, no rush" — the next week matters as much as the last.
- **Guideline compare rows (slide 17), Fawaz 2017 · AAP 2025 vs Europe/UK NSC/Asia:** Kasai target age — lesson ALIGNED (AAP < 30 d; European centers "first 4 weeks", achieved median ~61 d) vs the 02-14 MATERIAL rating and its "Europe: no number" — resolve at build, one verdict in DECISIONS; TODO(verify) against the Fawaz 2017 full text whether it really gives no target age (02-14 says so; the guideline discusses better outcomes with earlier surgery); England median: 54 d (Davenport 2011) replaces the 02-14 "UK median ~50 d"; universal screening — card 1 owns this row (neo_jaundice; 03-02 recorded MATERIAL, this lesson DIFFERS) (AAP: future goal; cards in Taiwan, Japan, Switzerland; UK NSC 2026 consultation: not recommended — TODO(verify) UK NSC 2026 status); centralization — MATERIAL (no North American requirement vs England/Nordic/Netherlands); older infant — GAP. Referral threshold / when to test rows — card 1 owns.
- **Cases (slide 20):** (1) 5 wk, direct 1.8 mg/dL, pale stools, US booked for next week → same-week hepatology admission, vitamin K, parallel work-up, cholangiogram within days; (2) 10 wk, atresia likely, no ascites, INR normal after vitamin K → Kasai now in an experienced center, counsel that transplant may be needed later (chapter wording; slide says "likely"); (3) 16 wk, ascites, INR 2.0 despite vitamin K, cirrhotic liver → transplant assessment first; (4) local surgeon does one Kasai a year → transfer to a high-volume center if it adds days, not weeks.

### 03-05 · Biliary atresia: the Kasai operation (synced 2026-10-27)
- **Organizing idea (slides 2–3):** the Kasai portoenterostomy is a **drainage** operation, not a reconstruction — it opens microscopic ductules in the fibrous cone at the porta into a Roux limb; it cannot create ducts. Outcome = substrate (age, ductule size, anatomy) × a flush transection without added harm × the experience of the whole team. Belongs in Why (Learn layer); the Duty layer needs only "Kasai in an experienced, high-volume center".
- **Report card (slides 3, 17; Superina 2011, Ann Surg 2011;254:577–85, ChiLDReN, 136 infants):** clearance = **total** bilirubin < 2 mg/dL within 3 months → HR 0.08 for transplant or death; achieved in 45.6%. Other HRs: Ohi subtype b–d 4.31; Ohi type II/III 2.03; splenic malformation 1.92; ascites > 20 mL 1.90; Kasai ≥ 75 d 1.73; nodular liver also adverse (chapter; no HR on the slide). Only age and the team are modifiable. **Follow-up line for the algorithm/workup (after the Kasai):** bilirubin at 1, 2 and 3 months; not cleared by 3 months (e.g., 4 mg/dL) → transplant assessment now (slides 17, 21).
- **Anatomy (slide 5):** Japanese types — I ~5% (common duct, often a cyst, best clearance), II ~2%, III > 90% (porta). Cystic atresia: earlier surgery, better outcome, not a choledochal cyst. Splenic malformation (polysplenia, situs, preduodenal portal vein, absent IVC) — record at the first operation; shapes a later transplant. "The operative note is a prognostic document."
- **Technique, for Why/pearls only (slides 4, 6–8; surgical convention, no guideline — GAP):** flush transection with the capsule, no diathermy on the plate; flush vs extended at Juntendo (Abe 2022, Pediatr Surg Int 2022;38:701–6, n = 55, non-randomized, sequential eras): jaundice-free native liver 70.2% (flush) vs 22.2% (extended), bile lakes 18.9% after flush vs 0% after extended — principle, not proven rule. One retrocolic Roux limb ~40 cm; antireflux valves, stomas and conduits did not reduce cholangitis and complicate transplant. Gallbladder Kasai only if gallbladder, cystic and common duct are patent to the duodenum (Hery 2017, 97 infants, 46% jaundice-free native liver at 5 y; cited secondhand via Chen 2022 — TODO(verify) primary citation before publishing the number).
- **Open vs laparoscopic (slides 9–13):** Hannover (Ure 2011, Ann Surg 2011;253:826–30) stopped early — lower native-liver survival; Hong Kong (Wong 2008) transplant within 1 y 6/9 vs 38.5% open; the only RCT (Son 2023, Hanoi, 122 infants, type III, median age 79.7 d): jaundice-free at 6 mo laparoscopic 52.5% vs open 60.7% (p = 0.23), 10-y survival laparoscopic 44.3% vs open 58.9% (p = 0.09) — not significant, every point estimate favors open (Son 2023, Pediatr Surg Int 2023, doi 10.1007/s00383-023-05391-4). Meta-analysis Okur 2023 (Pediatr Surg Int 2023;39:148; 23 studies, 689 vs 818): less blood loss (−18 mL), feeding ~3 d earlier, +33 min operating time, no difference in clearance, cholangitis or 2-y transplant-free survival; heterogeneity > 80% for most outcomes, mostly retrospective Asian series. Nagoya (Shirota 2020) and robotic (Guo 2025, 74 infants) — no significant difference; consistent benefit of laparoscopy = fewer adhesions at later transplant. **Card wording:** "Open Kasai is the standard; laparoscopy only in high-volume centers that audit native-liver survival" (Expert opinion).
- **Center volume (slides 14–16) — extends the 03-04 entry:** Nordic 2005–2016 (Pakarinen 2018, J Pediatr Surg 2018;53:1509–15; 154 infants, 148 operated, median 64 d): clearance 64%, native liver 53% at 5 y, overall survival 88%; Kasai < 65 d and caseload > 3/yr independent predictors of native-liver survival. Japan registry (lesson: "Nio 2025"; likely Okubo R, Nio M, World J Pediatr Surg 2025;8:e001024 — the same paper as "Okubo 2025" in the 03-01 entries; confirm and register once at build; 3,951 children): native liver 50.5 / 44.4 / 40.9% at 10 / 20 / 30 y without formal centralization. US Western consortium 2009–2017 (223 infants): cholangitis in the first year ~57%. Thresholds: Nordic > 3/yr, Indian consensus ≥ 6/yr (TODO(verify) source — lesson gives none; omit the number at build if unsourced), England 3 national centers; North America — none mandated. "Volume is a team, not a surgeon": parallel work-up, consistent technique, cholangitis protocol, nutrition (MCT, fat-soluble vitamins), transplant on site.
- **Older/decompensated infant (slides 19, 21) — consistent with 03-04:** ascites + coagulopathy not corrected by vitamin K at ~5 months → transplant assessment first; a Kasai only delays the graft.
- **Pitfalls (slide 19):** "Keyhole is gentler" (not in a low-volume unit); "Redo it" (redo Kasai only after good initial flow that stopped abruptly; otherwise transplant referral); operating on a decompensated infant; Kasai in a non-atretic cholestatic liver (step 1 — confirm by cholangiogram first; a patent distal duct changes the operation).
- **Guideline compare rows (slide 18), AASLD/AST/NASPGHAN 2014 (Squires, Hepatology 2014;60:362–98) · Fawaz 2017 · AAP 2025 vs national policy:** Kasai as first operation — ALIGNED; who should operate — MATERIAL (no society sets a caseload; UK 3 centers, Nordic > 3/yr) — merge with the 03-04 centralization row (one row, one verdict); open vs laparoscopic — DIFFERS (no society addresses; Europe open, some Asian centers laparoscopic); plate dissection and limb — GAP; primary transplant — ALIGNED (advanced cirrhosis/decompensation; Kasai offered even after 90 d — consistent with the 03-04 "older infant" row; merge, keep GAP vs ALIGNED decision in DECISIONS at build). - **Cases (slide 21):** (1) 6 wk, type III, local surgeon does 1 Kasai/yr → transfer within days (overlaps 03-04 case 4 — keep one); (2) parents ask for keyhole → open unless the center audits its laparoscopic results; (3) patent distal duct on cholangiogram → consider gallbladder Kasai; (4) splenic malformation + preduodenal portal vein → Kasai, record anatomy, early transplant planning; (5) bilirubin 4 mg/dL at 3 months → transplant assessment now; (6) cleared, then fever + jaundice → treat cholangitis first (→ kasai_fever); (7) 5 mo, ascites, uncorrected INR → transplant assessment first (overlaps 03-04 case 3 — keep one).
