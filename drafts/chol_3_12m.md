### 02-11 · Syndromic cholestasis (synced 2026-10-19)
- The exam (face, heart, vertebrae, eyes per §4.2) extends to joints, skin, kidneys, glucose: they point to a karyotype, sterol profile or genome rather than another liver panel.
- Other syndromic causes: Alagille (JAG1, NOTCH2 — commonest syndromic cholestasis, block 4); tricho-hepato-enteric (TTC37, SKIV2L: intractable diarrhea, woolly hair, immunodeficiency); NISCH (CLDN1: ichthyosis, scalp hypotrichosis, sclerosing cholangitis); Zellweger spectrum (VLCFA).
- Attenuated ARC survives beyond infancy (19 survivors aged 3–59 y, 18 with VPS33B variants — Zhu & Chen 2022; milder disease with missense variants, one causing isolated low-GGT cholestasis — Qiu 2019). Rule stays absolute: suspect ARC → no percutaneous biopsy; if a procedure is unavoidable, plan it with hematology.
- Negative cholestasis panel in a dysmorphic infant → trio genome with copy-number analysis; reanalyze every 2–3 years (EASL 2024: at least every 3 years).

### 02-12 · Genetics in the infant (synced 2026-10-20)
- Leading genes vary by origin (slide 5): US panels — JAG1, SERPINA1, ABCC2, ABCB11, CFTR (Hoskins 2026); China — SLC25A13 (citrin) commonest; Japan/Korea — neonatal Dubin–Johnson (ABCC2) and citrin; Türkiye — recessive low-GGT genes (consanguinity). Pick a panel that matches the local spectrum **and reports copy-number changes**; otherwise trio exome/genome.
- In Shanghai all 17 genetic disorders could present at 0–3 months; only 5 also presented after 4 months.
- Post-Kasai infant with poor bile flow and JAG1 on the panel → re-label as Alagille; IBAT inhibitor eligibility; family testing (slide 19).
- Normal report is not "not genetic" when the phenotype moves on: reanalyze (EASL 2024: at least every 3 years); VUS → phase with parents, re-analyze (3117 VUS vs 662 P/LP in 2171 tests, Karpen 2021).

### 02-13 · Supportive care in the cholestatic infant (synced 2026-10-21)
- Same supportive program at any age (Mouzaki 2019): energy 130%, protein 130–150%, fat 30–50% with MCT:LCT 30:70 (TODO(verify) MCT share in Mouzaki 2019); length + MUAC + triceps skinfold every 2–4 weeks — weight is inflated by organomegaly and ascites.
- Vitamins every 3–6 months (monthly if severely cholestatic): retinol:RBP, 25-OH D, vitamin E:total lipid ratio, INR ± PIVKA-II; red-cell fatty acid profile every 3–6 months; urea/creatinine every 3 months (→ c_vitamins). A normal INR does not prove adequate vitamin K for bone.
- **UDCA in ABCB4 (MDR3, high-GGT PFIC):** the one setting with a real target — ~87% of patients with ≥ 1 missense variant responded, none with severe genotypes (Gonzales 2023, n = 38); EASL 2024 recommends it (strong). Guideline compare candidate (slide 18): UDCA in ABCB4 — North America nothing specific vs EASL recommended with ≥ 1 missense variant (strong) — DIFFERS. Outside ABCB4: not first line (EASL), no controlled data in Alagille. EASL: insufficient data for or against UDCA in AATD.
- **Itch ladder (slide 14, EASL 2024 ratings):** skin care + night-time sedating antihistamine for sleep only (marginal) → rifampin ~5 mg/kg/day, range 4–10, check liver tests (moderate; complete relief 15%, partial 36%, Gregorio 1993) → sertraline 1–4 mg/kg/day (14/20 improved, Thébaut 2017) → IBAT inhibitor (Alagille, selected PFIC) → diversion (responsive BSEP genotypes) or transplant. Cholestyramine binds vitamins and UDCA — separate by hours; marginal. TODO(verify) rifampin/sertraline doses, maximum per day and frequency against EASL 2024 before publishing.
- **Alagille itch:** EASL recommends offering an IBAT inhibitor (strong). ICONIC (maralixibat) and ASSERT (odevixibat) positive for itch and bile acids. Class effect: diarrhea → watch fat-soluble vitamins. Labels (lesson, 2026): FDA maralixibat Alagille ≥ 3 mo, odevixibat Alagille ≥ 12 mo; EMA odevixibat ≥ 6 mo — TODO(verify) current labels at card build.
- Measure itch at each visit (caregiver scratch score or ItchRO(Obs)); sleep loss is the outcome families feel.
- HPS: oximetry upright and supine routinely in chronic cholestasis; < 97% → investigate (changes transplant timing).
- Case candidates (slide 21): Alagille, 14 months, scratching all night despite rifampin → IBAT inhibitor, watch diarrhea and vitamins; biliary atresia, 9 months, SpO2 95% supine → investigate for HPS.

### 02-14 · Block 2 synthesis (synced 2026-10-22)
- NICCD (pearl 12): high GGT, very high AFP and citrulline; lactose-free MCT formula; almost all recover by 12 months. Biallelic SLC25A13 → no surgery **only if it explains the phenotype**; persistent acholic stools → cholangiogram anyway (02-12).
- Alagille + Kasai: transplant 47% vs 14%, death 32% vs 3% (Kaye 2010) — late-presenting infant with suspected atresia: echo, spine film, eye exam before the cholangiogram.
- Weight z-score −2.1 vs −1.2 at 6 months after Kasai separated poor from good outcome (DeRusso 2007) — measure the arm, not only the weight.

### 03-01 · Biliary atresia: epidemiology and phenotypes (synced 2026-10-23)
- **Late presenters (slides 5, 12):** CMV IgM-positive atresia presents older, with a larger spleen and more fibrosis. An infant referred late (> 60–90 d) with pale stools still needs the atresia pathway — never relabel as "CMV hepatitis" without a cholangiogram (extrapolated from slides 12 and 18).
- Vitamin K deficiency bleeding before the Kasai in 8.8% (335/3,809, Okubo 2025) — INR and parenteral vitamin K in every cholestatic infant (consistent with the 02-14 day-1 list).

### 03-04 · Biliary atresia: timing of the Kasai (synced 2026-10-26)
- **Late-presenting atresia (slides 8, 18, 20):** never "too old for a Kasai". Native liver after a Kasai > 3 mo: 19% at 25 y (France, Fanna 2019); > 90 d: 23% at 4 y (Canada, Schreiber 2007). Offer a Kasai if not decompensated (no ascites, INR corrects with vitamin K, no established cirrhosis on imaging/biopsy); transplant assessment first if ascites, uncorrected coagulopathy, cirrhosis. A working Kasai buys growth before transplant. Judge the liver, not the calendar; discuss with a transplant center (lesson gives no timing; "same week" is team expert opinion). No guideline addresses the older infant (GAP; expert practice, Zhan 2018).
- Case (slide 20): 16 wk, ascites, INR 2.0 despite vitamin K, cirrhotic liver → transplant assessment first.

### 03-06 · Biliary atresia: adjuvant therapy after the Kasai (synced 2026-10-28)
- Late-presenting atresia: a Kasai after 90 days — no adjuvant steroids (any signal is confined to early surgery; harm persists — START early SAEs 37% vs 19%, growth penalty; slides 11, 19). IBAT inhibitors after the Kasai — trials only (BOLD 2026 and EMBARK 2023 negative). Contrast with Alagille/PFIC, where ducts drain and IBAT inhibitors treat pruritus — keep the two lines apart on the card.

### 03-08 · Biliary atresia: prognosis after the Kasai (synced 2026-10-30)
- Late Kasai is not futile (slides 10, 19): 19% native liver at 25 y after month 3 (France), 23% at 4 y after > 90 d (Canada) — same numbers as 03-04; "discuss case by case".
- Kasai at 3–12 months of age: total bilirubin < 2 mg/dL by 3 months after the Kasai is the early signal (extrapolated — ChiLDReN/Shneider 2016 was not a late-Kasai cohort); TB > 6 mg/dL beyond 3 months → prompt transplant referral (AASLD 2014 or its 2026 successor — TODO(verify) current guideline and wording, see chol_0_3m Inbox).

### 03-11 · Biliary atresia: nutrition and growth (synced 2026-11-02)
- Same nutrition frame applies to any persistent infant cholestasis (Mouzaki 2019): energy ≥ 130% of age requirement (many centers 130–150%), protein 130–150% — no routine restriction (02-13 nuance: ≤ 2–3 days only for severe encephalopathy); fat 30–50% of energy; MCT about 30% of fat to start, more in tube feeds (50 vs 50–60% — lesson conflict, TODO(verify) as in the 02-13 entry), < 80% of fat (EFA deficiency). Detail and cases live in `chol_0_3m` 03-11 entry.
- Tube trigger (Mouzaki 2019, lesson slide 11): energy goals unmet > 2 weeks, or 2–4 weeks of falling weight, arm or length → fine-bore NG (NJ if vomiting) with overnight pump feeds; varices are not a contraindication to a soft tube; **no percutaneous gastrostomy with portal hypertension**.
- Pitfall: weight overstates nutrition (organs, ascites) — MUAC and triceps skinfold every 2–4 weeks.

### 03-12 · Biliary atresia: transplantation (synced 2026-11-05)
- **Kasai first even when late, unless decompensated (slide 8):** AASLD/AST/NASPGHAN 2026 (per lesson; "Martinez 2026" TODO(verify)): Kasai as initial management; primary LT evaluation only for late presentation with decompensated cirrhosis — consistent with the 03-04 "judge the liver, not the calendar" line. A prior Kasai does not lower post-LT graft or patient survival (Dong H et al. J Clin Med 2025;14:3328; Lemoine CP et al. J Clin Med 2022;11:3012).
- **Caution on the late-Kasai subgroup:** in Dong 2025 the Kasai > 90 d group (n = 37) had more post-LT graft loss (13.5% vs 4.5%) and mortality (10.8% vs 3.1%, P = 0.06), but the Kasai was not an independent predictor on multivariable analysis — do not use it as an argument against a late Kasai in a compensated infant (Learn layer, if at all).
- **Failed Kasai in this age band (slides 2, 4, 18):** TB > 6 mg/dL at 3 months after the Kasai, or rising → refer to a transplant center this week; TB 2–6 mg/dL at 3 months (jaundice not cleared) → refer to a transplant center, evaluation by 6–9 months of age (slide 4 / chapter Part 2), and at once if any complication appears ("Refer at the first complication, not the third", slide 21). ### 04-01 · Alagille syndrome I: genetics (synced 2026-11-07)
- **Molecular yield:** JAG1 94.3% (CHOP, 401 probands; Gilbert 2019) and 94.7% (GALA, 952 patients; Vandriel 2025); NOTCH2 2.5–3.6%; no variant 1.7–3.2%. Genetic testing makes the diagnosis in ~97% of probands.
- **Order a cholestasis panel that includes JAG1 and NOTCH2 and reports copy number (CNV):** whole-gene JAG1 deletions are ~13% of JAG1 variants (Gilbert 2019, CHOP) — not the same denominator as the ~6.4% of all probands in GALA (02-12 entry); reconcile at card build; sequencing alone detects ~88% (GeneReviews 2024). Ask the lab whether the panel calls CNVs. "JAG1-negative" after sequencing alone is not negative.
- **Negative panel, strong suspicion (slide 13):** MLPA or microarray → NOTCH2 → exome/genome with reanalysis of non-coding JAG1. Developmental delay or hearing loss with a JAG1 deletion → chromosomal microarray (20p12 contiguous deletion).
- **NOTCH2 hides the classic face (GALA 2025, 34 NOTCH2 vs 902 JAG1):** facies 58 vs 90%, cardiac 64 vs 92%, posterior embryotoxon 19 vs 53%, butterfly vertebrae 3 vs 45%; neonatal cholestasis, paucity, renal anomaly and liver outcome the same. → A cholestatic infant with a normal face and spine can still have Alagille syndrome.
- **VUS:** segregation in parents, phenotype search (echo, eye exam, spine film), functional data; liver biopsy acceptable as tie-breaker (EASL 2024 per lesson — TODO(verify) wording in Verkade 2024). Pre-2025 NOTCH2 "likely pathogenic" reports → request reinterpretation (31% downgraded to VUS, Vandriel 2025).
- **No genotype–phenotype correlation** (JAG1 or NOTCH2): never forecast a child's liver from a sibling's or parent's course; prognosis = the child's own trajectory (GALA bilirubin data — later block 4 lesson).
- **Paucity may be absent early** (64.9% on first biopsy in GALA JAG1) — do not wait for paucity in a young infant; genetics answers sooner.
- **Inheritance (GeneReviews 2024):** ~60% de novo / 40% inherited (TODO(verify) GeneReviews range); 50% risk to each child of a carrier; parental mosaicism at least 5/61 families (8.2%, Giannakudis 2001, a minimum estimate); parents negative in blood → recurrence still above population risk (germline mosaicism). Test both parents, offer cascade testing, record the exact variant.
- **Guideline compare candidates (slide 16):** first-line NGS — ALIGNED (Fawaz 2017, EASL 2024); biopsy role — DIFFERS; CNV testing, NOTCH2 interpretation, parental mosaicism — GAP; related living donor — MATERIAL gap.
- **Case (slides 2, 19):** 9 weeks, conjugated bilirubin 5.8 mg/dL, GGT 610 U/L, intermittently pale stools, murmur in both axillae, heterozygous JAG1 frameshift; father "childhood murmur, broad forehead" → no biopsy needed for diagnosis; no cholangiogram or Kasai on the assumption of atresia; echo, eye exam, spine film, kidney ultrasound; test father for the familial variant; carrier → 50% recurrence and not a donor.

### 04-02 · Alagille syndrome II: clinical criteria (synced 2026-11-08)
- **Definitions (slide 12):** classic (Alagille 1987) = bile duct paucity on biopsy + ≥ 3 of 5 (cholestasis, heart, vertebrae, eye, face); expanded clinical = cholestasis may replace biopsy, 3 of 7 (adds kidney, vascular); family history (GeneReviews) = any 1 major feature in a relative of a known proband; molecular (GeneReviews 2024) = suggestive findings + pathogenic/likely pathogenic JAG1 or NOTCH2; genotype-anchored (Turnpenny & Ellard 2012) = variant + family history, or variant + ≥ 1 feature incl. renal or cardiovascular. **Rule: once the variant is known, one feature suffices.** A VUS neither makes nor excludes the diagnosis.
- **Feature frequency in probands (Emerick 1999, 92 CHOP patients; denominators differ):** murmur 97%, cholestasis 96%, facies 96%, growth retardation 87%, paucity 85%, posterior embryotoxon 78%, butterfly vertebrae 51%, renal 40%, intracranial bleed/stroke 14%. Referral-centre probands: a ceiling, not the average carrier. Outcome: 21% transplant, 17% died, 20-year predicted survival 75%; only intracardiac disease predicted death (20-year survival 40% vs 80%).
- **Heart (McElhinney 2002, 200 JAG1/AGS):** any cardiovascular involvement 94%, branch PA stenosis/hypoplasia 76%, tetralogy 12%; right-sided lesions dominate. Peripheral pulmonary stenosis should always prompt the diagnosis. Every suspected case → echocardiogram; RV pressure known before liver transplant.
- **Face (Kamath 2002, blinded):** sensitivity 76%, specificity 82%; least reliable in infants, adults, NOTCH2 and across ethnic backgrounds → supporting feature only; photograph it.
- **Eye and spine are soft:** posterior embryotoxon in 8–15% of the general population (≈ 70% in 22q11.2 deletion); butterfly vertebrae also in 22q11.2 and other segmentation syndromes. Optic disc drusen on ocular ultrasound ≈ 90% (TODO(verify) source — lesson cites GeneReviews/Saleh 2016).
- **Kidney — "the sixth criterion" (Kamath 2012, 187 JAG1-positive with renal data):** 39% renal involvement; of those: dysplasia 59%, RTA 10%, VUR 8%, obstruction 8%. NOTCH2 renal 34.5% vs JAG1 39.2% (GALA 2025, NS). Work-up in every patient: creatinine, electrolytes with bicarbonate, urinalysis, renal ultrasound — before calcineurin inhibitors.
- **Vessels (Kamath 2004: 268 patients, 9% non-cardiac vascular anomaly/event, 34% of deaths [10/29] vascular, mostly intracranial bleeding, some after trivial head trauma; Emerick 2005: MRI/MRA abnormal in 38% of 26, 23% of asymptomatic screened; Carpenter 2018):** non-sedated brain MRI/MRA when feasible; urgent imaging after head trauma or any new neurological sign. A normal screen is not permanent reassurance. **No society guideline grades this** — expert opinion.
- **Seven-system work-up (slide 16):** liver (bilirubin, GGT, bile acids, lipids, INR, vitamins A D E K) · heart (echo) · spine (AP chest/spine film) · eye (slit lamp) · kidney (above) · brain vessels (MRI/MRA) · genes (panel with CNV + parents). Same work-up whether the diagnosis came from criteria or gene.
- **Molecular yield by features (slide 15):** JAG1 in 54% of probands with 3 features (diagnostic lab series); ~1/3 of those referred with 1–2 features (Exeter; Guegan 2012 — TODO(verify) which paper gives which number).
- **GALA prognosis (pearl 10):** native liver survival 54% at 10 y, 40% at 18 y; bilirubin < 5 mg/dL at 6–12 months → 79% reach adulthood with the native liver (Vandriel 2023 — TODO(verify) exact figures and denominators before publishing).
- **Guideline compare (slide 17):** diagnostic definition — Fawaz 2017 no formal criteria vs EASL 2024 genotype-based (DIFFERS); genetic testing — panels / early NGS (ALIGNED); biopsy — part of infant work-up vs EASL not routine for prognosis, diagnostic for a VUS (DIFFERS); before cholangiogram look for features (Fawaz) vs not addressed (EASL); renal evaluation — GAP; neurovascular screening — MATERIAL gap (a third of deaths, no graded recommendation). No dedicated AASLD Alagille guidance.
- **Traps (slide 18) → pearls:** ducts on a biopsy < 6 months do not exclude (paucity 60% vs 95%); no isotope excretion ≠ atresia (61% of AGS infants); 3 of 5 demanded after a pathogenic variant; related donor cleared because "does not meet criteria" (47% of carrier relatives do not); lone embryotoxon in a healthy relative as proof; expecting classic face/eye/spine in NOTCH2 (butterfly 3%, embryotoxon 19%); stopping at five systems; normal screening MRA as permanent reassurance.
- **Cases (slides 2, 20):** (1) 7 weeks, conjugated bilirubin 4.9 mg/dL, GGT 480, no excretion at 24 h, biopsy ductular reaction with no paucity in 11 portal tracts, murmur in both axillae with branch PA stenosis → echo, spine film, slit lamp and panel sent in parallel (a pending result never delays the cholangiogram if atresia is still likely); JAG1 positive → diagnosis made, kidney and brain vessels join the work-up, mother tested for the variant (not judged by her embryotoxon). (2) Clinical 3 of 5, panel negative → copy number, NOTCH2, exome; diagnosis stands clinically. (3) Confirmed AGS, age 6, never imaged → non-sedated MRI/MRA, renal ultrasound and bicarbonate.

### 04-03 · Alagille syndrome III: the liver (synced 2026-11-09)
- **Paucity arrives late (Emerick 1999; GALA):** 60% of biopsies < 6 mo (29/48) vs 95% > 6 mo (38/40); GALA 70% of cholestatic patients (421/604); first biopsy 64.9% JAG1, 56.5% NOTCH2 (Vandriel 2025 — TODO(verify)). Mechanism: failed postnatal branching — hilum preserved, periphery lost (Libbrecht 2005). Paucity = ducts/portal tracts < 0.9 on ≥ 10 tracts; CK7/CK19; not specific (~11% of pediatric biopsies, one centre).
- **EASL 2024 (verified, full text, rec. table):** routine biopsy for prognosis or management in ALGS **not recommended** (LoE 3, strong, 100%); **statins not recommended** for hyperlipidaemia or xanthomas (LoE 4, strong; one case report); **IBAT inhibitor should be offered** for cholestatic pruritus when available (LoE 2, strong). Known variant → never repeat biopsy to prove paucity.
- **Reading the panel:** GGT high (median 612 U/L < 1 y, ChiLDReN; ~85% of cholestatic infants — TODO(verify) which cohort) = duct disease, **not a predictor of outcome**, does not separate from atresia. Bilirubin = strongest early predictor, but use a **median over months**, not one value. ALT erratic; may rise after biliary diversion (182 → 260, Wang 2017). Correct vitamin K before reading INR.
- **Cholesterol = largely lipoprotein X** when bilirubin > 100 µmol/L (5.8 mg/dL) (Davit-Spraul 1996, 22 pts); LCAT low (Gottrand 1995). Janowski 2020 (17 ALGS vs 19 BA): worse lipids but lower cIMT and PWV. Treat the cholestasis, not the number; do not promise lifelong harmlessness (LDL, apoB also up; small, short studies).
- **Xanthomas:** 30% (40/132) with neonatal onset, 0/31 later onset (Lykavieris 2001); 43% at trial baseline; on maralixibat 71% responders at wk 48, 64% at wk 96 (Hoskins 2026, post hoc, n=14 — uncontrolled; TODO(verify)). Surgical diversion (mixed PEBD, ileal exclusion, cholecystocolonic): cholesterol 695 → 457 mg/dL (Wang 2017, ALGS subgroup n=20; confirmed by lesson 04-09 slide 7).
- **Prognosis by bilirubin — GALA (Vandriel 2023, n=1,433, 67 centres):** median total bilirubin at **6–12 months** vs < 5 mg/dL: 5 to < 10 → HR 4.1 portal hypertension, 4.8 transplant; ≥ 10 → HR 8.0 portal hypertension; > 10 → HR 15.6 transplant (abstract wording differs by band). Bilirubin < 5 → 79% reach adulthood with native liver. Cohort predictor, not a transplant date; born 1997–2019, pre-IBAT. Other windows: Kamath 2010 (mean birth–5 y: bili > 6.5, conj > 4.5, chol > 520 → severe; n=33, derivation only); Mouzaki 2016 (12–24 mo: bili 3.8 + fibrosis + xanthomas, AUC 0.79, n=144). **Don't borrow a threshold without its window.** Practice (lesson, expert synthesis): median bilirubin over 6–12 mo, re-check 12–24 mo; middle/upper band → early transplant-centre review before decompensation. No society sets a bilirubin threshold (MATERIAL gap).
- **Native liver survival:** GALA 54.4% at 10 y, 40.3% at 18 y; transplanted 27% by 5 y, 38% by 10 y, 50% by 18 y; PHT/LT/death 51.5% by 10 y, 66% by 18 y. Paris 2001: 51% at 10 y, 38% at 20 y; neonatal onset worse (44/132 transplanted vs 0/31). Old "20–30% need transplant" is outdated.
- **Cirrhosis is slower, not rare:** fewer ductules and less fibrosis than BA (Fabris 2007), but cirrhosis in 35/76 neonatal-onset assessed (Lykavieris 2001).
- **Guideline compare (slide 17):** biopsy after diagnosis — DIFFERS; statins — GAP (only EASL); IBAT for pruritus — DIFFERS (Fawaz 2017 predates); bilirubin risk thresholds — MATERIAL gap; non-invasive fibrosis follow-up — GAP; HCC surveillance — MATERIAL gap.
- **Case (slides 2, 20):** 9 mo, JAG1 frameshift, bilirubin 7.4, GGT 820, cholesterol 610, early xanthomas, biopsy at 8 wk with ducts present → accept diagnosis, no repeat biopsy; middle GALA band → transplant-centre review now; median bilirubin through 12 mo and at 12–24 mo; IBAT for itch; nutrition and vitamins; no statin; GGT not prognostic; "four in ten keep their liver; better if bilirubin falls below 5".

### 04-04 · Alagille syndrome IV: the heart (synced 2026-11-10)
- **Cardiac spectrum (McElhinney 2002, Circulation, 200 JAG1/ALGS):** any cardiovascular involvement 94%; branch pulmonary artery (PA) stenosis or hypoplasia 76%; tetralogy of Fallot (TOF) 12% (23/200), a third of it with pulmonary atresia (8/23); left-sided lesions 11% (22/200). Variant type/position does not predict the heart. GeneReviews 2024: TOF 7–16%. TODO(verify) numbers against McElhinney 2002 at build.
- **Genotype (GALA, Vandriel 2025, Liver Int):** echo-confirmed cardiac anomaly JAG1 92.2% (789/856) vs NOTCH2 64.3% (18/28) → **NOTCH2 lowers the prior but every child still gets echo + cardiology review** (pitfall: skipping echo in NOTCH2). Emerick 1999: murmur in 97% (as common as cholestasis 96%, facies 96%). TODO(verify) GALA denominators.
- **Workup line for the card:** ECG + echo with Doppler at diagnosis in every child with suspected ALGS; follow-up with a cardiologist who knows ALGS. A murmur in ALGS = branch PA stenosis until imaged; **a normal echo does not see lobar/segmental branches** (Tretter 2018: undiagnosed right-sided disease in up to 22%).
- **Prognosis line:** intracardiac congenital heart disease was the only clinical feature tied to higher mortality (Emerick 1999, n=92; predicted 20-y survival 75% overall, 80% without LT, 60% with LT); later reviews (Kamath 2010, Ruth 2019) summarize the age pattern — cardiac deaths lead in infancy, liver deaths later. Counsel isolated branch PA stenosis and TOF with pulmonary atresia + collaterals very differently (Bauser-Heaton 2017: ALGS independently predicted worse survival among 458 with TOF/MAPCAs).
- **Don't miss / reverse direction:** JAG1 variants in ~2% (2/94) of isolated TOF and ~4% (2/50) of pulmonary valve or peripheral pulmonary stenosis without an ALGS diagnosis (Bauer 2010, Hum Mutat) → a cardiology patient with mildly high GGT may carry JAG1.
- **Guideline compare (slide 17):** cardiac imaging at LT evaluation — DIFFERS (Squires 2014: echo all, 2-B; EASL 2024 no work-up advice); extrahepatic assessment — DIFFERS (Squires 1-B; EASL: refer to a transplant centre if liver involvement is substantial); stress testing — GAP; repair before LT — GAP; living related donor — DIFFERS (Squires 2-B: may be considered, exclude duct hypoplasia, avoid if same allele; others silent); cardiac exclusion threshold — MATERIAL gap (no society defines it). **Note:** the lesson cites Squires 2014, which Martinez 2026 (AASLD/AST/NASPGHAN, doi 10.1097/HEP.0000000000001805) replaces — TODO(verify) the 2026 wording on echo/CT angiography and living related donors before using the Squires column.
- **Case (slides 2, 20):** 3 y, JAG1, bilirubin 9.8 on IBAT inhibitor, xanthomas, height z −2.6, bilateral branch PA stenosis, tricuspid jet 4.2 m/s (RVSP ≈ 71 mmHg + RA, ~0.8 systemic) → not pulmonary hypertension and not a veto: CT angiography + catheter with dobutamine stress, treat gradients before listing, test the mother for the family variant before any donor work-up.

### 04-05 · Alagille syndrome V: the vessels (synced 2026-11-11)
- **Systemic arteriopathy (Kamath 2004, Circulation, 268 ALGS, Philadelphia 1992–2002):** non-cardiac vascular anomaly or event in 9% (25/268 patients; lesion counts below exceed 25 because some had two): 9 intracranial events without a structural lesion, 7 internal carotid anomalies (4 stenosis, 2 agenesis, 1 dolichoectasia), 3 aortic aneurysms, 2 basilar and 1 MCA aneurysm, 2 coarctation, 1 moyamoya, 1 renal artery stenosis; 10 of 29 deaths (34%) non-cardiac vascular; events from 4 months to 44 years. TODO(verify) against Kamath 2004 at build.
- **Who dies of what (GALA, Vandriel 2023, 108 deaths in 1,433):** liver/transplant 22% (median 2.8 y), cardiac 18% (1.1 y), multiorgan failure 15%, **non-cardiac vascular 15% at median 2.2 y** (IQR 1.5–3.2) — years before most centres first screen. GeneReviews 2024: neurovascular accidents in up to 15%. TODO(verify) GALA figures.
- **Bleeding with normal tests (Lykavieris 2003, Pediatrics, Paris, 174 records reviewed):** 38 (22%) patients without liver failure bled (Hb fall ≥ 3 g/dL or transfusion), 49 episodes, median age 3.75 y; median platelets and PT normal; 21 during surgery/procedures, 17 spontaneous; 8 deaths (4 after surgery, 2 GI, 1 after needle liver biopsy, 1 intracranial). → **Pitfall:** a normal INR and platelet count do not clear a child with ALGS for biopsy or surgery.
- **Red flag for the card:** head injury with vomiting or drowsiness, or any headache/seizure/weakness/speech change/TIA in ALGS → **emergency CT/CTA or MRI/MRA tonight + parenteral vitamin K** (a head bleed is vascular until proven otherwise, even with no lesion on the first scan). Expert practice (Ayoub 2023, GeneReviews 2024 — "pursue aggressively"); no graded guideline. ### 04-06 · Alagille syndrome VI: kidney, skeleton, eyes and face (synced 2026-11-12)

### 04-08 · Alagille syndrome VIII: IBAT inhibitors (synced 2026-11-14)
- **Mechanism:** ~95% of bile acids are reabsorbed in the terminal ileum by IBAT (ASBT, SLC10A2); maralixibat and odevixibat act in the gut lumen, minimally absorbed → more bile acid in stool, serum pool falls (C4 rises, FGF19 falls). **Needs bile in the gut — no effect with complete obstruction or after a failed Kasai.** Drug version of surgical biliary diversion; class effects = diarrhea and fat-soluble vitamin loss.
- **Two US labels for ALGS cholestatic pruritus (TODO(verify) against current Livmarli and Bylvay labels before publishing any dose or age):** maralixibat from 3 months, 190 µg/kg once daily × 1 wk then 380 µg/kg once daily, 30 min before breakfast (solution 9.5 mg/mL; tablets if ≥ 25 kg and able to swallow; reviewer: current Livmarli label 4/2025 caps the ALGS dose at 28.5 mg/day solution, 30 mg/day tablets — not in the lesson; TODO(verify) odevixibat ALGS maximum); odevixibat from 12 months, 120 µg/kg once daily in the morning with a meal (pellets < 19.5 kg, capsules above). Both: contraindicated with prior or active hepatic decompensation (added to both labels in 2025 FDA safety update, with an expanded bleeding warning — check Livmarli 4/2025 and Bylvay 12/2025 labels); give ≥ 4 h apart from cholestyramine. No head-to-head trial; labelled doses are not equivalent → choose by age, formulation, access, tolerance.
- **Evidence:** ITCH (Shneider 2018, Hepatol Commun, n = 37, 13 wk, low doses) — primary endpoint missed (−0.47, p = 0.16). ICONIC (Gonzales 2021, Lancet, n = 31, randomized withdrawal wk 18–22) — placebo switch: itch +1.7 (95% CI 1.2–2.2), bile acids +94 µmol/L; primary drug−placebo −117 µmol/L; itch −1.6 by wk 48. ASSERT (Ovchinsky 2024, Lancet Gastroenterol Hepatol, n = 52, 2:1, 24 wk) — PRUCISION scratching −1.7 vs −0.8 (difference −0.9, p = 0.002); diarrhea 29% vs 6%, none stopped. → Expect ~1 point beyond placebo; not a cure.
- **Durability (Kamath 2024, EASL Congress abstract — do not publish numbers before a full paper):** pooled maralixibat n = 86, itch ~1–2 points lower to year 7; 36/38 responders kept response > 3 y; height z-score −1.6 at baseline, about 0.8 higher by year 7 (≈ −0.8; survivorship bias).
- **Liver outcomes = matched historical comparison, not RCT:** Hansen 2024 (Hepatology, MRX n = 84 vs GALA 469 matched) EFS HR 0.305 (0.189–0.491), transplant-free survival aHR 0.33; manufacturer-funded; bile acids not matchable; 69% of GALA transplants were for itch → fewer itch transplants ≠ proven slower liver disease. **Tell families: reliably eases itch; liver benefit promising, not proven.** No guideline endorses IBAT inhibitors to prolong native liver survival.
- **Week-48 checkpoint (Sokol 2023, Hepatology, n = 76):** 6-y EFS responder vs non-responder — itch fall > 1 point 88% vs 57%; bilirubin < 6.5 mg/dL 90% vs 43%; bile acids < 200 µmol/L 85% vs 49%. 21% had an event over median 4.7 y. **None of the three responses at wk 48 (itch fall > 1 point, bilirubin < 6.5 mg/dL, bile acids < 200 µmol/L) → transplant assessment** (slide 12: "a child with none of the three"); 43–57% = 6-y EFS of non-responders by each criterion taken separately. Starting < 36 mo linked to worse outcome (sicker infants enrolled — confounding).
- **Safety (labels):** pooled maralixibat uncontrolled — diarrhea 56%, abdominal pain 54%, vomiting 41%, fat-soluble vitamin deficiency 26%, ALT/AST rise 19%, fracture 9%; GI dose reduction/interruption 6%. Labelled liver injury: new liver test rise → reduce or interrupt; restart at the last tolerated dose once tests return to baseline; stop if it recurs or the liver decompensates. Maralixibat solution contains propylene glycol (watch < 5 y). Maralixibat inhibits OATP2B1 in vitro → review statins. TODO(verify) each line against the labels.
- **Monitoring plan written on day one:** ALT/AST/bilirubin at baseline, then frequently for the first 6–8 months (label); vitamins A, D, E and INR at baseline and through treatment; stool/hydration each visit (persistent diarrhea, blood, fever, dehydration → interrupt); 2-wk ItchRO(Obs) diary before and during (responder = ≥ 1-point fall); bile acids, cholesterol, xanthomas, height every 3–6 mo (practice). **Alagille labs are always abnormal — compare with the patient's own baseline.**
- **When to start (lesson synthesis, not a graded algorithm):** confirmed ALGS (JAG1/NOTCH2), bile reaches the gut, compensated → 2-wk diary + baseline labs → start when itch matters to the family, early (EASL 2024 "offer when available", LoE 2 strong) → diary at ~3 mo, labs at 6 mo; keep rifampin only if it adds → wk 48 response check. No data for starting without itch to protect the liver, none under 3 months.
- **Apparent failure:** check adherence, morning timing, weight-based volume, resin spacing (≥ 4 h) first. ICONIC extension allowed maralixibat 380 µg/kg twice daily (above label — specialist decision). Switching between agents: 3 adolescents (Ovchinsky 2026, abstract) — proves nothing. **Never let a switch delay a transplant assessment.** Decompensation (variceal bleed, ascites, encephalopathy) → stop the drug + transplant assessment.
- **Guideline compare (slide 17):** use for ALGS itch — ALIGNED (EASL 2024 + labels); age — DIFFERS (3 vs 12 months between labels); preferred agent — ALIGNED (none); **place in the ladder — MATERIAL** (early per EASL); **to prolong native liver — MATERIAL** (itch only; transplant remains standard); monitoring — GAP (labels only). Merge with the 04-07 "place of IBAT — MATERIAL" row: one verdict at build. **This resolves the 04-07 ladder conflict:** IBAT inhibitor early, not after sertraline (record in DECISIONS at build).
- **Pitfalls (slide 19):** IBAT kept as last rung; promising the liver will be saved; cholestyramine in the same hour; no baseline diary; no vitamins/INR → fracture or bleed; continuing after decompensation; missing drug-induced ALT rise; expecting response without bile flow; reading the 0.8 placebo fall as drug effect; switch delaying transplant work-up.

### 04-09 · Alagille syndrome IX: surgical biliary diversion (synced 2026-11-15)
- **Mechanism:** all options break the enterohepatic loop (~95% of bile acid reclaimed in the terminal ileum). PEBD (gallbladder → jejunal conduit → skin stoma) and internal diversion (gallbladder → colon) need a gallbladder with bile flow; ileal exclusion and IBAT inhibitors need bile in the gut. ALGS ductopenia limits how much bile reaches either → weaker, less predictable effect than in BSEP deficiency.
- **Evidence ladder:** Emerick 2002 (Hepatology 35:1501, 9 ALGS, uncontrolled): itch grade 4.0 → 1.1 at 1 y, bile salts 137 → 37 µmol/L (n = 5), cholesterol 724 → 367 mg/dL (n = 7), extensive xanthomas resolved in 3 of 3; reversal at 21 y → itch back to grade 4 in 2 mo. ChiLDReN (Wang 2017, Hepatology 65:1645; ALGS n = 20): cholesterol fell, **ALT rose (182 → 260)**; bilirubin fell only in FIC1 after PEBD; 15 major complications in 58. GALA (Vandriel 2023, **AASLD abstract only — do not publish numbers before the full paper**): 62/1673 diverted (3.7%), median 2.5 y, PEBD 55%; bilirubin 8.8 → 9.1, itch prevalence 100% → 92% (ns), bile acids 257 → 98 (n = 10); bile-acid responders: 8 of 10 kept the native liver; HR 2.5 for transplant or death — **likely confounded by indication and severity, not proof of harm (abstract, no adjustment published)**; post-SBD bilirubin < 4 mg/dL linked to longer native liver survival (sens 94%, spec 52%).
- **Take-home for the card:** no evidence that diversion buys native-liver years in ALGS; **IBAT inhibitor first** (EASL 2024, LoE 2 strong); diversion only after an adequate, adherent IBAT trial (label dose, resin ≥ 4 h apart, judged at ~3 mo and wk 48), never to postpone a needed transplant assessment.
- **Who still gets an operation (lesson synthesis, expert opinion — not a graded algorithm; no guideline defines it):** compensated (no variceal bleed, no ascites), bilirubin ideally < ~4 mg/dL, bile-acid-driven itch, a reason to delay transplant (heart/vessels, no donor, family wish), stoma-ready family. Prefer PEBD over ileal exclusion; internal diversion only with surgical experience; stoma teaching first. Judge bile acids + itch at 1, 3, 6 mo; no bile-acid fall → transplant meeting. Thresholds borrowed from GALA (bilirubin < 4) and BSEP data (bile acids < ~100 µmol/L or ≥ 75% fall) — pragmatic markers, not validated targets. Note: GALA < 4 mg/dL was a post-operative value; using it as a pre-operative filter is the lesson's own borrowing.
- **Ileal exclusion:** effect fades (adaptation); removes the IBAT target → an IBAT inhibitor afterwards unlikely to help (mechanistic reasoning). Prefer PEBD or stay on drug.
- **Complications to plan for:** PEBD — stoma prolapse/retraction, high output → dehydration, low sodium, acidosis (worse with gastroenteritis) → written sick-day plan, stoma nurse before surgery; internal and ileal exclusion — bile acid diarrhea, perianal skin injury; early failure more often after ileal exclusion than PEBD; internal — **ascending cholangitis (fever → cultures + antibiotics)**; all — fat-soluble vitamin and energy loss (levels every 3–6 mo).
- **Existing PEBD + family wants reversal:** start IBAT inhibitor first, then reverse (Nwachukwu 2025, JPGN Rep, two-patient case series; itch returned within days of reversal without drug; both needed 380 µg/kg twice daily, above label — specialist decision, do not publish as a dose).
- **Guideline compare (slide 18):** IBAT for ALGS itch — MATERIAL (EASL 2024 offer; NA documents silent); diversion for ALGS itch — GAP; diversion to save the ALGS liver — GAP; transplant in progressive ALGS — ALIGNED (EASL refer early; AASLD/AST/NASPGHAN 2014 cardiac/vascular work-up before listing). Merge with 04-07/04-08 rows at build — note 04-08 called this row ALIGNED by counting FDA labels; slide 18 compares guidelines only → pick one rule at build (guidelines only, or guidelines + labels) and record it in DECISIONS.
- **Pitfalls (slide 19):** borrowing the BSEP liver benefit for ALGS; operating before an IBAT trial; diverting at bilirubin ~9 expecting recovery; ileal exclusion for convenience; calling PEBD a success because the stoma drains (check bile acids + itch score); no sick-day plan; reading HR 2.5 as harm; reversing a stoma without the drug; diversion to postpone a transplant work-up; forgetting that a used/removed gallbladder changes later surgery.
- **Case (slides 2, 21):** boy 6 y, JAG1, compensated, repaired heart, bilirubin 3.1, bile acids 240, ItchRO(Obs) 2.9 after 9 mo of maralixibat at label dose, xanthomas → confirm dose/adherence, separate resin, check bile acids; still no change → joint meeting: PEBD only with a stoma-ready family and a reason to delay transplant; otherwise transplant evaluation.

### 04-10 · Alagille syndrome X: natural history (synced 2026-11-16)
- **Delta vs 04-03 (same GALA paper, Vandriel 2023, Hepatology 77:512):** denominator for liver outcomes = 1,184 with neonatal cholestasis (85% of 1,387 with data); 98% of 892 genotyped had JAG1/NOTCH2; median follow-up 6.0 y; born 1997–2019 → pre-IBAT. Native liver 66.8% / 54.4% / 40.3% at 5 / 10 / 18 y; transplant 27.1% / 37.8% / 50.4%; death without transplant 9.3% by 18 y. Median age at transplant 2.8 y; 72% before age 5. Without neonatal cholestasis: overall (not native liver) survival 100% / 97% at 10 / 18 y (vs 89% / 86%).

### 04-11 · Alagille syndrome XI: liver transplantation (synced 2026-11-17)

### 04-12 · Alagille syndrome XII: other causes of ductopenia (synced 2026-11-18)
- **Paucity is a pattern, not a diagnosis (slides 2, 5, 7):** a ductopenic biopsy in an infant with no syndrome means "keep sequencing", not "nonsyndromic paucity". Send a cholestasis panel or exome that includes JAG1 and NOTCH2 (EASL 2024: panel/exome, single-gene Sanger discouraged; reanalyze at least every 3 years — already in ref_genetics). Causes by group: syndromic (ALGS); genetic nonsyndromic (A1AT, CF, TJP2, KIF12, ZFYVE19); chromosomal (trisomy 21, 18); congenital infection (CMV, rubella, syphilis); endocrine/metabolic (hypopituitarism, hypothyroidism, peroxisomal — VLCFA); acquired (drugs, LCH); idiopathic as a shrinking remainder.
- **Bruguera 1992 (Hepatology 1992;15:830–4):** all 3 neonatal-onset cases among 11 with "unexplained" paucity had Alagille, recognized through cardiovascular lesions — verified against the abstract (PMID 1568724) 2026-11-18. ALGS has variable expressivity — a JAG1 child can lack face, vertebrae and eye findings.
- **Reading the count (slide 4; Kahn 1989, Hepatology 1989;10:21–3):** normal ratio 0.9–1.8 ducts per portal tract; before 38 weeks post-conceptional age a ratio < 0.9 may be physiological (≥ 0.9 in 8 of 9 after 38 weeks); Kahn advises biopsy for paucity only after 38 weeks — verified against the abstract (PMID 2737599) 2026-11-18. "Paucity < 0.5" and "count ≥ 10 portal tracts" are **not** in Kahn — TODO(verify) against a separate source (Alagille definition papers). **Conflict with the 04-03 entry** (which gave "paucity < 0.9 on ≥ 10 tracts") — the lesson now separates the *normal lower limit* (0.9) from the *paucity threshold* (< 0.5); resolve at build against the primary source and say which metric (ratio; Banff share of tracts without a duct; DILIN share with ducts).
- **Trisomy 21 (slides 8–9):** Stockholm population cohort (Arnell & Fischler, J Pediatr 2012;161:899–902 — **verified against the abstract 2026-11-18**): 8 of 206 newborns with Down syndrome cholestatic (3.9%); always with another organ involved (CHD 8/96 vs 0/110; GI 3/23 vs 5/183; marrow disease 3/7 vs 5/199); all 3 severe cases had marrow disease, 2 with liver failure and early death; 5 transient. Cairo referral cohort (Kotb 2019, Clin Exp Gastroenterol 2019;12:401–8): 61 of 779 cholestatic infants (7.8%) had Down syndrome; of 55 followed, nonsyndromic paucity in 18, cleared in 17 of 18; ursodiol group cleared less often (13/29 vs 22/26) — confounded, retrospective. Verified against the full text (PMC6815214) 2026-11-18; overall clearance 35 of 55 (63.6%). → Card: "Trisomy 21 + cholestasis + abnormal blood count → hematology today: transient abnormal myelopoiesis can infiltrate the liver." Do **not** publish "no biliary atresia among 55" as reassurance — a single-center zero does not exclude BA; stool color and the BA pathway still apply.
- **Case (slides 2, 20):** 4-month-old girl, trisomy 21, repaired AVSD, TAM at birth, GGT 410, ratio 0.4 in 12 tracts → adequate sample past the 38-week window, but AVSD ≠ peripheral pulmonary stenosis; panel incl. JAG1/NOTCH2, blood film with hematology, no reflexive ursodiol; ducts usually recover (17 of 18, Kotb 2019) but TAM puts her in the subgroup where Arnell's severe cases clustered → follow GGT, bilirubin and INR, marrow follow-up with hematology, re-evaluate if GGT keeps rising. Candidate `case` for this card.
- **Pitfalls (slide 18):** paucity called on a core with 6 tracts; ratio 0.6 in a 35-week preterm read as disease; ALGS excluded by normal ducts or ductular reaction in an infant (already in ref_biopsy); "nonsyndromic" label without sequencing; missing the marrow in trisomy 21.
- **Guideline compare (slide 17):** genetic testing for unexplained cholestasis — ALIGNED; paucity in trisomy 21 — GAP (no society addresses it); age limit for interpreting paucity — the lesson marks MATERIAL, but no document sets a tract count or post-conceptional age → probably `gap` (decide at build).

### 04-13 · Alagille syndrome XIII: long-term outcomes (synced 2026-11-19)
- **Normal bilirubin is not cure (slides 2–3, 19):** the long-term plan follows the syndrome, not the bilirubin — HCC, portal hypertension (second wave), vessels, kidney, bone, growth and cognition run for life. Never discharge an anicteric teenager as "outgrown" ALGS.
- **Native liver into adulthood — depends on who was counted (slide 4):** GALA 40.3% at 18 (already in the 04-10 entry); ChiLDReN 24% at 18.5 y (cholestatic enrolment; Kamath 2020 — TODO(verify) in the paper); Korean single-centre 76.6% (Choi 2026, n = 43 — TODO(verify), do not publish). Adult transplants: 44 of 451 ALGS liver transplants in UNOS 1987–2008 (~10%) were in adults (Arnon 2012, Clin Transplant 26:E94 — verified against the abstract, PMID 22211770; Ayoub 2023 restates this as "~10% of ALGS individuals require LT in adulthood" and gives 507 children — the abstract says 407; quote Arnon, not the restatement). Adult post-LT survival 95.5% / 90.9% at 1 / 5 y vs 88.7% / 86.2% in children (Arnon abstract).
- **HCC (slides 6–8):** literature review (Schindler 2021, Am J Med Genet A 185:719 — **verified in the full text, PMC7898517**): pediatric cases mostly cirrhotic (16 of 18 with data), adult cases mostly not (4 of 9 with data cirrhotic); two adults with no overt ALGS liver phenotype (the 58-year-old JAG1 index case, AFP 3.1 ng/mL — HCC with a normal AFP; Keeffe 1993); all pediatric cases with a described liver phenotype had paucity and cholestasis before HCC. Ayoub 2023 (verified; different case set — 14 adults vs Schindler's 13): 11% of children and 67% of adults non-cirrhotic; AFP normal in 38% → AFP alone is not a screen. **Surveillance (Ayoub 2023, expert opinion, verified wording):** "ALGS patients of all ages, regardless of the severity of hepatic involvement" — abdominal ultrasound + serum AFP every 6 months; contrast CT/MRI only for a questionable lesion on US. No society guideline (EASL 2024 addresses HCC only for BSEP deficiency) → `guideline_compare` row: lesson marks MATERIAL; by §5.2 a missing guideline is `gap` — decide at build (same issue as the 04-03/04-10 rows).
- **Regenerative nodule vs HCC (slide 8):** benign regenerative nodules in up to 30% (Ayoub 2023 — verified), often > 5 cm, central, near the right portal vein, T2-hypointense, iso-enhancing; stable on serial scans. Rule for the card: "Change over time, not size, is the signal" — a large nodule on the first US → contrast MRI to characterize it (lesson slide 21; Ayoub Table 2), then it becomes the baseline; poor US visibility → contrast CT/MRI (Ayoub Table 2); a new, growing or arterially enhancing lesion → contrast MRI and transplant tumour board before any biopsy or resection. Already in ref_imaging (Rapp 2017 pearl, 04-03).
- **Growth (slides 9–11; Huysentruyt 2025, JAMA Netw Open 8:e2545294 — verified in the full text, PMC12645329):** GALA condition-specific charts (1,204 for weight, 1,106 for height, term-born, native liver); median height at 18: 171.5 cm boys / 156.5 cm girls vs CDC 176 / 163; 52% born small for gestational age → part of the deficit is prenatal. Drift in the ≥ 10-year subcohort (verified in the full text): height z −0.03/yr (≈ −0.3 over 10 y), weight z −0.01/yr, steeper in girls → "falling across ALGS centiles" means crossing centile lines, not this small drift. SGA: 362 of 701 with corrected birth weight (51.6%); charts built from term-born children only — caution when plotting a preterm child.
- **Growth mechanisms (slide 10):** fat malabsorption and vitamin deficiency; cholestasis (ChiLDReN: per 10 mg/dL bilirubin ≈ 0.10 lower height z, 0.15 lower weight z — TODO(verify) Kamath 2020); GH insensitivity with low IGF-1 (Bucuvalas 1993); cardiac energy needs; intrinsic Notch bone/growth-plate effects. Weight z-score the only independent determinant of QoL in ChiLDReN (Kamath 2015 — TODO(verify)). Growth failure despite nutrition + IBAT → transplant-centre referral (already in the 04-10/04-11 entries).
- **Neurocognition (slides 12–13; Leung 2022, JPGN 74:96 — verified against the abstract, PMID 34694263):** ChiLDReN, native liver, median 7.6 y: full-scale IQ < 85 in 29% of ALGS (n = 70) vs 15.9% expected (p = 0.003), PFIC 18.6%, A1AT 12.8%; mean FSIQ ALGS 94 vs A1AT 101; associations — bilirubin, ALP, albumin, hemoglobin, parental education. → Card line (Learn layer): formal neuropsychological testing before school entry and again at transition, educational support when needed (lesson chapter); falling behind at school → testing, and brain MRI/MRA if the trajectory changes (GeneReviews 2024 yearly developmental screen — TODO(verify) wording). Contributors: infant cholestasis and malnutrition, cyanotic/operated CHD, intracranial vascular events, large 20p12 deletions (developmental delay, hearing loss, autism).
- **Bone (slide 14; Bales 2010, JPGN 51:66 — verified against the abstract, PMID 20453673):** 12 of 42 (28%) had fractures, 27 in total, mean age 5 y, 70% in lower-limb long bones, 84% with little or no trauma; femur 127.6 per 10,000 person-years (survey; ascertainment bias). Bone deficits with mostly normal vitamin D (Kindler, per Ayoub 2023 — only 10% low 25-OH-D) → intrinsic Notch bone biology. Card: "A femur fracture after a minor fall is a disease feature → bone and nutrition review (vitamin D, DXA adjusted for height)." Ask about fractures at every visit (lesson); DXA at transplant evaluation (Ayoub 2023 — verified).
- **Adult handover (slides 15–16; Ayoub 2023 — verified):** hand over a list of organs — HCC surveillance; portal hypertension (Baveno for varices); brain MRA if never done (5 adults with SAH aged 21–30, 2 died; mostly vertebrobasilar); kidney — yearly BP, creatinine/electrolytes, urine protein:creatinine ratio (Ayoub Table 2 — verified), renal Doppler if hypertensive (lesson slide 15); adult congenital cardiology. Pregnancy: autosomal dominant 50% per pregnancy, ~40% inherited; counselling, prenatal/preimplantation testing, MRA, echo, variceal assessment, maternal-fetal medicine plan. Transplant in adults: sicker at LT (MELD/PELD 22 vs 17; HE 23% vs 5% — Arnon 2012 via Ayoub 2023, secondary; TODO(verify) in Arnon) but survive as well as children → refer before decompensation.
- **Bile acids and IBAT (slide 17):** same data as the 04-10 entry (Murillo Perez 2025; Hansen 2024 HR 0.305, external control) — slide gives transplant HR 3.78 for bile acids > 102; conflicts with the 04-10 numbers (event HR 2.04; native-liver loss adjusted HR 1.72, NS) → **do not publish 3.78**; probably the unadjusted model; TODO(verify) in the full text.

### 05-01 · PFIC I: physiology of bile secretion (synced 2026-11-21)
- **High-GGT genetic branch (slide 15):** micelle defect — ABCB4 (MDR3): pump works, bile lacks phosphatidylcholine → duct injury, high GGT, biliary fibrosis, cholesterol stones (Smit 1993; de Vree 1998). Ductal — JAG1, NOTCH2, CFTR, DCDC2, KIF12 (face, heart, vertebrae, kidneys, lungs). Panel must include ABCB4.
- **Family history clue:** mother with intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy, gallstones or LPAC in a high-GGT infant → think ABCB4 (heterozygotes present as LPAC, ICP or DILI in adults). Pitfall: ignoring the mother's history.
- **UDCA in ABCB4 disease:** needs intact secretion; missense variants with residual MDR3 often respond, two truncating variants usually do not; EASL 2024 recommends UDCA for missense ABCB4 (lesson: LoE 3, strong — TODO(verify) wording). Pitfall: stopping UDCA in an ABCB4 missense child because "UDCA does not work in PFIC". guideline_compare: GAP (no North American statement).
- Mild/missense or single-copy ABCB4 can show near-normal GGT between episodes → do not exclude ABCB4 on one normal GGT.
- **Case (slides 2, 21):** Infant B — 3 mo girl, direct bili 2.1 mg/dL, GGT 520 U/L, bile acids 140 µmol/L, mother had ICP → panel including ABCB4, start UDCA.
- Unexpected result (ABCB11 variant in a high-GGT child) → second look (phasing, second diagnosis), not a new paradigm (slide 15).
