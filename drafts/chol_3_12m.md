### 02-11 · Syndromic cholestasis (synced 2026-10-19)
- The exam (face, heart, vertebrae, eyes per §4.2) extends to joints, skin, kidneys, glucose: they point to a karyotype, sterol profile or genome rather than another liver panel.
- Other syndromic causes: Alagille (JAG1, NOTCH2 — commonest syndromic cholestasis, block 4); tricho-hepato-enteric (TTC37, SKIV2L: intractable diarrhea, woolly hair, immunodeficiency); NISCH (CLDN1: ichthyosis, scalp hypotrichosis, sclerosing cholangitis); Zellweger spectrum (VLCFA).
- Attenuated ARC survives beyond infancy (19 survivors aged 3–59 y, 18 with VPS33B variants — Zhu & Chen 2022; milder disease with missense variants, one causing isolated low-GGT cholestasis — Qiu 2019). Rule stays absolute: suspect ARC → no percutaneous biopsy; if a procedure is unavoidable, plan it with hematology.
- Negative cholestasis panel in a dysmorphic infant → trio genome with copy-number analysis; reanalyze every 2–3 years (EASL 2024: at least every 3 years).

### 02-12 · Genetics in the infant (synced 2026-10-20)
- Leading genes vary by origin (slide 5): US panels — JAG1, SERPINA1, ABCC2, ABCB11, CFTR (Hoskins 2026); China — SLC25A13 (citrin) commonest; Japan/Korea — neonatal Dubin–Johnson (ABCC2) and citrin; Türkiye — recessive low-GGT genes (consanguinity). Pick a panel that matches the local spectrum **and reports copy-number changes**; otherwise trio exome/genome.
- In Shanghai all 17 genetic disorders could present at 0–3 months; only 5 also presented after 4 months.
- Post-Kasai infant with poor bile flow and JAG1 on the panel → re-label as Alagille; IBAT inhibitor eligibility; family testing (slide 19).
- Normal report is not "not genetic" when the phenotype moves on: reanalyze (EASL 2024: at least every 3 years); VUS → phase with parents, re-analyze (3117 VUS vs 662 P/LP in 2171 tests, Karpen 2021).

### 02-13 · Supportive care in the cholestatic infant (synced 2026-10-21)
- Same supportive program at any age (Mouzaki 2019): energy 130%, protein 130–150%, fat 30–50% with MCT:LCT 30:70 (TODO(verify) MCT share in Mouzaki 2019); length + MUAC + triceps skinfold every 2–4 weeks — weight is inflated by organomegaly and ascites.
- Vitamins every 3–6 months (monthly if severely cholestatic): retinol:RBP, 25-OH D, vitamin E:total lipid ratio, INR ± PIVKA-II; red-cell fatty acid profile every 3–6 months; urea/creatinine every 3 months (→ c_vitamins). A normal INR does not prove adequate vitamin K for bone.
- **UDCA in ABCB4 (MDR3, high-GGT PFIC):** the one setting with a real target — ~87% of patients with ≥ 1 missense variant responded, none with severe genotypes (Gonzales 2023, n = 38); EASL 2024 recommends it (strong). Guideline compare candidate (slide 18): UDCA in ABCB4 — North America nothing specific vs EASL recommended with ≥ 1 missense variant (strong) — DIFFERS. Outside ABCB4: not first line (EASL), no controlled data in Alagille. EASL: insufficient data for or against UDCA in AATD.
- **Itch ladder (slide 14, EASL 2024 ratings):** skin care + night-time sedating antihistamine for sleep only (marginal) → rifampin ~5 mg/kg/day, range 4–10, check liver tests (moderate; complete relief 15%, partial 36%, Gregorio 1993) → sertraline 1–4 mg/kg/day (14/20 improved, Thébaut 2017) → IBAT inhibitor (Alagille, selected PFIC) → diversion (responsive BSEP genotypes) or transplant. Cholestyramine binds vitamins and UDCA — separate by hours; marginal. TODO(verify) rifampin/sertraline doses, maximum per day and frequency against EASL 2024 before publishing.
- **Alagille itch:** EASL recommends offering an IBAT inhibitor (strong). ICONIC (maralixibat) and ASSERT (odevixibat) positive for itch and bile acids. Class effect: diarrhea → watch fat-soluble vitamins. Labels (lesson, 2026): FDA maralixibat Alagille ≥ 3 mo, odevixibat Alagille ≥ 12 mo; EMA odevixibat ≥ 6 mo — TODO(verify) current labels at card build.
- Measure itch at each visit (caregiver scratch score or ItchRO(Obs)); sleep loss is the outcome families feel.
- HPS: oximetry upright and supine routinely in chronic cholestasis; < 97% → investigate (changes transplant timing).
- Case candidates (slide 21): Alagille, 14 months, scratching all night despite rifampin → IBAT inhibitor, watch diarrhea and vitamins; biliary atresia, 9 months, SpO2 95% supine → investigate for HPS.

### 02-14 · Block 2 synthesis (synced 2026-10-22)
- NICCD (pearl 12): high GGT, very high AFP and citrulline; lactose-free MCT formula; almost all recover by 12 months. Biallelic SLC25A13 → no surgery **only if it explains the phenotype**; persistent acholic stools → cholangiogram anyway (02-12).
- Alagille + Kasai: transplant 47% vs 14%, death 32% vs 3% (Kaye 2010) — late-presenting infant with suspected atresia: echo, spine film, eye exam before the cholangiogram.
- Weight z-score −2.1 vs −1.2 at 6 months after Kasai separated poor from good outcome (DeRusso 2007) — measure the arm, not only the weight.

### 03-01 · Biliary atresia: epidemiology and phenotypes (synced 2026-10-23)
- **Late presenters (slides 5, 12):** CMV IgM-positive atresia presents older, with a larger spleen and more fibrosis. An infant referred late (> 60–90 d) with pale stools still needs the atresia pathway — never relabel as "CMV hepatitis" without a cholangiogram (extrapolated from slides 12 and 18).
- Vitamin K deficiency bleeding before the Kasai in 8.8% (335/3,809, Okubo 2025) — INR and parenteral vitamin K in every cholestatic infant (consistent with the 02-14 day-1 list).

### 03-04 · Biliary atresia: timing of the Kasai (synced 2026-10-26)
- **Late-presenting atresia (slides 8, 18, 20):** never "too old for a Kasai". Native liver after a Kasai > 3 mo: 19% at 25 y (France, Fanna 2019); > 90 d: 23% at 4 y (Canada, Schreiber 2007). Offer a Kasai if not decompensated (no ascites, INR corrects with vitamin K, no established cirrhosis on imaging/biopsy); transplant assessment first if ascites, uncorrected coagulopathy, cirrhosis. A working Kasai buys growth before transplant. Judge the liver, not the calendar; discuss with a transplant center (lesson gives no timing; "same week" is team expert opinion). No guideline addresses the older infant (GAP; expert practice, Zhan 2018).
- Case (slide 20): 16 wk, ascites, INR 2.0 despite vitamin K, cirrhotic liver → transplant assessment first.

### 03-06 · Biliary atresia: adjuvant therapy after the Kasai (synced 2026-10-28)
- Late-presenting atresia: a Kasai after 90 days — no adjuvant steroids (any signal is confined to early surgery; harm persists — START early SAEs 37% vs 19%, growth penalty; slides 11, 19). IBAT inhibitors after the Kasai — trials only (BOLD 2026 and EMBARK 2023 negative). Contrast with Alagille/PFIC, where ducts drain and IBAT inhibitors treat pruritus — keep the two lines apart on the card.

### 03-08 · Biliary atresia: prognosis after the Kasai (synced 2026-10-30)
- Late Kasai is not futile (slides 10, 19): 19% native liver at 25 y after month 3 (France), 23% at 4 y after > 90 d (Canada) — same numbers as 03-04; "discuss case by case".
- Kasai at 3–12 months of age: total bilirubin < 2 mg/dL by 3 months after the Kasai is the early signal (extrapolated — ChiLDReN/Shneider 2016 was not a late-Kasai cohort); TB > 6 mg/dL beyond 3 months → prompt transplant referral (AASLD 2014 or its 2026 successor — TODO(verify) current guideline and wording, see chol_0_3m Inbox).

### 03-11 · Biliary atresia: nutrition and growth (synced 2026-11-02)
- Same nutrition frame applies to any persistent infant cholestasis (Mouzaki 2019): energy ≥ 130% of age requirement (many centers 130–150%), protein 130–150% — no routine restriction (02-13 nuance: ≤ 2–3 days only for severe encephalopathy); fat 30–50% of energy; MCT about 30% of fat to start, more in tube feeds (50 vs 50–60% — lesson conflict, TODO(verify) as in the 02-13 entry), < 80% of fat (EFA deficiency). Detail and cases live in `chol_0_3m` 03-11 entry.
- Tube trigger (Mouzaki 2019, lesson slide 11): energy goals unmet > 2 weeks, or 2–4 weeks of falling weight, arm or length → fine-bore NG (NJ if vomiting) with overnight pump feeds; varices are not a contraindication to a soft tube; **no percutaneous gastrostomy with portal hypertension**.
- Pitfall: weight overstates nutrition (organs, ascites) — MUAC and triceps skinfold every 2–4 weeks.

### 03-12 · Biliary atresia: transplantation (synced 2026-11-05)
- **Kasai first even when late, unless decompensated (slide 8):** AASLD/AST/NASPGHAN 2026 (per lesson; "Martinez 2026" TODO(verify)): Kasai as initial management; primary LT evaluation only for late presentation with decompensated cirrhosis — consistent with the 03-04 "judge the liver, not the calendar" line. A prior Kasai does not lower post-LT graft or patient survival (Dong H et al. J Clin Med 2025;14:3328; Lemoine CP et al. J Clin Med 2022;11:3012).
- **Caution on the late-Kasai subgroup:** in Dong 2025 the Kasai > 90 d group (n = 37) had more post-LT graft loss (13.5% vs 4.5%) and mortality (10.8% vs 3.1%, P = 0.06), but the Kasai was not an independent predictor on multivariable analysis — do not use it as an argument against a late Kasai in a compensated infant (Learn layer, if at all).
- **Failed Kasai in this age band (slides 2, 4, 18):** TB > 6 mg/dL at 3 months after the Kasai, or rising → refer to a transplant center this week; TB 2–6 mg/dL at 3 months (jaundice not cleared) → refer to a transplant center, evaluation by 6–9 months of age (slide 4 / chapter Part 2), and at once if any complication appears ("Refer at the first complication, not the third", slide 21). ### 04-01 · Alagille syndrome I: genetics (synced 2026-11-07)
- **Molecular yield:** JAG1 94.3% (CHOP, 401 probands; Gilbert 2019) and 94.7% (GALA, 952 patients; Vandriel 2025); NOTCH2 2.5–3.6%; no variant 1.7–3.2%. Genetic testing makes the diagnosis in ~97% of probands.
- **Order a cholestasis panel that includes JAG1 and NOTCH2 and reports copy number (CNV):** whole-gene JAG1 deletions are ~13% of JAG1 variants (Gilbert 2019, CHOP) — not the same denominator as the ~6.4% of all probands in GALA (02-12 entry); reconcile at card build; sequencing alone detects ~88% (GeneReviews 2024). Ask the lab whether the panel calls CNVs. "JAG1-negative" after sequencing alone is not negative.
- **Negative panel, strong suspicion (slide 13):** MLPA or microarray → NOTCH2 → exome/genome with reanalysis of non-coding JAG1. Developmental delay or hearing loss with a JAG1 deletion → chromosomal microarray (20p12 contiguous deletion).
- **NOTCH2 hides the classic face (GALA 2025, 34 NOTCH2 vs 902 JAG1):** facies 58 vs 90%, cardiac 64 vs 92%, posterior embryotoxon 19 vs 53%, butterfly vertebrae 3 vs 45%; neonatal cholestasis, paucity, renal anomaly and liver outcome the same. → A cholestatic infant with a normal face and spine can still have Alagille syndrome.
- **VUS:** segregation in parents, phenotype search (echo, eye exam, spine film), functional data; liver biopsy acceptable as tie-breaker (EASL 2024 per lesson — TODO(verify) wording in Verkade 2024). Pre-2025 NOTCH2 "likely pathogenic" reports → request reinterpretation (31% downgraded to VUS, Vandriel 2025).
- **No genotype–phenotype correlation** (JAG1 or NOTCH2): never forecast a child's liver from a sibling's or parent's course; prognosis = the child's own trajectory (GALA bilirubin data — later block 4 lesson).
- **Paucity may be absent early** (64.9% on first biopsy in GALA JAG1) — do not wait for paucity in a young infant; genetics answers sooner.
- **Inheritance (GeneReviews 2024):** ~60% de novo / 40% inherited (TODO(verify) GeneReviews range); 50% risk to each child of a carrier; parental mosaicism at least 5/61 families (8.2%, Giannakudis 2001, a minimum estimate); parents negative in blood → recurrence still above population risk (germline mosaicism). Test both parents, offer cascade testing, record the exact variant.
- **Guideline compare candidates (slide 16):** first-line NGS — ALIGNED (Fawaz 2017, EASL 2024); biopsy role — DIFFERS; CNV testing, NOTCH2 interpretation, parental mosaicism — GAP; related living donor — MATERIAL gap.
- **Case (slides 2, 19):** 9 weeks, conjugated bilirubin 5.8 mg/dL, GGT 610 U/L, intermittently pale stools, murmur in both axillae, heterozygous JAG1 frameshift; father "childhood murmur, broad forehead" → no biopsy needed for diagnosis; no cholangiogram or Kasai on the assumption of atresia; echo, eye exam, spine film, kidney ultrasound; test father for the familial variant; carrier → 50% recurrence and not a donor.

### 04-02 · Alagille syndrome II: clinical criteria (synced 2026-11-08)
- **Definitions (slide 12):** classic (Alagille 1987) = bile duct paucity on biopsy + ≥ 3 of 5 (cholestasis, heart, vertebrae, eye, face); expanded clinical = cholestasis may replace biopsy, 3 of 7 (adds kidney, vascular); family history (GeneReviews) = any 1 major feature in a relative of a known proband; molecular (GeneReviews 2024) = suggestive findings + pathogenic/likely pathogenic JAG1 or NOTCH2; genotype-anchored (Turnpenny & Ellard 2012) = variant + family history, or variant + ≥ 1 feature incl. renal or cardiovascular. **Rule: once the variant is known, one feature suffices.** A VUS neither makes nor excludes the diagnosis.
- **Feature frequency in probands (Emerick 1999, 92 CHOP patients; denominators differ):** murmur 97%, cholestasis 96%, facies 96%, growth retardation 87%, paucity 85%, posterior embryotoxon 78%, butterfly vertebrae 51%, renal 40%, intracranial bleed/stroke 14%. Referral-centre probands: a ceiling, not the average carrier. Outcome: 21% transplant, 17% died, 20-year predicted survival 75%; only intracardiac disease predicted death (20-year survival 40% vs 80%).
- **Heart (McElhinney 2002, 200 JAG1/AGS):** any cardiovascular involvement 94%, branch PA stenosis/hypoplasia 76%, tetralogy 12%; right-sided lesions dominate. Peripheral pulmonary stenosis should always prompt the diagnosis. Every suspected case → echocardiogram; RV pressure known before liver transplant.
- **Face (Kamath 2002, blinded):** sensitivity 76%, specificity 82%; least reliable in infants, adults, NOTCH2 and across ethnic backgrounds → supporting feature only; photograph it.
- **Eye and spine are soft:** posterior embryotoxon in 8–15% of the general population (≈ 70% in 22q11.2 deletion); butterfly vertebrae also in 22q11.2 and other segmentation syndromes. Optic disc drusen on ocular ultrasound ≈ 90% (TODO(verify) source — lesson cites GeneReviews/Saleh 2016).
- **Kidney — "the sixth criterion" (Kamath 2012, 187 JAG1-positive with renal data):** 39% renal involvement; of those: dysplasia 59%, RTA 10%, VUR 8%, obstruction 8%. NOTCH2 renal 34.5% vs JAG1 39.2% (GALA 2025, NS). Work-up in every patient: creatinine, electrolytes with bicarbonate, urinalysis, renal ultrasound — before calcineurin inhibitors.
- **Vessels (Kamath 2004: 268 patients, 9% non-cardiac vascular anomaly/event, 34% of deaths [10/29] vascular, mostly intracranial bleeding, some after trivial head trauma; Emerick 2005: MRI/MRA abnormal in 38% of 26, 23% of asymptomatic screened; Carpenter 2018):** non-sedated brain MRI/MRA when feasible; urgent imaging after head trauma or any new neurological sign. A normal screen is not permanent reassurance. **No society guideline grades this** — expert opinion.
- **Seven-system work-up (slide 16):** liver (bilirubin, GGT, bile acids, lipids, INR, vitamins A D E K) · heart (echo) · spine (AP chest/spine film) · eye (slit lamp) · kidney (above) · brain vessels (MRI/MRA) · genes (panel with CNV + parents). Same work-up whether the diagnosis came from criteria or gene.
- **Molecular yield by features (slide 15):** JAG1 in 54% of probands with 3 features (diagnostic lab series); ~1/3 of those referred with 1–2 features (Exeter; Guegan 2012 — TODO(verify) which paper gives which number).
- **GALA prognosis (pearl 10):** native liver survival 54% at 10 y, 40% at 18 y; bilirubin < 5 mg/dL at 6–12 months → 79% reach adulthood with the native liver (Vandriel 2023 — TODO(verify) exact figures and denominators before publishing).
- **Guideline compare (slide 17):** diagnostic definition — Fawaz 2017 no formal criteria vs EASL 2024 genotype-based (DIFFERS); genetic testing — panels / early NGS (ALIGNED); biopsy — part of infant work-up vs EASL not routine for prognosis, diagnostic for a VUS (DIFFERS); before cholangiogram look for features (Fawaz) vs not addressed (EASL); renal evaluation — GAP; neurovascular screening — MATERIAL gap (a third of deaths, no graded recommendation). No dedicated AASLD Alagille guidance.
- **Traps (slide 18) → pearls:** ducts on a biopsy < 6 months do not exclude (paucity 60% vs 95%); no isotope excretion ≠ atresia (61% of AGS infants); 3 of 5 demanded after a pathogenic variant; related donor cleared because "does not meet criteria" (47% of carrier relatives do not); lone embryotoxon in a healthy relative as proof; expecting classic face/eye/spine in NOTCH2 (butterfly 3%, embryotoxon 19%); stopping at five systems; normal screening MRA as permanent reassurance.
- **Cases (slides 2, 20):** (1) 7 weeks, conjugated bilirubin 4.9 mg/dL, GGT 480, no excretion at 24 h, biopsy ductular reaction with no paucity in 11 portal tracts, murmur in both axillae with branch PA stenosis → echo, spine film, slit lamp and panel sent in parallel (a pending result never delays the cholangiogram if atresia is still likely); JAG1 positive → diagnosis made, kidney and brain vessels join the work-up, mother tested for the variant (not judged by her embryotoxon). (2) Clinical 3 of 5, panel negative → copy number, NOTCH2, exome; diagnosis stands clinically. (3) Confirmed AGS, age 6, never imaged → non-sedated MRI/MRA, renal ultrasound and bicarbonate.

### 04-03 · Alagille syndrome III: the liver (synced 2026-11-09)
- **Paucity arrives late (Emerick 1999; GALA):** 60% of biopsies < 6 mo (29/48) vs 95% > 6 mo (38/40); GALA 70% of cholestatic patients (421/604); first biopsy 64.9% JAG1, 56.5% NOTCH2 (Vandriel 2025 — TODO(verify)). Mechanism: failed postnatal branching — hilum preserved, periphery lost (Libbrecht 2005). Paucity = ducts/portal tracts < 0.9 on ≥ 10 tracts; CK7/CK19; not specific (~11% of pediatric biopsies, one centre).
- **EASL 2024 (verified, full text, rec. table):** routine biopsy for prognosis or management in ALGS **not recommended** (LoE 3, strong, 100%); **statins not recommended** for hyperlipidaemia or xanthomas (LoE 4, strong; one case report); **IBAT inhibitor should be offered** for cholestatic pruritus when available (LoE 2, strong). Known variant → never repeat biopsy to prove paucity.
- **Reading the panel:** GGT high (median 612 U/L < 1 y, ChiLDReN; ~85% of cholestatic infants — TODO(verify) which cohort) = duct disease, **not a predictor of outcome**, does not separate from atresia. Bilirubin = strongest early predictor, but use a **median over months**, not one value. ALT erratic; may rise after biliary diversion (182 → 260, Wang 2017). Correct vitamin K before reading INR.
- **Cholesterol = largely lipoprotein X** when bilirubin > 100 µmol/L (5.8 mg/dL) (Davit-Spraul 1996, 22 pts); LCAT low (Gottrand 1995). Janowski 2020 (17 ALGS vs 19 BA): worse lipids but lower cIMT and PWV. Treat the cholestasis, not the number; do not promise lifelong harmlessness (LDL, apoB also up; small, short studies).
- **Xanthomas:** 30% (40/132) with neonatal onset, 0/31 later onset (Lykavieris 2001); 43% at trial baseline; on maralixibat 71% responders at wk 48, 64% at wk 96 (Hoskins 2026, post hoc, n=14 — uncontrolled; TODO(verify)). PEBD: cholesterol 695 → 457 mg/dL (Wang 2017, n=20; TODO(verify)).
- **Prognosis by bilirubin — GALA (Vandriel 2023, n=1,433, 67 centres):** median total bilirubin at **6–12 months** vs < 5 mg/dL: 5 to < 10 → HR 4.1 portal hypertension, 4.8 transplant; ≥ 10 → HR 8.0 portal hypertension; > 10 → HR 15.6 transplant (abstract wording differs by band). Bilirubin < 5 → 79% reach adulthood with native liver. Cohort predictor, not a transplant date; born 1997–2019, pre-IBAT. Other windows: Kamath 2010 (mean birth–5 y: bili > 6.5, conj > 4.5, chol > 520 → severe; n=33, derivation only); Mouzaki 2016 (12–24 mo: bili 3.8 + fibrosis + xanthomas, AUC 0.79, n=144). **Don't borrow a threshold without its window.** Practice (lesson, expert synthesis): median bilirubin over 6–12 mo, re-check 12–24 mo; middle/upper band → early transplant-centre review before decompensation. No society sets a bilirubin threshold (MATERIAL gap).
- **Native liver survival:** GALA 54.4% at 10 y, 40.3% at 18 y; transplanted 27% by 5 y, 38% by 10 y, 50% by 18 y; PHT/LT/death 51.5% by 10 y, 66% by 18 y. Paris 2001: 51% at 10 y, 38% at 20 y; neonatal onset worse (44/132 transplanted vs 0/31). Old "20–30% need transplant" is outdated.
- **Cirrhosis is slower, not rare:** fewer ductules and less fibrosis than BA (Fabris 2007), but cirrhosis in 35/76 neonatal-onset assessed (Lykavieris 2001).
- **Guideline compare (slide 17):** biopsy after diagnosis — DIFFERS; statins — GAP (only EASL); IBAT for pruritus — DIFFERS (Fawaz 2017 predates); bilirubin risk thresholds — MATERIAL gap; non-invasive fibrosis follow-up — GAP; HCC surveillance — MATERIAL gap.
- **Case (slides 2, 20):** 9 mo, JAG1 frameshift, bilirubin 7.4, GGT 820, cholesterol 610, early xanthomas, biopsy at 8 wk with ducts present → accept diagnosis, no repeat biopsy; middle GALA band → transplant-centre review now; median bilirubin through 12 mo and at 12–24 mo; IBAT for itch; nutrition and vitamins; no statin; GGT not prognostic; "four in ten keep their liver; better if bilirubin falls below 5".

### 04-04 · Alagille syndrome IV: the heart (synced 2026-11-10)
- **Cardiac spectrum (McElhinney 2002, Circulation, 200 JAG1/ALGS):** any cardiovascular involvement 94%; branch pulmonary artery (PA) stenosis or hypoplasia 76%; tetralogy of Fallot (TOF) 12% (23/200), a third of it with pulmonary atresia (8/23); left-sided lesions 11% (22/200). Variant type/position does not predict the heart. GeneReviews 2024: TOF 7–16%. TODO(verify) numbers against McElhinney 2002 at build.
- **Genotype (GALA, Vandriel 2025, Liver Int):** echo-confirmed cardiac anomaly JAG1 92.2% (789/856) vs NOTCH2 64.3% (18/28) → **NOTCH2 lowers the prior but every child still gets echo + cardiology review** (pitfall: skipping echo in NOTCH2). Emerick 1999: murmur in 97% (as common as cholestasis 96%, facies 96%). TODO(verify) GALA denominators.
- **Workup line for the card:** ECG + echo with Doppler at diagnosis in every child with suspected ALGS; follow-up with a cardiologist who knows ALGS. A murmur in ALGS = branch PA stenosis until imaged; **a normal echo does not see lobar/segmental branches** (Tretter 2018: undiagnosed right-sided disease in up to 22%).
- **Prognosis line:** intracardiac congenital heart disease was the only clinical feature tied to higher mortality (Emerick 1999, n=92; predicted 20-y survival 75% overall, 80% without LT, 60% with LT); later reviews (Kamath 2010, Ruth 2019) summarize the age pattern — cardiac deaths lead in infancy, liver deaths later. Counsel isolated branch PA stenosis and TOF with pulmonary atresia + collaterals very differently (Bauser-Heaton 2017: ALGS independently predicted worse survival among 458 with TOF/MAPCAs).
- **Don't miss / reverse direction:** JAG1 variants in ~2% (2/94) of isolated TOF and ~4% (2/50) of pulmonary valve or peripheral pulmonary stenosis without an ALGS diagnosis (Bauer 2010, Hum Mutat) → a cardiology patient with mildly high GGT may carry JAG1.
- **Guideline compare (slide 17):** cardiac imaging at LT evaluation — DIFFERS (Squires 2014: echo all, 2-B; EASL 2024 no work-up advice); extrahepatic assessment — DIFFERS (Squires 1-B; EASL: refer to a transplant centre if liver involvement is substantial); stress testing — GAP; repair before LT — GAP; living related donor — DIFFERS (Squires 2-B: may be considered, exclude duct hypoplasia, avoid if same allele; others silent); cardiac exclusion threshold — MATERIAL gap (no society defines it). **Note:** the lesson cites Squires 2014, which Martinez 2026 (AASLD/AST/NASPGHAN, doi 10.1097/HEP.0000000000001805) replaces — TODO(verify) the 2026 wording on echo/CT angiography and living related donors before using the Squires column.
- **Case (slides 2, 20):** 3 y, JAG1, bilirubin 9.8 on IBAT inhibitor, xanthomas, height z −2.6, bilateral branch PA stenosis, tricuspid jet 4.2 m/s (RVSP ≈ 71 mmHg + RA, ~0.8 systemic) → not pulmonary hypertension and not a veto: CT angiography + catheter with dobutamine stress, treat gradients before listing, test the mother for the family variant before any donor work-up.
