### 03-13 · Biliary atresia: the adult with BA (synced 2026-11-05)
BA is a model for malignancy in a cholestatic, cirrhotic native liver; every number — TODO(verify) against the cited paper at build.
- **Systematic review (slide 15; Madadi-Sanjani O et al. Pediatr Surg Int 2025;41:165, 37 publications):** 57 malignancies in BA — 42 HCC, 8 cholangiocarcinoma (CCA), 5 hepatoblastoma, 2 other (bowel, thyroid); reported cases, not incidence; publication bias certain.
- **HCC (slides 15–16):** median age 4 y (7 months–34 y; a 38-year-old also reported); persistent jaundice in 77% of those with data; AFP raised in 68% with data → **a normal AFP does not exclude HCC** (and infant AFP norms differ — use `labs.json` bands); often incidental in the explant; 28 alive without recurrence after LT. King's (Hadžić N et al. J Pediatr 2011;159:617–22): 3/387 infants (0.8%, 1990–2008), median age 2 y, + 2 referred cases found in explants; only 2/5 raised AFP; all 5 transplanted and well.
- **CCA (slides 15–16):** median age 23.5 y (11–63); long-standing Roux loop, chronic biliary injury, stones, residual cysts; CA 19-9 raised in all 3 with data (CEA also mentioned); presents symptomatically — new stricture, porta mass, cholangitis that will not settle; 4 of 5 with outcome data died (Nio M et al. Surg Case Rep 2019;5:185: 63-year-old, cyst-type BA, unresectable hilar CCA). → Don't miss (adult/teen with BA): "A new hilar stricture is a tumor until proven otherwise — MRI/MRCP, CA 19-9, tissue, transplant team."
- **Surveillance (slide 17) — no BA-specific guideline; label "Adult cirrhosis guidance, extrapolated" and "Expert proposal":** every native-liver survivor — US + AFP every 6 months (AASLD 2023, Singal AG et al. Hepatology 2023;78:1922–65; EASL HCC CPG J Hepatol 2018;69:182–236: US every 6 months, AFP optional); persistent jaundice in childhood — US + AFP every 3–6 months and yearly CA 19-9 from adolescence (Madadi-Sanjani 2025 proposal, not a guideline); poor US window (adhesions, small liver) → contrast MRI; LT candidate → cross-sectional imaging at listing.
- **Guideline compare (slide 18):** HCC surveillance — DIFFERS (AASLD US + AFP vs EASL AFP optional); CCA surveillance in BA — lesson MATERIAL, no document addresses it → GAP by §5.2, MATERIAL reading in `note` (03-06/03-07 rule; DECISIONS at build).
- **Pitfall (slide 20):** stopping surveillance after childhood — HCC reported into the fourth decade.

### 03-14 · Block 3 synthesis: biliary atresia (synced 2026-11-06)
- **Synthesis (pearl 20, slides 3, 14):** native-liver tumours in BA split by age — HCC in young children (median 4 y), cholangiocarcinoma in adults (median 23.5 y) (57-tumour review — Madadi-Sanjani O et al. Pediatr Surg Int 2025;41:165, as in the 03-13 entry; Hadžić 2011 is the King's series); 57 tumours published, no surveillance guideline (gap). Surveillance frame: US + AFP every 6 months for life, CA 19-9 in adults; any new biliary stricture in an adult native liver = cholangiocarcinoma until proven otherwise.

### 04-03 · Alagille syndrome III: the liver (synced 2026-11-09)
- **Alagille: giant central regenerative nodule (Rapp 2017, verified abstract):** 6 of 20 imaged ALGS patients, segment VIII, cirrhotic features; T2-hypointense 6/6, isointense in all post-contrast phases 5/6, preserved ducts in 5 (pathology available in 5) → benign; MRI first, biopsy only if atypical (peripheral, arterial enhancement, rising AFP — lesson synthesis). Benign nodules up to 30% (Ayoub 2023 adult review — TODO(verify)).
- **HCC in ALGS (Ayoub 2023 — TODO(verify)):** ~35 cases (14 adults, 21 children); 67% of adult cases with known status non-cirrhotic; 38% normal AFP → AFP alone is not surveillance. US + AFP every 6 mo is expert opinion; no guideline interval (MATERIAL gap; EASL 2024 none).

### 04-13 · Alagille syndrome XIII: long-term outcomes (synced 2026-11-19)
- **TODO(verify) above now closed (Ayoub 2023, Hepatol Int 17:1098 — full text PMC10522532, verified 2026-11-19):** regenerative nodules "in up to 30% of patients", often > 5 cm, near the right portal vein without vascular invasion, T2-hypointense; HCC: 11% of pediatric and 67% of adult cases (with data) non-cirrhotic (Ayoub's set of 14 adults; Schindler's 13 adults give 5 of 9 non-cirrhotic — different case sets, not a contradiction); AFP normal in 38%; recommendation (expert): US + AFP every 6 months at all ages regardless of liver severity; contrast CT/MRI for questionable US lesions irrespective of AFP. Schindler 2021 (PMC7898517, verified): pediatric 16 of 18 cirrhotic, adult 4 of 9; two adults with no overt ALGS liver phenotype (58-year-old JAG1 index case with AFP 3.1 ng/mL; Keeffe 1993).
- **Card rule (slide 8):** in ALGS a size that alarms in cirrhosis is typical of a benign nodule → change over time, not size, is the signal; large nodule on the first US → contrast MRI to characterize, then it is the baseline (lesson slide 21; Ayoub Table 2); poor US visibility → contrast CT/MRI; new/growing/arterially enhancing → contrast MRI → transplant tumour board before any biopsy or resection → biopsy only if still unclear. Treatment: resection/locoregional in adults; transplant preferred in children (lesson; Valamparampil 2020 — TODO(verify)).
- **> 5 years branch:** add ALGS (any severity, incl. adults with mild disease) to the HCC-risk list alongside BA native liver, PFIC2 (BSEP), tyrosinemia — surveillance frame identical (US + AFP q6 mo, expert opinion).

### 05-01 · PFIC I: physiology of bile secretion (synced 2026-11-21)
- **BSEP deficiency (ABCB11) — HCC in infancy and early childhood, mostly truncating genotypes.** NAPPED (van Wessel 2020, J Hepatol 73:84): HCC by age 15 — BSEP1 4%, BSEP2 7%, BSEP3 (two truncating) 34%. FXR deficiency (NR1H4): very high AFP (to 400,000 ng/mL). → HCC-risk list: BSEP deficiency, especially BSEP3; surveillance with AFP + US even when well (interval TODO(verify) — EASL 2024 / block 5 lessons). Pitfall: skipping surveillance because the child looks well.

### 05-03 · PFIC III: BSEP deficiency (ABCB11) (synced 2026-11-23)
- **HCC in toddlers — VERIFIED (Knisely 2006, Hepatology 44:478; abstract):** 11 pediatric HCCs in PFIC/"neonatal hepatitis", age 13–52 mo; 9 (+ a sibling of a tenth) had BSEP deficiency. Strautnieks 2008 (abstract): HCC or cholangiocarcinoma 15%; 38% with two truncating variants. NAPPED: HCC by 15 y 4% / 7% / 34% by group. EASL 2024 (full text): ~15% HCC before age 5 with biallelic PPTMs; screen from diagnosis with US + AFP every 3 mo (PPTMs) or 3–6 mo (missense); keep screening after a good response to diversion.
- **→ card 19:** a mass in a cholestatic child < 5 y with low GGT → think BSEP deficiency; AFP is high in young infants — follow the trend; new nodule or rising AFP → contrast MRI/CT; cholangiocarcinoma also reported — image the whole liver. Low-GGT cholestasis belongs in the "1–5 years" and "< 1 year" rows as a risk factor.
