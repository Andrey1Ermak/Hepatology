## Inbox

### 02-12 · Genetics in the infant (synced 2026-10-20)
- Genetic yield is lowest in the sick preterm on PN: clinical exome 6.4% in NICU cholestasis (n = 140; 49% of 160 had PN-associated cholestasis — Wang H 2022); 4.3% in cholestasis with prematurity (Japan, n = 46 — Togawa 2016).
- **"Genome for everyone" is a trap** (slide 17): PN cholestasis in a preterm → yield about 4–6% (Togawa 2016; Wang H 2022), VUS anxiety, no gain. Send genetics only if cholestasis fails to improve off PN, or if the phenotype looks genetic (syndromic, low GGT, progressive disease in a term baby).
- This never removes the atresia check: acholic stools in a preterm still need the atresia pathway (cross-check with lesson 02-13/02-14 when it arrives).

### 02-13 · Supportive care in the cholestatic infant (synced 2026-10-21)
- Prophylactic UDCA in preterm PN-fed infants: meta-analysis of 5 studies (3 RCTs) — no fall in cholestasis, death, sepsis or NEC; peak conjugated bilirubin −0.13 mg/dL; low to very low certainty (Anne RP et al. Fundam Clin Pharmacol 2024;38:685–94) → "No benefit shown"; treatment data limited. TODO(verify) prevention vs treatment design in Anne 2024.
- Send urine bile acids by mass spectrometry before the first dose of UDCA (Fawaz 2017; lesson slide 20) — no exception for the preterm.
- Vitamin K and fat-soluble vitamins apply here too: INR on day 1, parenteral vitamin K if prolonged; vitamin levels once cholestasis persists (→ c_vitamins). TODO(verify) preterm-specific vitamin dosing — Mouzaki 2019 doses are for cholestatic infants generally.

### 02-14 · Block 2 synthesis (synced 2026-10-22)
- **Core rule (case 5, slide 14):** PN cholestasis is common, not exclusive — still exclude atresia. Preterm atresia had Kasai at a mean of 72 vs 53 days at term, largely because cholestasis was blamed on PN.
- Dose-response (Christensen 2007): cholestasis in ~43% after 4–8 weeks of PN, 72–85% beyond 8 weeks; overall 14% → 85% with longer exposure (pearl 13). TODO(verify) the exposure bands against Christensen 2007 J Perinatol 27:284–90.
- HIDA and biopsy are confounded by PN → stool color and bilirubin trajectory decide. Daily stool card in the NICU.
- Treatment: change the lipid emulsion; fish oil 1 g/kg/day rescues established PNAC (Puder 2009); advancing enteral feeds is the cure.
- **Escalation (blocking fix from review):** pale stool → fasting ultrasound **now** + daily stool card; persistent pallor or direct bilirubin rising despite advancing feeds → cholangiogram. The lesson says it two ways (slide 14 decision: "persistent pale stools … ultrasound and cholangiogram"; self-test slide 18 and chapter: "ultrasound, a daily stool card, and a cholangiogram if the pallor persists") — the card follows the self-test: ultrasound is not conditional. Preterm Kasai already comes late (72 vs 53 days — Chiu CY, J Pediatr 2013;163:100–3).
- Timing for preterm infants: NICE CG98 — prolonged jaundice > 21 days preterm; Fawaz 2017 has no separate preterm timing; AAP 2022 excludes < 35 weeks → guideline_compare row "Preterm infants" = GAP. No validated direct bilirubin cut-off below 35 weeks (gap, slide 17) → "Limited data".
- Genetic yield lowest here (~4%, pearl 17), consistent with 02-12 Inbox.
- Case candidate (slide 14): born 29 weeks, now 35 weeks corrected, 6 weeks PN after NEC resection, direct 4.4 and rising, GGT 310, feeds 30 mL/kg/day, pale stools on 2 days → fasting ultrasound now, switch lipid, advance feeds, daily card; persistent pallor or rising direct bilirubin → cholangiogram. Self-test answer (slide 18): "No: ultrasound, stool card, cholangiogram if persistent."
