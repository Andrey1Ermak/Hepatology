### 03-01 · Biliary atresia: epidemiology and phenotypes (synced 2026-10-23)
- **Cystic biliary atresia vs choledochal cyst (slides 14, 18):** an antenatal hilar cyst (seen at 18–20 weeks; 5.5% of the Japanese registry diagnosed antenatally) plus pale stools and raised conjugated bilirubin (chapter wording; either alone → same-day hepatology/surgical review, Expert opinion) = cystic atresia until proven otherwise → urgent cholangiogram and Kasai on the atresia clock, not elective cyst repair.
- Clues to atresia: cyst usually smaller, small or irregular gallbladder, no dilated intrahepatic ducts. Stool color and conjugated bilirubin decide, not the cyst (Davenport 2021; Fawaz 2017 notes a cyst may coexist with atresia — guideline_compare ALIGNED).
- Outcome: clearance 79% in type I cyst vs 60% in type III (Okubo 2025); > 75% clearance and native-liver preservation after early surgery (Davenport 2021). Some decompensate in adulthood → lifelong follow-up.
- Case (slide 20): antenatal 1.5 cm hilar cyst; at 2 weeks pale stools, conjugated bilirubin 2.4 mg/dL → treat as cystic atresia. Link → chol_0_3m.

### 03-03 · Biliary atresia: diagnosis (synced 2026-10-25)
- **MMP-7 is high in choledochal cyst:** 7 of 8 above the 52.8 ng/mL single-plex cut-off in ChiLDReN (Pandurangi 2024) — a high MMP-7 with a cyst is not atresia by default.
- **Porta cyst on US (slides 12, 15):** non-dilated intrahepatic ducts → cystic atresia; dilated intrahepatic ducts → choledochal cyst. At cholangiogram, a bile-free cyst with abnormal intrahepatic ducts is cystic atresia.
- Case (slide 20): 8-week-old, high MMP-7, porta cyst with dilated intrahepatic ducts → choledochal cyst more likely; MRCP or cholangiogram, not a Kasai by default.
