### 02-11 · Syndromic cholestasis (synced 2026-10-19)
- Low/normal GGT + conjugated hyperbilirubinemia narrows the list to ARC, SLOS, bile-acid synthesis defects and PFIC types (lesson slide 3).
- When GGT is low, look beyond the liver: contractures, Fanconi, ichthyosis, large platelets (ARC); 2–3 toe syndactyly, genital anomalies, microcephaly (SLOS); diarrhea, pancreatitis, deafness (PFIC1).
- **ARC (VPS33B ~75%, VIPAS39 most of the rest):** arthrogryposis 97%, normal GGT, renal Fanconi or nephrogenic DI, ichthyosis (Gissen 2006; Eastham 2001). **Do not biopsy** (liver or kidney): 7/16 biopsied children had life-threatening hemorrhage; platelet count normal in 9/11 severe bleeds (Gissen 2006). Diagnose by genome/panel, urine studies, platelet EM or flow cytometry.
- No liver guideline warns against biopsy in suspected ARC (NASPGHAN/ESPGHAN 2017, EASL 2024 silent) → guideline_compare row, verdict MATERIAL (changes tactics); the warning lives in case series.
- **SLOS (DHCR7):** send plasma 7-dehydrocholesterol by GC-MS; total cholesterol is normal in ~10% (GeneReviews, Nowaczyk). Draw sterols before cholesterol-containing feeds or transfusion. Treatment: dietary cholesterol (± bile acids in cholestasis); statins not recommended.
- Bile-acid level as splitter (matches §4.2 card 5): low GGT + normal/low serum bile acids → synthesis defect (urine bile acids by mass spectrometry; cholic acid works); low GGT + high bile acids → transport/trafficking defect.
- Zellweger spectrum (PEX; GGT variable): hypotonia, large fontanelle, seizures, renal cysts → VLCFA first.
- When building the card, also show panhypopituitarism (classic low-GGT cause in infants) and TJP2 / MYO5B / USP53 explicitly (reviewer note; verify sources then).

### 02-12 · Genetics in the infant (synced 2026-10-20)
- GGT is the gene filter (slide 6, Wang NL 2016, n = 207, Shanghai): restricting ATP8B1/ABCB11 sequencing to low peak GGT raised yield 18.8% → 35.1% and missed no case; 0/96 above cut-off had PFIC1/2. Age cut-offs that captured all cases: < 70 U/L at 2–6 mo, < 60 U/L at 7–12 mo, < 50 U/L over 1 y. **Caveat:** single-center, pre-panel era → use GGT to read a panel or pick single genes, not to refuse broad testing. - Low/normal GGT genes: ATP8B1, ABCB11, TJP2, MYO5B, USP53; synthesis defects (HSD3B7, AKR1D1); ARC, SLOS.
- Biallelic ATP8B1/ABCB11/TJP2/USP53 (phased with parents) + low GGT before surgery → no Kasai; genotype guides IBAT inhibitor and biliary diversion (slide 13).

### 02-13 · Supportive care in the cholestatic infant (synced 2026-10-21)
- Urine bile acids by mass spectrometry **before** UDCA: synthesis defects (low GGT, low serum bile acids) are cured by cholic acid; UDCA blurs the profile (Fawaz 2017).
- UDCA cannot replace a missing transporter (FIC1, BSEP) or correct a synthesis defect; EASL 2024 rates efficacy low → not first line in PFIC1/2.
- IBAT inhibitors in PFIC (PEDFIC1 odevixibat PFIC1/2: positive itch 55% vs 30%, bile acid response 33% vs 0%, diarrhea 31% vs 10%; MARCH-PFIC maralixibat: BSEP cohort ItchRO −1.7 vs −0.6, diarrhea 57% vs 20%). Need residual secretion: severe BSEP deficiency with no functional protein is excluded from the maralixibat label. Bile acid responders are the patients in whom native-liver survival may improve. FDA labels (lesson, 2026): odevixibat PFIC ≥ 3 mo, maralixibat PFIC ≥ 12 mo — TODO(verify) at card build.
- **Biliary diversion by genotype (EASL 2024):** not routinely in FIC1 (ATP8B1) (LoE 3, strong per lesson 04-09 slide 18; TODO(verify) wording — same TODO as the 04-09 entry); consider in BSEP with a responsive genotype — itch response 54% overall, 66% with E297G or D482G, 36% other missense, 0/3 with two truncating variants.
- Two truncating ABCB11 variants + refractory itch → IBAT inhibitor and diversion unlikely to work → plan transplant (lesson case, slide 21).
- Diarrhea on IBAT inhibitors worsens fat malabsorption → recheck fat-soluble vitamins after starting.
- Guideline compare candidates (slide 18): IBAT inhibitors in PFIC (FDA odevixibat ≥ 3 mo, maralixibat ≥ 12 mo vs EMA odevixibat ≥ 6 mo — DIFFERS; TODO(verify) current labels; the Alagille statement lives in chol_3_12m); biliary diversion (EASL genotype-guided vs no North American graded statement — GAP).

### 03-03 · Biliary atresia: diagnosis (synced 2026-10-25)
- A normal or low GGT in a cholestatic infant points away from atresia → PFIC1/2, bile acid synthesis defects, TJP2 (slide 4). Atresia with low GGT exists but is uncommon — pale stools still go to the atresia pathway.
- GGT is physiologically higher in neonates — interpret "low" against age norms (→ lab_norms / ref_norms).
- Case (slide 20): 5-week-old, GGT 90 U/L, normal contracting gallbladder, MMP-7 below the assay cut-off → atresia unlikely; work up PFIC, bile acid synthesis, genetics.

### 04-07 · Alagille syndrome VII: the pruritus ladder (synced 2026-11-13)
- The ladder is extrapolated from ALGS (expert opinion; the PFIC ladder is genotype-dependent — block 5 lessons decide): measure with a 2-wk ItchRO(Obs) diary (0–4, twice daily; ≥ 1-point fall = responder); give each rung a scored 4–6-wk trial; remove a failed rung before adding the next.
- Natural control (Kremer 2016, slide 4): children with bile acid synthesis defects are jaundiced but make few toxic bile acids and do not itch. → Low-GGT cholestasis + low or normal serum bile acids (often no itch) → suspect a synthesis defect; confirm with urine bile acid analysis (FAB-MS) before UDCA. **Absent itch does not exclude PFIC in young infants** — scratching appears later (reviewer, blocking).
- Rifampin 5–10 mg/kg/day with ALT/bilirubin/INR at 2, 4, 8 wk (adult hepatitis 7.3%, Prince 2002); strong CYP3A4 inducer, lowers 25-OH D. Naltrexone titrated, blocks opioid analgesia. Sertraline — boxed warning. Cholestyramine — separate from UDCA and vitamins, hyperchloremic acidosis. TODO(verify) doses at build (see chol_3_12m 04-07 entry).

### 04-09 · Alagille syndrome IX: surgical biliary diversion (synced 2026-11-15)
- **BSEP deficiency is where diversion helps:** NAPPED (van Wessel 2020, J Hepatol 73:84) — median native liver survival by genotype BSEP1 (≥ 1 p.E297G or p.D482G) 20.4 y, BSEP2 (other non-truncating) 7.0 y, BSEP3 (two truncating) 3.5 y; in BSEP1–2, surgical biliary diversion linked to longer native liver survival (HR 0.50, 95% CI 0.27–0.94); serum bile acids < 102 µmol/L or a fall ≥ 75% soon after surgery predicted ≥ 15 y with the native liver. EASL 2024: consider surgical interruption of the enterohepatic circulation in BSEP deficiency (LoE 3, strong); FIC1 — not routine (LoE 3, strong; TODO(verify) wording in EASL 2024 at build — block 5 lessons decide, including why bilirubin fell after PEBD in FIC1 yet diversion is not routine). BSEP3 (two truncating variants): no diversion benefit shown → plan early transplant assessment (reviewer; block 5 confirms); HCC surveillance in BSEP deficiency — block 5.
- ChiLDReN (Wang 2017): bilirubin fell after PEBD in FIC1 (8.1 → 2.9 mg/dL), not in ALGS. Genotype changes the answer → genetic panel before any diversion decision.
- Ileal exclusion removes the IBAT-inhibitor target (mechanistic) and its effect fades → prefer PEBD or drug; IBAT inhibitor before surgery where licensed for the genotype (block 5 decides).

### 05-01 · PFIC I: physiology of bile secretion (synced 2026-11-21)
- **Why (one line):** GGT is stripped off canalicular and duct membranes by bile acids; serum GGT rises only when bile acids reach them. Low GGT + high serum bile acids = export, membrane, sensor, trafficking or seal failure (BSEP, FIC1, FXR, MYO5B, TJP2); high GGT = bile acids without phospholipid (MDR3) or a diseased duct (lesson slides 2, 14; Fawaz 2017; EASL 2024).
- **Splitter rule (slide 14) → pathway node:** low GGT + high serum bile acids + itch → retention defect until proved otherwise (panel now); low GGT + low/normal serum bile acids + no itch → synthesis defect until urine bile acid mass spectrometry says otherwise (oral cholic acid). Keep the 04-07 caveat: absent itch does not exclude PFIC in a young infant.
- **Sorting table (slide 15; EASL 2024, Sambrotta 2014, Gomez-Ospina 2016, Gonzales 2017):** retention — ABCB11, ATP8B1, TJP2, NR1H4, MYO5B, USP53 (diarrhea, pancreatitis, hearing loss → ATP8B1, TJP2, USP53); synthesis — HSD3B7, AKR1D1, CYP7B1, peroxisomal (vitamin deficiency out of proportion, no itch); syndromic — VPS33B, VIPAS39 (ARC). Candidate `table` block in Why.
- **Five exceptions (slide 16) → pearls/pitfalls:** (1) neonatal GGT is physiologically high — "low" means low for the degree of cholestasis (lesson example: 150 U/L at 2 weeks can still be low — illustrative, not a cut-off; do not publish as a threshold); (2) end-stage BSEP/FIC1 can raise GGT modestly; (3) missense/single-copy ABCB4 may have near-normal GGT between episodes; (4) synthesis defects — low GGT, no itch, low/normal acids; (5) ARC, Crigler–Najjar, Gilbert are low-GGT too, only ARC is cholestatic. Rule: read GGT with conjugated bilirubin, bile acids, itch and age; repeat after the neonatal period; one borderline GGT never delays genetics in an itching child.
- **NR1H4/FXR (Gomez-Ospina 2016, Nat Commun 7:10713; 4 children, 2 families):** no canalicular BSEP despite normal ABCB11; GGT 45–59 U/L; very high AFP (146,000 ng/mL at presentation in one child, 400,000 before transplant); early coagulopathy, INR 2.0 not behaving like vitamin K deficiency; 2 died in infancy, 2 transplanted. → Don't miss / pearl: low GGT + early vitamin-K-resistant coagulopathy + very high AFP → think FXR; transplant assessment early. TODO(verify) numbers against the paper at build.
- **MYO5B (Gonzales 2017; Qiu 2017):** low-GGT cholestasis with BSEP mislocalized inside the cell, with or without microvillus inclusion disease; a normal ABCB11 sequence ≠ BSEP at the canaliculus → BSEP immunostaining on any biopsy helps. Liver transplant can worsen MVID diarrhea.
- **TJP2 (Sambrotta 2014, Nat Genet 46:326; 33 children/29 families with unexplained low-GGT cholestasis):** homozygous truncating TJP2 in 12 children from 8 families; GGT never raised after the neonatal period; 9/12 transplanted, 2 stable with mild PH, 1 died at 13 months. Later series: hearing loss, respiratory, neurological features. TODO(verify) abstract.
- **BSEP genotype (NAPPED, van Wessel 2020 — already in Inbox 04-09):** adds HCC by age 15: BSEP1 4%, BSEP2 7%, BSEP3 34% → Don't miss: AFP + US surveillance in BSEP deficiency even when well (frequency per block 5 lessons / EASL 2024; TODO(verify)).
- **FIC1 (NAPPED FIC1, van Wessel 2021, Hepatology 74:892):** fewer than half reach adulthood with native liver; number of truncating variants did not predict course (unlike BSEP). Systemic epithelial disease: diarrhea, pancreatitis, hearing loss, short stature; after LT diarrhea worsens and graft steatosis follows → diversion often preferred over LT (EASL 2024: diversion not routine in FIC1 — conflict to resolve at build; lesson synthesis slide 21 suggests diversion of the graft for post-LT diarrhea — expert opinion only).
- **Therapy by residual function (slide 17) → pathway "treatment" section:** UDCA — low efficacy in FIC1/BSEP (EASL 2024); IBAT inhibitor — PEDFIC1 (Thompson 2022, Lancet Gastroenterol Hepatol 7:830; n = 62, 24 wk): positive itch assessments 55% vs 30%, serum bile acid response 33% vs 0%; needs some pump; McKiernan 2023 expert opinion: may start while genetics pending; BSEP3 and FXR deficiency rarely respond → early transplant assessment + HCC surveillance. Post-LT anti-BSEP antibodies can mimic recurrence (→ post_lt).
- **guideline_compare rows (slide 18; lesson verdicts):** GGT as sorting test — ALIGNED (Fawaz 2017 / EASL 2024); how to test genes — DIFFERS (EASL: panel or exome, reanalysis ≥ every 3 y); biopsy/immunostaining — DIFFERS (EASL: when non-invasive work-up inconclusive); UDCA in ABCB4 — GAP (only EASL, LoE 3 strong; belongs to chol_3_12m); IBAT inhibitors — DIFFERS (labels vs McKiernan 2023 vs EASL "residual function", LoE 2); surgical diversion — MATERIAL (only EASL grades it). No AASLD PFIC guidance. TODO(verify) EASL wording and LoE at build.
- **Traps (slide 19) → pitfalls:** normal GGT reassuring in an itching infant; GGT 150 at 2 weeks called "high" → atresia-only thinking; missing a synthesis defect because no itch; assuming normal BSEP sequence = BSEP present; expecting cure from IBAT/diversion with two truncating ABCB11 variants; forgetting gut/pancreas/ear in FIC1; skipping AFP + US in BSEP deficiency; never reanalysing a negative exome.
- **Cases (slides 2, 21) → `case`:** Infant A — 3 mo boy, direct bili 5.8 mg/dL, GGT 42, bile acids 310 µmol/L, itch, watery stools → panel now, consider IBAT inhibitor while waiting, vitamins; FIC1 favoured by diarrhea. Child C — low GGT, low bile acids, no itch, low vitamin E → urine bile acid MS, cholic acid. BSEP3 + rising AFP → transplant assessment and imaging now.

### 05-02 · PFIC II: FIC1 deficiency (ATP8B1) (synced 2026-11-22)
- **Why (one line):** FIC1 is a P4-type ATPase lipid flippase, not a bile acid pump; without it the canalicular membrane loses its rigid outer leaflet, bile acids strip cholesterol from it and BSEP works poorly → low GGT, bland canalicular cholestasis. ATP8B1 is expressed in every apical epithelium (liver, gut, pancreas, inner ear, sweat gland, kidney) → a membrane disease of many organs (Paulusma 2006, 2008; Stapelbroek 2009; lesson slides 3–4). CFTR downregulated in FIC1-deficient liver and gut — plausible link to diarrhea, pancreatitis, high sweat chloride (Demeilliers 2006; mechanism, not proven).
- **Bedside rule (slide 6) → pathway node / pearl:** low GGT + diarrhea, pancreatic disease or hearing loss → favors FIC1; low GGT + high aminotransferases, giant-cell hepatitis, gallstones or a liver mass → favors BSEP (Pawlikowska 2010; Davit-Spraul 2010 — TODO(verify) abstracts at build).
- **FIC1 vs BSEP table (slide 7) → candidate `table` in Why:** aminotransferases modest vs higher; bland cholestasis vs giant-cell hepatitis; coarse granular "Byler bile" on EM vs amorphous/filamentous; BSEP stain present vs often absent; extrahepatic disease common vs rare; no hepatobiliary cancer link vs HCC/cholangiocarcinoma in childhood; after LT — diarrhea, steatosis, pancreatitis vs anti-BSEP antibody recurrence.
- **Spectrum (slide 5):** PFIC1 and BRIC1 are ends of one continuum; BRIC — episodes of weeks to months, normal bile acids between; siblings with the same variants can differ; BRIC can progress. Founder variants: p.G308V (Amish, Byler), p.D554N (Inuit/Greenland); p.I661T common in Europeans, often BRIC (Klomp 2004 — TODO(verify)). Heterozygous carriers: ICP and adult cryptogenic cholestasis → ask family history.
- **Natural history — VERIFIED (van Wessel 2021, Hepatology 74:892; abstract, Europe PMC 33666275; published in ref_genetics 2026-11-22):** n = 130; native liver survival (NLS) 44% at 18 y; NLS at 10 y by truncating-allele count 67% / 41% / 59% (p = 0.12) — genotype does not sort FIC1 (contrast BSEP); serum bile acids at presentation ≥ 194 µmol/L → NLS at 10 y 15% vs 49% (p = 0.03); surgical biliary diversion lowered bile acids 230 → 74 µmol/L (p = 0.005); diversion HR 0.55 (95% CI 0.28–1.03, p = 0.06) and post-diversion bile acids < 65 µmol/L (p = 0.05) only *tended* to longer NLS. Authors: bile acids give "limited prognostic information". → Pitfall: promising a mild course from the genotype. Pearl: track bile acids, not genotype.
- **Resolves the 05-01 / 04-09 conflict (diversion in FIC1):** lesson position — in FIC1 diversion is symptom control (itch) with trend-level liver benefit; EASL 2024: surgical interruption not recommended routinely to prolong native liver survival in FIC1 (lesson: LoE 3, strong — TODO(verify) wording in full text, easl_2024_genetic_cholestasis). Bull 2018 abstract: diversion improved biochemistry, itch and growth; FIC1 and BSEP-common survived longer after diversion than BSEP-other. → At build: "Offer diversion for refractory itch with preserved synthetic function; do not promise liver rescue" + guideline_compare row MATERIAL (only EASL grades it).
- **Medical therapy (slide 10):** UDCA — low efficacy in FIC1 (EASL 2024); rifampicin — moderate, off label, monitor ALT; cholestyramine — marginal, vitamin loss; odevixibat — PEDFIC1 (Thompson 2022, FIC1 + BSEP pooled): itch 55% vs 30%, bile acid response 33% vs 0% (already in Inbox 05-01); maralixibat — MARCH-PFIC (Miethke 2024): bile acids −157 vs +3 µmol/L, diarrhea 57% vs 20% (TODO(verify) abstract). FIC1-specific: trials pooled types, FIC1-alone response small. **Before an IBAT inhibitor, record baseline stool frequency** (pre-existing secretory diarrhea vs drug-induced bile acid diarrhea).
- **Pre-LT / surveillance (slides 6, 16):** audiogram — GeneReviews suggests screening every 5 years without known hearing loss (expert; TODO(verify) GeneReviews ATP8B1, updated 2021); avoid aminoglycosides; lipase + fecal elastase for pancreatic disease; no HCC surveillance shown to be needed in FIC1 (contrast BSEP). Listing: decompensated cirrhosis, or intractable itch after IBAT inhibitor and diversion failed or are unsuitable.
- **guideline_compare rows (slide 17; lesson verdicts):** low GGT → genetic work-up ALIGNED (Fawaz 2017 / EASL 2024); UDCA in FIC1 DIFFERS (only EASL: low efficacy); IBAT inhibitors DIFFERS (FDA labels vs EASL "variable by type and genotype"); diversion to save the native liver MATERIAL (EASL: not routine); post-LT diarrhea/steatosis MATERIAL (EASL LoE 5, weak — belongs to post_lt); hearing/pancreas surveillance GAP (no document). No AASLD PFIC guidance.
- **Traps (slide 18) → pitfalls:** chronic diarrhea in a low-GGT infant called "cholestatic malabsorption"; HCC surveillance as for BSEP but never an audiogram; diversion sold as liver-saving; IBAT inhibitor without baseline stool count; "transplant cures FIC1"; high sweat chloride → chasing CF first (panel with ATP8B1 instead — lesson slide 20, expert).
- **Cases (slides 2, 20) → `case`:** boy with FIC1 — low GGT from 2 mo, bile acids 280 µmol/L, height z −2.5, loose stools; itch failed rifampicin and IBAT; cirrhosis; LDLT at 6 y; year 1 — 8 watery stools/day, 60% macrovesicular graft steatosis → binder (timed away from tacrolimus), then off-label IBAT inhibitor, steatosis imaging, lipase, audiogram (illustrative — built from Lykavieris 2003 and Bull 2018 patterns). Teen with FIC1 never tested → audiogram now.

### 05-03 · PFIC III: BSEP deficiency (ABCB11) (synced 2026-11-23)
- **Why (one line):** BSEP is the rate-limiting canalicular bile acid pump; without it bile acids stay in the hepatocyte and act as detergents → hepatitis-range ALT, giant-cell hepatitis, low GGT (little bile acid reaches the ducts) — more hepatitic and faster than FIC1, and the PFIC with real childhood cancer risk (Strautnieks 1998; EASL 2024).
- **Variant classes — VERIFIED (Strautnieks 2008, Gastroenterology 134:1203; abstract, Europe PMC 18395098):** 82 mutations in 109 families (9 nonsense, 10 small indels, 15 splice, 3 whole-gene deletions, 45 missense); p.E297G and/or p.D482G in 58% of European families (52/89); immunostaining abnormal or absent in 93% of 88, but some BSEP visible in 45% (19/42) with E297G/D482G (published in ref_biopsy 2026-11-23); HCC or cholangiocarcinoma 15% (19/128); two truncating 38% (8/21) vs 10% (11/107), RR 3.7 (1.7–8.1).
- **NAPPED genotype groups (van Wessel 2020; already in ref_genetics):** BSEP1 ≥ 1 E297G/D482G (n = 72), BSEP2 other missense (n = 136), BSEP3 two predicted truncating (n = 56); median NLS 20.4 / 7.0 / 3.5 y; NLS at 18 y 32% overall; HCC by 15 y 4% / 7% / 34%; diversion bile acids 363 → 48 µmol/L, < 102 µmol/L predicts NLS ≥ 15 y after surgery (AUC 0.78), HR 0.50 (0.27–0.94) in BSEP1+2; HCC 2/61 after diversion vs 13/180 without (p = 0.32 — diversion is not protection). Lesson extras (homozygous D482G vs E297G diversion 26% vs 90%; 16 deaths before LT) — TODO(verify) full text.
- **CORRECTION to the lesson's BSEP1 table (slide 13) — VERIFIED (Felzen 2023, JHEP Rep 5:100626; abstract):** one E297G/D482G + one truncating (BSEP1/3, n = 30) is as severe as two truncating: NLS at 10 y 21% vs 75% (BSEP1/1) and 23% (BSEP3/3); poor response to diversion (10 y after: 27% / 92% / 14%). EASL 2024 adopts it ("may require a treatment approach similar to two PPTMs"). → At build: the default plan keys on "two mild" vs "any truncating", not on "at least one mild" (published in ref_genetics 2026-11-23; D-2026-11-23-1).
- **Spectrum (slide 5):** severe PFIC2 → moderate (missense) → BRIC2 (episodes, normal interval tests; can progress; gallstones) → ICP and drug-induced cholestasis in heterozygotes or common variants. A teen with first low-GGT cholestasis may sit at the mild end.
- **Bedside rule (slide 6):** low GGT + hepatitis-range ALT + giant cells (± gallstones, liver mass) → BSEP over FIC1; diarrhea, pancreatitis or deafness → FIC1. NGS panel or exome early; reanalyze at least every 3 y when the first round gave no diagnosis (EASL 2024, VERIFIED full text p.304); baseline AFP, ultrasound, vitamins, INR; record genotype group in the first letter.
- **Medical therapy (slide 10, EASL 2024 Table 3 per lesson — TODO(verify) table):** UDCA low; rifampicin moderate for itch; cholestyramine limited (separate from vitamins); 4-phenylbutyrate experimental, missense only. **EASL 2024 (VERIFIED full text, Table/recommendations):** IBAT inhibition should be considered for itch before LT in missense variants (LoE 2, strong); surgical EHC interruption to prolong NLS in a responsive genotype (LoE 3, strong) and for itch (LoE 3, weak); not yet possible to prefer medical over surgical interruption for NLS (LoE 5, weak); BSEP with two PPTMs — no surgical interruption, early transplant.
- **MARCH-PFIC — VERIFIED (Miethke 2024, Lancet Gastroenterol Hepatol 9:620; abstract):** primary cohort = biallelic **non-truncated** BSEP only (14 vs 17): ItchRO(Obs) −1.7 vs −0.6 (difference −1.1, −1.8 to −0.3); bile acids −176 vs +11 µmol/L (difference −187, −293 to −80); diarrhea 57% vs 20% (whole trial). Note: the 05-02 Inbox figure "−157 vs +3" is not the BSEP cohort — TODO(verify) which cohort before using either. PEDFIC 1 excluded complete absence of BSEP protein. Lesson: no truncating ABCB11 patient had a bile acid response in the phase 2 maralixibat study — TODO(verify).
- **Default plan by genotype (slide 13; synthesis, individualize):** two mild variants → IBAT inhibitor, then diversion, aim bile acids < 102 µmol/L, US + AFP every 3–6 mo; other missense → same, individualized; any truncating (incl. BSEP1/3) → rifampicin for itch, US + AFP every 3 mo, list early.
- **guideline_compare rows (slide 18):** low GGT → genetic work-up ALIGNED (Fawaz 2017 / EASL 2024); management by genotype DIFFERS (only EASL); HCC surveillance MATERIAL (only EASL, genotype-based interval); IBAT in BSEP DIFFERS (FDA labels vs EASL missense only); diversion DIFFERS; anti-BSEP after LT MATERIAL (→ post_lt). No AASLD/NASPGHAN PFIC guidance.
- **Traps (slide 19) → pitfalls:** normal GGT read as reassurance; trusting a positive BSEP stain over the genotype; surveillance started at school age; IBAT inhibitor or diversion in truncating disease delaying transplant; weaning immunosuppression in BSEP3 recipients.
- **Cases (slides 2, 21):** 3-mo, GGT normal, ALT 400, giant cells → NGS + baseline AFP/US; homozygous D482G with itch → IBAT, diversion if fails; two nonsense at 18 mo → US + AFP every 3 mo, list early; bile acids 240 µmol/L 6 mo after diversion → plan transplant, keep surveillance.

### 05-04 · PFIC IV: MDR3 deficiency (ABCB4) (synced 2026-11-24)
- **Contrast for the card (slides 3, 18):** MDR3 deficiency (PFIC3) is the **high-GGT** PFIC — bile acid secretion is normal but bile lacks phosphatidylcholine, so free bile acids injure cholangiocytes (ductular reaction, biliary fibrosis). A normal GGT points away from ABCB4; a high GGT must not be read as "not PFIC" (→ chol_3_12m, ggt_isolated). Mechanism per EASL 2024 and Davit-Spraul 2009.
- **Table row for "Low vs high GGT PFIC" (slide 4):** FIC1, BSEP, TJP2, NR1H4, MYO5B → low GGT; MDR3 → high GGT, cholangiopathy, intrahepatic stones. → publish at card build (EASL 2024 full text to cite).
- **IBAT inhibitors in MDR3 (slide 9):** MARCH/MARCH-ON MDR3 subgroup is 9 children, conference poster only (Sokal/Thompson, ESPGHAN 2024); placebo arm also improved; EASL 2024 has no MDR3-specific IBAT recommendation. Do not publish numbers (poster) — "Limited data".

### 05-05 · PFIC V: the new genes (synced 2026-11-25)
- **Why (one line):** the newer genes break bile secretion around the canalicular pumps, not in them — tight junction (TJP2), bile acid sensor (FXR/NR1H4), apical trafficking (MYO5B), deubiquitinase at junctions (USP53), signalling (SEMA7A); all low/normal GGT. High-GGT newcomers (KIF12, ZFYVE19) belong to chol_3_12m/duct_dilation (lesson slides 3, 14; EASL 2024 Table 5 — TODO(verify) table wording).
- **Rule → pathway/pearl:** low GGT no longer means "FIC1 or BSEP"; a negative ATP8B1/ABCB11/ABCB4 panel is not the end → exome or a current panel (lesson slide 3, 22). → publish at build.
- **FXR deficiency (Gomez-Ospina 2016, Nat Commun 7:10713 — qualitative claims VERIFIED abstract: "vitamin K-independent coagulopathy, low-to-normal… GGT… elevated… AFP and undetectable liver BSEP"; numbers TODO(verify) Table 1):** onset birth–6 wk; GGT ~40–60 U/L; vitamin K–independent coagulopathy in all 4 (INR 2.0 to 7.4), AFP up to 400,000 ng/mL; BSEP undetectable with normal ABCB11, MDR3 preserved; 2 transplanted, 2 died. → Red flag/pathway node: **low-GGT neonatal cholestasis + INR not corrected by parenteral vitamin K → treat as liver failure (PALF pathway, transplant center; GALD, HSV/enterovirus, galactosemia, tyrosinemia, HLH, sepsis first) and include NR1H4 in the exome**; AFP is supportive only — read it against neonatal norms (P1 had AFP 716 ng/mL), not a required criterion; IBAT inhibitor and diversion have no rationale (no BSEP). After LT: progressive graft steatosis (gut FXR absent).
- **TJP2 deficiency (Sambrotta 2014, Nat Genet 46:326 — abstract checked, numbers not in abstract → TODO(verify) full text):** 12 of 33 unexplained low-GGT children; onset < 3 mo; GGT normal after neonatal period; 9 transplanted, 2 stable with mild PH, 1 died at 13 mo. Later: relapsing forms (EASL lists among relapsing). Childhood HCC reported (Zhou 2015 Hepatology 62:1914; Vij 2017 Virchows Arch 471:679 — TODO(verify)) → survey like BSEP (US + AFP; **no validated interval** — "expert opinion"), image before listing.
- **MYO5B — VERIFIED (Matarazzo 2022, JPGN 74:e115; abstract, Europe PMC 35129155):** 6 of 32 children with cryptogenic intrahepatic cholestasis (exome); median onset 0.8 y, follow-up 4.2 y; GGT persistently normal; 2 with intestinal involvement; coarse MYO5B granules in hepatocytes on biopsy. Lesson extras (0 of 6 needed diversion/transplant; itch controlled on UDCA/rifampicin/cholestyramine) — TODO(verify) full text. Qiu 2017 (Hepatology 65:1655): biallelic null variants 11/38 MVID vs 0/13 isolated cholestasis — VERIFIED abstract. → Ask about neonatal watery diarrhea/PN (MVID changes the plan; LT can worsen MVID diarrhea — already in Inbox 05-01).
- **USP53 (Maddirevula 2019 Genet Med 21:1164; Alhebbi 2021 J Hum Genet 66:151 — TODO(verify)):** normal GGT, often mild/BRIC-like, rifampicin responses, **late sensorineural hearing loss** (found at 9 and 14 y in first family) → audiogram at diagnosis and repeat (interval: expert opinion). Front Genet 2025 review of 39 cases: spectrum BRIC → PFIC (abstract, Europe PMC 41356217).
- **Single-family genes (SEMA7A, LSR, WDR83OS; Pan 2021 EMBO Mol Med):** a variant in a one-family gene is a hypothesis → segregation, phenotype fit, keep looking (lesson slide 13). → pitfall.
- **BSEP stain trap → pitfall:** an abnormal BSEP stain ≠ BSEP deficiency when ABCB11 is normal (lesson slides 7, 19: other defects "change BSEP staining"): absent BSEP → think FXR (NR1H4) (Gomez-Ospina 2016); patchy/mislocalized BSEP → think MYO5B (Qiu 2017); a normal BSEP stain does not exclude MYO5B (Matarazzo 2022: BSEP normally expressed) — all abstracts VERIFIED.
- **Treatment by "what the defect leaves" (slide 16; case-level evidence, no trials):** FXR — UDCA/IBAT/diversion no role, early transplant; MYO5B, USP53 — medical therapy often enough (rifampicin); TJP2 — often transplant. EASL 2024: these rare types "outside the scope", tertiary care only (TODO(verify) wording, easl_2024_genetic_cholestasis). → Why/pearls; "Limited data" wording.
- **guideline_compare rows (slide 18; lesson verdicts):** broad NGS ALIGNED; gene-based naming DIFFERS; management of TJP2/FXR/MYO5B/USP53 GAP; HCC surveillance beyond BSEP GAP; IBAT inhibitors in rare types GAP.
- **Naming (slide 4):** write "biallelic TJP2 variants (TJP2 deficiency)", not "PFIC4"; numbering past 10 is inconsistent across catalogues (EASL 2024 uses gene names). → pearl + D-entry at build.
- **Cases (slides 2, 21):** newborn, GGT 45, INR 2.0 despite IV vitamin K, AFP > 100,000, BSEP absent, 3-gene panel negative → exome: homozygous NR1H4 nonsense, listed (illustrative); teen with recurrent low-GGT episodes + deafness → USP53, rifampicin trial; unsolved 2017 panel → exome/reanalysis.

### 05-06 · PFIC VI: BRIC and intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy (synced 2026-11-26)
- **Why (one line):** BRIC, PFIC and ICP are one spectrum of the same genes (ATP8B1, ABCB11, plus heterozygous ABCB4/TJP2/ABCC2 in ICP). Residual transporter function sets the baseline; infection, oestrogen/progesterone metabolites and drugs push it under the threshold (lesson slides 3–4; Klomp 2004, van Mil 2004, EASL 2024). The "≈ 40% threshold" diagram is a teaching schematic, not measured — do not publish the percentages.
- **Rule → pathway (older child/teen branch):** recurrent low-GGT cholestasis with symptom-free intervals → ask two questions: how much function is left (genetics: ATP8B1, ABCB11 and a broad cholestasis panel), and what was the trigger this time (infection, combined pill, pregnancy, drug). → publish at build.
- **BRIC phenotype — Luketic–Shiffman criteria (Luketic & Shiffman, Clin Liver Dis 2004;8:133–49 — TODO(verify) exact wording, criteria list taken from lesson slide 6):** ≥ 2 episodes of jaundice separated by symptom-free intervals of months to years; intense itch (often precedes jaundice by days to weeks); laboratory intrahepatic cholestasis with normal/low GGT and high bile acids; normal ducts on imaging; bland centrilobular cholestasis without fibrosis between attacks; no other cause. Attack: bilirubin may exceed 20 mg/dL, ALT normal or mildly raised, INR rises from vitamin K malabsorption; steatorrhea, weight loss.
- **BRIC1 vs BRIC2 (van Mil, Gastroenterology 2004;127:379–84 — TODO(verify) numbers by abstract):** ABCB11 variants in 11 patients from 8 of 20 families with normal ATP8B1; gallstones 7 of 11, pancreatitis 0 of 11. BRIC1 (ATP8B1) is systemic: pancreatitis, diarrhea, hearing loss. → pearl: gallstones point to ABCB11, pancreatitis/diarrhea to ATP8B1.
- **Continuum (van Ooteghem, J Hepatol 2002;36:439–43 — TODO(verify); contrast Tygstrup 1999, Faroe founder haplotype: no progression, attacks fade with age):** BRIC can convert to persistent progressive cholestasis; "benign" is a hindsight label → re-stage after every long attack (elastography, platelets; biopsy if not recovering — expert opinion). Letter wording: "episodic FIC1 or BSEP deficiency".
- **Treatment ladder for an attack (no trial; lesson slide 12 — expert practice; EASL 2024 per lesson: evidence lacking, rifampicin listed for pruritus):** (1) remove trigger — treat infection, stop combined pill/culprit drug; ultrasound once to exclude obstruction; (2) parenteral vitamin K if INR raised, check A, D, E, calories/MCT; (3) rifampicin first line, start at the first itch of a new attack, liver tests within 2–4 weeks, warn about drug interactions and contraceptive failure; (4) cholestyramine (separate from other drugs) or off-label IBAT inhibitor; UDCA adds little; (5) refractory → nasobiliary drainage; albumin dialysis/plasmapheresis as bridge. Adjuncts for itch only: sertraline, naltrexone, sedating antihistamine. No pediatric rifampicin dose in the lesson — dose only from a pediatric source at build (TODO(verify)).
- **Rifampicin evidence (Helgadottir, Scand J Gastroenterol 2023, doi 10.1080/00365521.2022.2143725 — TODO(verify) by abstract):** 5 adult men with BRIC1, 65 attacks in 12 years, 28 treated with rifampicin alone — relief in 28/28, no side effects attributed; observational, no controls. Safety: hepatitis in a minority of cholestatic patients, usually early; orange secretions; interactions.
- **Nasobiliary drainage:** Stapelbroek 2006 (Hepatology 43:51–3; 3 patients, itch gone within 24 h, next attack 8–12 mo in 2); Yakar 2016 (Clin Invest Med 39:164–72; 16 patients, 25 procedures, median 17 days, post-ERCP pancreatitis 1/16); Dądalski 2025 (Clin Exp Hepatol 11:228–31; 3 boys 14–17 y, pancreatitis 1/3) — TODO(verify) all three by abstract before any number is published. Needs pediatric ERCP; consent for pancreatitis.
- **IBAT inhibitors (Di Giorgio, Liver Int 2025;45:e70216 — TODO(verify)):** 6 patients with episodic biallelic ATP8B1 cholestasis, odevixibat 28–120 µg/kg/day off label; itch improved in all (4 marked, 2 partial), fast bile acid fall in 3; no preventive effect → use during an episode, then stop; diarrhea may worsen in FIC1 disease. Do not publish the dose range (case series, off label).
- **guideline_compare rows (slide 17):** treatment of BRIC attacks — GAP (no AASLD or NASPGHAN guidance; RCOG not addressed; EASL 2024: evidence lacking); genetic testing in ICP — EASL offers if bile acids ≥ 100, recurrent or early onset vs RCOG not routine vs North America not addressed (DIFFERS; adult/obstetric scope — only as a family-history pearl).
- **Traps (slide 18) → pitfalls:** first attack labelled viral hepatitis, the second missed for years; INR found prolonged only at a procedure; repeated imaging/ERCP for jaundice with normal ducts; no follow-up after a long attack that never fully resolved; non-fasting bile acids misread (fasting sample).
- **Case (slides 2, 20):** 16-year-old girl, third low-GGT attack (GGT 22, bilirubin 9 mg/dL at a previous one), combined pill 6 weeks ago + cold, itch before jaundice, bile acids 280 µmol/L; mother had ICP twice → BRIC: ultrasound once, vitamin K, rifampicin, sequence ATP8B1/ABCB11, non-oestrogen contraception (illustrative).
