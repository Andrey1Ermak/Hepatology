### 02-11 · Syndromic cholestasis (synced 2026-10-19)
- Low/normal GGT + conjugated hyperbilirubinemia narrows the list to ARC, SLOS, bile-acid synthesis defects and PFIC types (lesson slide 3).
- When GGT is low, look beyond the liver: contractures, Fanconi, ichthyosis, large platelets (ARC); 2–3 toe syndactyly, genital anomalies, microcephaly (SLOS); diarrhea, pancreatitis, deafness (PFIC1).
- **ARC (VPS33B ~75%, VIPAS39 most of the rest):** arthrogryposis 97%, normal GGT, renal Fanconi or nephrogenic DI, ichthyosis (Gissen 2006; Eastham 2001). **Do not biopsy** (liver or kidney): 7/16 biopsied children had life-threatening hemorrhage; platelet count normal in 9/11 severe bleeds (Gissen 2006). Diagnose by genome/panel, urine studies, platelet EM or flow cytometry.
- No liver guideline warns against biopsy in suspected ARC (NASPGHAN/ESPGHAN 2017, EASL 2024 silent) → guideline_compare row, verdict MATERIAL (changes tactics); the warning lives in case series.
- **SLOS (DHCR7):** send plasma 7-dehydrocholesterol by GC-MS; total cholesterol is normal in ~10% (GeneReviews, Nowaczyk). Draw sterols before cholesterol-containing feeds or transfusion. Treatment: dietary cholesterol (± bile acids in cholestasis); statins not recommended.
- Bile-acid level as splitter (matches §4.2 card 5): low GGT + normal/low serum bile acids → synthesis defect (urine bile acids by mass spectrometry; cholic acid works); low GGT + high bile acids → transport/trafficking defect.
- Zellweger spectrum (PEX; GGT variable): hypotonia, large fontanelle, seizures, renal cysts → VLCFA first.
- When building the card, also show panhypopituitarism (classic low-GGT cause in infants) and TJP2 / MYO5B / USP53 explicitly (reviewer note; verify sources then).
