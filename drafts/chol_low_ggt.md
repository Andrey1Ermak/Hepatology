### 02-11 · Syndromic cholestasis (synced 2026-10-19)
- Low/normal GGT + conjugated hyperbilirubinemia narrows the list to ARC, SLOS, bile-acid synthesis defects and PFIC types (lesson slide 3).
- When GGT is low, look beyond the liver: contractures, Fanconi, ichthyosis, large platelets (ARC); 2–3 toe syndactyly, genital anomalies, microcephaly (SLOS); diarrhea, pancreatitis, deafness (PFIC1).
- **ARC (VPS33B ~75%, VIPAS39 most of the rest):** arthrogryposis 97%, normal GGT, renal Fanconi or nephrogenic DI, ichthyosis (Gissen 2006; Eastham 2001). **Do not biopsy** (liver or kidney): 7/16 biopsied children had life-threatening hemorrhage; platelet count normal in 9/11 severe bleeds (Gissen 2006). Diagnose by genome/panel, urine studies, platelet EM or flow cytometry.
- No liver guideline warns against biopsy in suspected ARC (NASPGHAN/ESPGHAN 2017, EASL 2024 silent) → guideline_compare row, verdict MATERIAL (changes tactics); the warning lives in case series.
- **SLOS (DHCR7):** send plasma 7-dehydrocholesterol by GC-MS; total cholesterol is normal in ~10% (GeneReviews, Nowaczyk). Draw sterols before cholesterol-containing feeds or transfusion. Treatment: dietary cholesterol (± bile acids in cholestasis); statins not recommended.
- Bile-acid level as splitter (matches §4.2 card 5): low GGT + normal/low serum bile acids → synthesis defect (urine bile acids by mass spectrometry; cholic acid works); low GGT + high bile acids → transport/trafficking defect.
- Zellweger spectrum (PEX; GGT variable): hypotonia, large fontanelle, seizures, renal cysts → VLCFA first.
- When building the card, also show panhypopituitarism (classic low-GGT cause in infants) and TJP2 / MYO5B / USP53 explicitly (reviewer note; verify sources then).

### 02-12 · Genetics in the infant (synced 2026-10-20)
- GGT is the gene filter (slide 6, Wang NL 2016, n = 207, Shanghai): restricting ATP8B1/ABCB11 sequencing to low peak GGT raised yield 18.8% → 35.1% and missed no case; 0/96 above cut-off had PFIC1/2. Age cut-offs that captured all cases: < 70 U/L at 2–6 mo, < 60 U/L at 7–12 mo, < 50 U/L over 1 y. **Caveat:** single-center, pre-panel era → use GGT to read a panel or pick single genes, not to refuse broad testing. TODO(verify) cut-offs in Wang 2016 before publishing.
- Low/normal GGT genes: ATP8B1, ABCB11, TJP2, MYO5B, USP53; synthesis defects (HSD3B7, AKR1D1); ARC, SLOS.
- Biallelic ATP8B1/ABCB11/TJP2/USP53 (phased with parents) + low GGT before surgery → no Kasai; genotype guides IBAT inhibitor and biliary diversion (slide 13).
- **Pitfall (slide 17):** "two hits in ABCB11" without parental testing — both variants may sit on one allele → wrong diagnosis, wrong drug. Phase with parents; BSEP stain if biopsied.
- One heterozygous ABCB4/ABCB11 variant is a modifier at most, never the explanation: 77.5% of a Taiwanese cohort carried ≥ 1 variant (Chen 2019); in another comparison single variants in cholestasis genes were found in ~25% of patients vs 26% of controls (TODO(verify) source).
- Scenario (slide 19): 8-week-old, low GGT, itch, normal stools → atresia unlikely; ATP8B1/ABCB11/TJP2/USP53 panel, urine bile acids.

### 02-13 · Supportive care in the cholestatic infant (synced 2026-10-21)
- Urine bile acids by mass spectrometry **before** UDCA: synthesis defects (low GGT, low serum bile acids) are cured by cholic acid; UDCA blurs the profile (Fawaz 2017).
- UDCA cannot replace a missing transporter (FIC1, BSEP) or correct a synthesis defect; EASL 2024 rates efficacy low → not first line in PFIC1/2.
- IBAT inhibitors in PFIC (PEDFIC1 odevixibat PFIC1/2: positive itch 55% vs 30%, bile acid response 33% vs 0%, diarrhea 31% vs 10%; MARCH-PFIC maralixibat: BSEP cohort ItchRO −1.7 vs −0.6, diarrhea 57% vs 20%). Need residual secretion: severe BSEP deficiency with no functional protein is excluded from the maralixibat label. Bile acid responders are the patients in whom native-liver survival may improve. FDA labels (lesson, 2026): odevixibat PFIC ≥ 3 mo, maralixibat PFIC ≥ 12 mo — TODO(verify) at card build.
- **Biliary diversion by genotype (EASL 2024):** not routinely in FIC1 (ATP8B1); consider in BSEP with a responsive genotype — itch response 54% overall, 66% with E297G or D482G, 36% other missense, 0/3 with two truncating variants.
- Two truncating ABCB11 variants + refractory itch → IBAT inhibitor and diversion unlikely to work → plan transplant (lesson case, slide 21).
- Diarrhea on IBAT inhibitors worsens fat malabsorption → recheck fat-soluble vitamins after starting.
- Guideline compare candidates (slide 18): IBAT inhibitors in PFIC (FDA odevixibat ≥ 3 mo, maralixibat ≥ 12 mo vs EMA odevixibat ≥ 6 mo — DIFFERS; TODO(verify) current labels; the Alagille statement lives in chol_3_12m); biliary diversion (EASL genotype-guided vs no North American graded statement — GAP).

### 02-14 · Block 2 synthesis (synced 2026-10-22)
- **Endocrine mimic (case 3):** GGT is normal in about half of hypopituitary cholestatic infants → hypoglycemia + low-GGT cholestasis = cortisol + free T4 before (or alongside) the low-GGT genetic panel; normal TSH does not exclude central hypothyroidism. Hydrocortisone before levothyroxine. Belongs in Don't miss.
- Split by clue (slide 3): low GGT with intense itch → transporter defect (PFIC/BSEP); low GGT with low serum bile acids and **no itch** → bile acid synthesis defect → urine bile acids by mass spectrometry **before** UDCA (pearl 11, self-test).
