- **When to fractionate (slides 2, 4, 19):** formula-fed and jaundiced at 2 weeks; breastfed, jaundiced and well by 3 weeks (Fawaz 2017) / at 3–4 weeks (Kemper 2022); **pale stool or dark urine at any age → total + direct bilirubin the same day**. Breastfed and thriving at 3 weeks → total and direct whatever the stool color (self-test, slide 18).
- **Look up the newborn direct bilirubin** of every jaundiced infant: a value above the lab range is a signal, not a footnote (Harpavat 2025 AAP; Harpavat 2011 — atresia is abnormal from birth). Day 2 direct 0.5 with lab limit 0.3 → abnormal: repeat by 2 weeks, ask about stools.
- **Threshold:** direct/conjugated > 1.0 mg/dL is abnormal at any total (Fawaz 2017); the 20% rule is dropped — 79% of atresia had direct/total ≤ 0.2 on day 2 (Harpavat 2011). Card text must not use a ratio rule.

- **Stool color:** ask at every visit 2–4 weeks; look at the stool yourself; teach parents the card. A normal card is not reassurance in a jaundiced infant (pigmented stools early in atresia); a pale stool is a same-day referral (slide 7).

### 03-02 · Biliary atresia: pathogenesis (synced 2026-10-24)
- **Why the ratio fails (slide 4, Harpavat 2011):** mean direct bilirubin in the first days 1.4 vs 0.19 mg/dL in later-diagnosed atresia vs controls; most atresia infants had direct/total ≤ 0.2. Follow-up series: 35/35 atresia infants had a raised newborn direct bilirubin; 98.2% of others were normal (Harpavat, JPGN 2016;62:799–803; 8,936/9,102 non-atresia infants in range). Pitfall: "Normal ratio, so not cholestatic."
- **Newborn screening data for the universal-screen row (slide 5, Harpavat JAMA 2020;323:1141–50):** two-stage screen, 14 Texas hospitals, 124,385 newborns; stage 1 < 60 h above the site reference interval (conjugated > 0.2 mg/dL); stage 2 (2-week visit) higher than stage 1 or > 1 mg/dL; 119 positive at stage 2, 7 atresia (0.6/10,000), sensitivity 100% (95% CI 56–100; false negatives not fully ascertained — upper bound), specificity 99.9%, PPV 5.9%; mean Kasai age 56 → 36 d. Single-center replication (Rabbani, J Med Screen 2025;32:61–6, n = 3,861): 1.3% flagged initially, net false-positive rate 0.3%. Lesson verdict vs Fawaz 2017: MATERIAL (guideline predates screening evidence) — consistent with the 02-14 universal-screen row (there rated DIFFERS across AAP 2025/UK NSC); keep one row, decide the verdict at card build.
- **Case (slide 20):** newborn day 2, total bilirubin 9 mg/dL, direct 1.1 mg/dL (ratio 0.12) → repeat fractionated bilirubin by 2 weeks; the ratio does not reassure; check stool color.
- Pitfall: a normal bilirubin at birth lowers the odds of atresia but does not remove the need to look at the stools.

### 03-04 · Biliary atresia: timing of the Kasai (synced 2026-10-26)
- **AAP 2025 (Harpavat, clinical report) — primary-care strategy at 2–4 weeks (chapter part 8):** test direct bilirubin if jaundiced, if **pale stools at any time**, or if a **previous direct bilirubin was high**; direct ≥ 1 mg/dL → urgent consultation; schedule the well-child visit at 2 weeks. Fawaz 2017: fractionate at 2 weeks; breastfed and well may be reviewed at 3 weeks. NICE: conjugated > 25 µmol/L (≈ 1.5 mg/dL) at the prolonged-jaundice check. Lesson rates "when to test" and "referral threshold" ALIGNED; this draft (02-14) records "abnormal value" as DIFFERS — card 1 decides one verdict (note "≥ 1" AAP vs "> 1.0" Fawaz wording). **Also unresolved:** breastfed timing — 02-14 rated MATERIAL with AAP 2022 (Kemper) 3–4 weeks and NICE 14 days, while AAP 2025 (Harpavat) tests at 2–4 weeks with a 2-week visit; the lesson's "when to test: ALIGNED" conflicts with that — card 1 decides, records in DECISIONS.
- **Pitfalls (slide 18):** "breastfed — recheck in two weeks" (breast-milk jaundice is unconjugated; fractionate at the 2-week visit); "stools look yellow" (look yourself, pale can be intermittent); "total bilirubin is falling" (physiological fall hides a rising direct fraction; a prior high direct value counts even if the next looks better).
- **Screening facts for Why/guideline_compare (slides 11–12):** Texas two-stage screen (Harpavat 2020): 7/7 found, mean Kasai 56 → 36 d, specificity 99.9%, PPV 5.9% (~16 false positives per true case). Pooled: stool card sens 79.6% / spec 99.9% (7 studies, 996,262); direct bilirubin sens 100% / spec 98.8% (5 studies, 662,141) — Hari Gopal 2024 (PLOS ONE 2024;19:e0307837) — supersedes the 02-14 attribution "Gu 2020" for the 79.6% figure (same 7 studies, 996,262 infants; verify at build, record in DECISIONS). Taiwan card (Lien 2011, Hepatology 2011;53:202–8): Kasai < 60 d 49.4% → 65.7%; 5-y survival 55.7% → 89.3%. UK NSC 2026: neither screen recommended (TODO(verify)). AAP wording to keep distinct: "measure direct/conjugated bilirubin whenever bilirubin is drawn" (02-14) vs "universal screening is a future goal, not yet advised" (03-04) — check against the full AAP 2025 report.
- **Cases (slide 20):** 2-week visit, breastfed, mildly jaundiced, stools "normal" per parents → fractionated bilirubin today, show the stool card, do not defer to 4 weeks; newborn screen direct above reference and higher at 2 weeks → treat as atresia until excluded, refer the same day (→ chol_0_3m).
