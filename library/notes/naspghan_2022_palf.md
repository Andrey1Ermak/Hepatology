## Answers to what was needed

1. **Vitamin K in PALF — route and dose by age:**
   - Text (p. 145): give an **age- and weight-appropriate dose from 1 mg in infants to 10 mg in older adolescents**. **Oral vitamin K should not be used** to correct the INR in PALF. **Subcutaneous** route should be used to ensure administration; **IM and IV have also been used but carry a Black Box warning** for rare serious reactions.
   - **Internal inconsistency:** Table 5 (p. 144) says "Vitamin K repletion — **consider intravenous route** to assure administration". The text prefers subcutaneous; the table suggests IV.
   - No mg/kg dose, no number of doses, no timing of the recheck. Coagulopathy from vitamin K deficiency "should rapidly correct" after repletion.
2. **Plasma / FFP before procedures:** do **not** try to "correct" a persisting coagulopathy with FFP or other pro-coagulants; plasma improves labs transiently, has **no proven prophylactic benefit**, adds volume (p. 145). Table 5: FFP and/or platelets **for active bleeding or an invasive procedure**; avoid FFP/platelets just to correct the INR or platelet count without bleeding (TRALI, fluid overload). Text: **"most centers"** use plasma and/or platelets before invasive procedures (liver biopsy, ICP monitor) or for life-threatening bleeding — a description of practice, not a threshold. Cryoprecipitate for fibrinogen **< 100 mg/dL**. Recombinant factor VIIa has been used before ICP monitor placement (expensive, thrombosis risk). TEG often normal (balanced deficits); promising but needs study.
3. **PALF definition and INR thresholds (Table 1, PALFSG entry criteria, all three required):** acute onset without known chronic liver disease; biochemical evidence of severe liver injury; **coagulopathy not corrected by vitamin K**: **PT ≥ 15 s or INR ≥ 1.5 with hepatic encephalopathy**, or **PT ≥ 20 s or INR ≥ 2.0 with or without encephalopathy** (the table prints the second INR as "> 2"; PT uses ≥). The criteria were designed as **study entry criteria**, not a definitive diagnosis, but are now widely used clinically. PALF can be an acute presentation of chronic disease (Wilson, AIH) (p. 139).
4. **Workup tables (Table 4, p. 142):** monitoring frequency by HE grade (0–I, II, III–IV) — neuro checks from q2h down to q30 min; glucose checks q4h → more often; liver function/injury tests twice daily or more. **Age-based etiology testing** in two columns: **< 3 months** and **3 months–18 years** — infection (blood culture, HSV PCR, enterovirus PCR, adenovirus, HHV-6, parvovirus, influenza), GALD screen (ferritin), galactosemia/tyrosinemia (confirm newborn screen; urine succinylacetone), metabolic (lactate/pyruvate, acylcarnitines, amino acids, ammonia), maternal HBsAg in newborns, HLH (sIL-2R, ferritin, triglycerides), Doppler ultrasound; in older children HAV IgM, HBsAg, EBV, ANA/SMA/LKM, IgG, acetaminophen level, ceruloplasmin and 24-h urine copper (Wilson "unlikely needed < 1 y"; enterovirus "unlikely needed > 3 y").

## Other facts useful for cards

- palf — fluids: ~**90% of maintenance** if no resuscitation needed; D10 ½NS + 15 mEq K/L; avoid fixed-electrolyte fluids (Lactated Ringer's); **glucose 90–120 mg/dL**, glucose infusion up to **10–15 mg/kg/min** (often > 20% dextrose centrally); **phosphate > 3 mg/dL**; Na 2–3 mEq/kg/day; treat hyponatremia if symptomatic or Na < 120; Na 145–155 can lower ICP temporarily (pp. 141, 143).
- palf — ammonia: **< 75 µmol/L** rarely intracranial hypertension; **> 100** on admission independent risk for high-grade HE; **> 200** strongly linked to herniation; venous sample on ice acceptable (p. 141, 144). Lactulose 0.5 mL/kg per dose (max 30 mL), 2–4 stools/day; rifaximin sparse pediatric data; protein reduction suggested (Table 5).
- palf — ICP monitoring considered with grade III–IV coma, ventilation, EEG slowing, rising ammonia, CT edema; goals ICP < 20 mmHg, CPP > 50 (< 4 y), > 55 (4–10 y), > 60 mmHg (> 10 y); mannitol 0.5–1 g/kg (no added benefit if osm > 320); hypertonic saline to Na 145–155 (Table 5).
- palf — infection: cultures and broad-spectrum antibiotics if clinical or biochemical change (Table 5).
- palf — liver biopsy can be done safely, especially **transjugular**; helpful in indeterminate PALF (immune patterns) (p. 141).
- palf — etiology: no cause in ~30–50%; indeterminate 43% overall in PALFSG (1,144 children, 1999–2014), falling from 48% to 30.8% after standardized order sets (pp. 146, 148). Metabolic 28–36%. Autoantibodies positive in 28% — non-specific; steroids did not improve survival overall in autoantibody-positive children, but did in known AIH (p. 147). Non-APAP DILI ~3%, mostly > 10 y; valproate failure with mitochondrial disease = contraindication to LT (p. 148).
- palf — acetaminophen: NAC if level > 10 µg/mL or transaminases raised; NAC **not beneficial in non-APAP PALF** — reserve for confirmed/suspected APAP; LT needed in ~5% (p. 148).
- palf — GALD: most common cause of neonatal ALF; 70–90% preterm; ferritin > 1000; treatment **exchange transfusion + high-dose IVIG**; IVIG in later pregnancies (p. 147).
- palf — Wilson ALF: KF rings in about half; liver copper > 250 µg/g dry weight gold standard (p. 149).
- palf — prognostic scores (King's, LIU) not validated when death and LT are separated; no satisfactory tool (p. 146). PALF = up to 10.3% of pediatric LT.
- palf — plasma exchange: benefit shown in adults (high-volume); in children only as a bridge (p. 145).

## Conflicts with what the app says now

- **inr_prolonged "Vitamin K IV or subcutaneous, 1–10 mg — not oral":** consistent with the 1–10 mg range and "not oral". But NASPGHAN 2022 text **prefers subcutaneous** and flags **IV/IM Black Box warnings**; only Table 5 says "consider IV". The red-flag lines "IV vitamin K at once" (active/intracranial bleeding) are not covered by this paper (it does not discuss VKDB emergencies) — they need their own source, not naspghan_2022_palf.
- **inr_prolonged guideline_compare "Plasma or platelets only for active bleeding or an invasive procedure, not to improve the number":** **matches** Table 5. The card's note "the PALF paper still allows plasma before an invasive procedure" is accurate — the paper describes it as what most centers do.
- **inr_prolonged red flag "INR not corrected by vitamin K — ≥ 1.5 with encephalopathy or ≥ 2.0 without":** matches the PALFSG criteria in substance; the table itself prints "INR > 2" for the second limb (PT ≥ 20 s). The 2014 AASLD/AST/NASPGHAN guideline wording is "2.0 and higher" — the app's ≥ 2.0 is defensible.
- **"The PALF INR is the INR after vitamin K"** — confirmed (coagulopathy not corrected by vitamin K is a required criterion).
