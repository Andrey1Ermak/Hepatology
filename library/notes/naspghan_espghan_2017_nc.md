# naspghan_espghan_2017_nc — full-text note

- **Source:** Fawaz R, Baumann U, Ekong U, et al. Guideline for the Evaluation of Cholestatic Jaundice in Infants: Joint Recommendations of NASPGHAN and ESPGHAN. JPGN 2017;64:154-168, doi:10.1097/MPG.0000000000001334
- **Read:** 2026-11-21 from the full text (15 PDF pages = journal pp. 154–168). Recommendations 1–10, Tables 2–4, sections on imaging, biopsy, IOC, select genetic/metabolic disorders.
- **Status of document:** joint NASPGHAN/ESPGHAN clinical practice guideline; GRADE-style class (1 = strong, 2 = weak) + level (A–C), AASLD method. Literature search 2002–2015. **No algorithm figure** in the main text (Figures 1–2 are histology); the stepwise approach is Table 4 (Tier 1 / Tier 2).

## Answers to what was needed

1. **Genetic testing — wording and strength.** There is **no numbered recommendation** on genetics. Genetics appears in **Table 4, Tier 2** (p. 155 of PDF p. 8): "in discussion with pediatric gastroenterologist/hepatologist", with a **low threshold for gene panels or exome sequencing** (plus e.g. CFTR testing as appropriate); "Metabolic/Genetic — consider when to involve, especially when there is consideration for gene panels or whole exome sequencing". Abstract: consider broad next-generation sequencing "in the proper clinical context". → "Low threshold for panel/exome, no class" (D-2026-11-09-3) is **confirmed**.
2. **Expedite evaluation for biliary atresia.** Text, not a numbered recommendation (p. 10 of PDF): evaluation to rule out BA should be **expedited, especially when the infant is older than 6 weeks**; the younger the age at diagnosis, the more likely HPE succeeds. Background: HPE before 60 days → ~70% establish bile flow; after 90 days < 25% (p. 5). US mean age at HPE 61 days, 44% after 60 days.
   - **"Red flags" that mandate BA evaluation** (p. 8): acholic stools; high-GGT cholestasis without alternative cause; abnormal or absent gallbladder on ultrasound. Mimics (A1AT deficiency, CF, Alagille) should be excluded early.
3. **Alagille before cholangiography** (p. 10): practitioners are encouraged to look for associated features of Alagille (by liver biopsy or characteristic facies) **before** intraoperative cholangiogram; in up to 20% of cases the cholangiogram can mislead (hypoplastic biliary tree in Alagille or CF). Not a numbered recommendation. → confirmed.
   - Alagille: 1 in 30,000; JAG1 ~95%, NOTCH2 ~5%; GGT often disproportionately high, sometimes up to 20× normal; ALT and bile acids usually raised.
4. **Timing of fractionation:**
   - **Rec 1 (1A):** any **formula-fed** infant jaundiced after **2 weeks** → total and conjugated (direct) bilirubin. Depending on local practice, **breastfed** infants who otherwise appear well **may be followed clinically until 3 weeks**, then tested if still icteric.
   - Text (p. 5): the most direct option is testing at the 2-week visit (or at any age if concerned); a breastfed 2-week-old with normal exam and no dark urine/acholic stool can be re-seen in 1 week; if jaundice persists at 3 weeks → labs.
   - **Rec 2 (1A):** always fractionate serum bilirubin.
   - **Rec 3 (1A):** direct/conjugated **> 1.0 mg/dL (17 µmol/L)** is pathological and warrants evaluation — **regardless of total bilirubin** (p. 5: the 20%-of-total rule was dropped as too complex).
5. **Algorithm (figure):** none in the main article — use Table 4 instead:
   - **Tier 1** (after cholestasis confirmed): CBC + differential, INR, AST, ALT, ALP, GGT, total and direct bilirubin, (also glucose, urine studies per table), verify newborn screen (galactosemia, hypothyroidism), **fasting ultrasound**.
   - **Tier 2** (with pediatric GI/hepatologist): TSH/T4, serum bile acids, cortisol, then targeted tests by suspected cause (A1AT level and phenotype, sweat test/CFTR, infection studies, metabolic studies, echo for Alagille, genetics, HBS, biopsy, cholangiogram).

## Other recommendations (for guideline_compare rows)

- **Rec 4 (1A):** thorough exam; hepatomegaly, splenomegaly and ill appearance need special consideration.
- **Rec 5 (1A):** look at the stool directly.
- **Rec 6 (1A):** ultrasound excludes choledochal cyst / stones; may show absent or abnormal gallbladder or other features suggestive (not diagnostic) of BA. A normal ultrasound does **not** rule out nonsyndromic BA (p. 8). Choledochal cyst in a cholestatic neonate → still evaluate for BA (cystic type 1 BA).
- **Rec 7 (1B):** HBS not a stand-alone test; demonstrated bile flow may help exclude BA. Phenobarbital premedication for 5 days often delays diagnosis.
- **Rec 8:** MRCP, ERCP, PTCC — limited role (no grade printed). ERCP sensitivity 86–100%, specificity 87–94% in series.
- **Rec 9 (1B):** liver biopsy read by an experienced pediatric pathologist (ductular proliferation, bile plugs, fibrosis) is the **most supportive test**; it also identifies non-BA causes.
- **Rec 10 (1A):** intraoperative cholangiogram + histology of the duct remnant = gold standard.
- Epidemiology: BA 25–40% of infant cholestasis; individually rare genetic disorders ~25%; jaundice at 2 weeks in 2.4–15% of newborns; PN cholestasis in up to one-fifth of neonates on PN > 2 weeks; BA and genetic causes are as common in preterm as in term infants (p. 2).
- MMP-7 is **not addressed** (2017).
