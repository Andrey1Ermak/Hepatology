## Answers to what was needed

1. **First imaging for PVT — Doppler?** Yes, in the text: **Doppler ultrasound is usually the first imaging study**; Doppler is essential because a visible echogenic thrombus may be absent; confirm by contrast CT and/or MRI (p. 409). Recommendations (recent PVT without cirrhosis):
   - **Ultrasound with or without contrast is as good as contrast CT or MRI to diagnose recent PVT** (LoE 5, strong).
   - **Contrast CT or MRI should be used to characterise extension** (vessels, degree of occlusion) — better than US (LoE 5, strong).
   - **Contrast CT** to look for **intestinal ischaemia** (LoE 5, strong).
   - CT/MRI more sensitive than Doppler but can give false positives with poorly timed contrast phase.
   - Standardised description (site, extent, % occlusion of main PV lumen, recent < 6 months / chronic > 6 months, cavernoma, response categories) is required (LoE 5, strong; Table 4).
2. **Anticoagulation:**
   - **Recent PVT without cirrhosis:** start **as soon as possible** (less intestinal ischaemia, more recanalisation) (LoE 4, strong); continue **at least 6 months** (LoE 4, strong). Send thrombophilia work-up before starting if possible, but do not delay. Thrombolysis/endovascular only for ischaemia not improving, in expert centres (LoE 5, strong). Recanalisation of portal / splenic / SMV 39% / 80% / 73% on anticoagulation; 30% intestinal infarction without anticoagulation (p. 409–410).
   - **Chronic PVT/cavernoma without cirrhosis:** long-term anticoagulation if a **major permanent prothrombotic factor** (active cancer, MPN, homozygous FII/FV, antiphospholipid syndrome, PNH, Behçet) (LoE 1, strong); otherwise "can be considered", guided by extent, factor VIII and D-dimer (LoE 2, weak). DOACs may be preferred over VKAs except double/triple-positive APS (Fig. 2). Rivaroxaban 15 mg RCT (Plessier): recurrence 0 vs 19.7/100 person-years.
   - **PVT with cirrhosis:** DOACs usable in Child-Pugh A/B, not preferred over VKA/LMWH; **not** in Child-Pugh C (LoE 3, weak).
3. **Pediatric caveats:**
   - **Recent PVT in children:** adult management may be applied **except in neonates and premature babies** (LoE 4, weak, strong consensus). PVT usually starts in the **neonatal period**, most often the **left portal vein**, mostly non-occlusive and often resolving in days–weeks; > 30% may develop portal hypertension, no clear predictors. **Premature babies: expectant management preferred** (intracranial bleeding risk). Systematic review (children 12 months–18 years): recanalisation ~33% with anticoagulation, 41% complete with radiological management (p. 412).
   - **Chronic PVT/cavernoma in children:** **restore portal flow — Meso-Rex bypass or portal vein recanalisation — regardless of symptoms** (LoE 4, strong); if not feasible, watchful management of portal hypertension complications (LoE 4, strong); porto-systemic surgical shunt (distal spleno-renal or mesocaval) may delay or avoid LT (LoE 4, weak) (p. 412). Assess complications as in cirrhosis.
   - **Work-up of risk factors:** same in children as adults, using **age-specific cut-offs for protein C, protein S and antithrombin**; repeat in adolescence if suspicion persists (LoE 4, strong) (p. 403). Pediatric PVT risk factors: umbilical vein catheter, omphalitis, surgery, abdominal infection, neuroblastoma/Wilms/hepatoblastoma/lymphoma/germ cell tumours, neonatal sepsis, chemotherapy, JAK2V617F (more in adolescents), thrombophilia; < 10% unprovoked in children.
   - BCS in children: endovascular first-line + post-procedure anticoagulation; LT if not possible or failed (LoE 5, weak).
   - SOS in children: **defibrotide as soon as SOS is suspected, regardless of severity** (LoE 4, strong); pediatric mortality 80%; infants < 12 months especially vulnerable (PDF p. 32).
   - PSVD, sinusoidal dilatation/peliosis, splanchnic aneurysms: manage children as adults (LoE 4). Aneurysms over-represented in Alagille, NF1, Wiskott-Aldrich.
4. **Replaces EASL 2016:** yes — presented as the update of the 2016 CPG (summary, p. 399).

## Other facts useful for cards

- ref_imaging / splenomegaly_ph: liver stiffness **< 10 kPa** very rarely means cirrhosis; HVPG < 10 mmHg; biopsy if persistently abnormal liver tests, atypical morphology, or LSM > 10 kPa (adult data) (p. 408). Changes typical of chronic PVT without cirrhosis: left-lobe atrophy, caudate and segment IV hypertrophy, smooth surface. PVT occurs in ~30% of PSVD.
- splenomegaly_ph: portal cavernoma cholangiopathy on MRCP in > 80% of portal vein obstruction, symptomatic in ~20%; treat only symptomatic (UDCA, stenting, recanalisation) (LoE 4) (p. 411).
- Adults with chronic PVT: first variceal bleeding despite primary prophylaxis ~33% at 3 years; rebleeding ~50% at 5 years on secondary prophylaxis (p. 411).
- Chronic PVT long-term survival > 70% at 5 years.
