# aap_2025_ba_primary_care — full-text note

- **Source:** Harpavat S, Aucott SW, Karpen SJ, Shneider BL, Wang K; AAP Section on Gastroenterology, Hepatology, and Nutrition; Committee on Fetus and Newborn; Section on Surgery. Guidance for the Primary Care Provider in Identifying Infants With Biliary Atresia by 2–4 Weeks of Life: Clinical Report. Pediatrics 2025;155(3):e2024070077, doi:10.1542/peds.2024-070077
- **Read:** 2026-11-24 from the full text (7 pages + references). Strategy: Figure 1 and pp. 2–4; Table 1 p. 4; Appendix pp. 5–6.
- **Status of document:** AAP **Clinical Report** (not a policy statement, no graded recommendations, no evidence levels). States it is not a standard of care; expires 5 years after publication unless reaffirmed (p. 7). Expert consensus built on cited studies.

## Answers to what was needed

1. **≥ 1 or > 1 mg/dL?** — **≥ 1 mg/dL** (inclusive). A direct or conjugated value **≥ 1 mg/dL** drawn at 2–4 weeks warrants **urgent consultation with a pediatric gastroenterologist** (p. 3). The GI may ask for a recheck, more tests, an urgent outpatient visit (**ideally seen within 5 days**) or send the infant to the ED for admission (p. 4). The report advises **against ordering liver enzymes or abdominal ultrasound before consulting GI** — normal results can falsely reassure and delay referral (p. 4).
2. **"Any value above the lab range in a newborn — repeat" (by when):**
   - Before 2 weeks of life, "high" = above the **laboratory-derived** reference range, **even by 0.1 mg/dL**; do **not** use a fixed cut-off or a direct/total ratio before 2 weeks (Fig. 1 footnote *, p. 3; Appendix p. 6).
   - A high **initial** value warrants **considering repeat testing** — the repeat is done at the **"By 1 month" well-child visit, between 2 and 4 weeks** (Step 3, p. 3). The report itself prefers the visit **at 2 weeks** so that Kasai can happen before 30 days (p. 4).
   - Newborn unit / 3–5-day visit: consider consulting GI **earlier** if the first value is already **≥ 1 mg/dL and subsequent values are rising** (Table 1, p. 4).
   - After 2 weeks the fixed 1 mg/dL cut-off applies, because by then levels in BA have risen enough (Appendix, p. 6).
3. **Exceptions — "< 1 and falling":** at the 2–4-week visit a blood draw **may not be needed** if (a) **any** prior direct/conjugated value was within the lab-derived range, or (b) all prior values were abnormal but **both** ≤ the initial value **and** < 1 mg/dL (Fig. 1 footnote **, p. 3). Rationale: in BA levels are high from birth and rise over the first weeks before plateauing.
   - **New point:** a value drawn at 2–4 weeks that is **< 1 mg/dL but above the reference interval** does **not** need urgent GI consultation; these infants are unlikely to have BA; monitor clinically, no further testing unless jaundice or pale stools develop (p. 4).
4. **Kasai target age:** no formal target recommendation. Background: "ideally" treated **before 30 to 45 days** of life for best outcomes; US average is **after 60 days** (p. 1). The 2-week visit timing "will help … ensure" Kasai **before 30 days of life** (p. 4). → Usable as "AAP 2025: ideally before 30–45 days" (background statement, not a graded target).

## Other facts useful for cards

- neo_jaundice / chol_0_3m — the 3-step strategy at 2–4 weeks: (1) eyes (or skin) jaundiced **to any degree beyond 2 weeks**; (2) stools pale, gray or white **at any time** (by inspection or caregiver report); (3) prior direct/conjugated results — was the initial value above the lab range? Any "yes" → consider direct/conjugated bilirubin (Fig. 1, pp. 2–3).
- neo_jaundice: emphasize **eye** color, not just skin, to avoid missed cases in infants with more melanin; one study found jaundice recognized equally in Black and white infants (pp. 2, 5).
- neo_jaundice: stool color — ~**77%** of infants with BA have pale/gray/white stools at 30 days, **84%** at 45 days, **97%** at 60 days (p. 2). Ways to check: ask parents, inspect at visit, pictures, stool color card, phone app (PopòApp) (p. 3).
- neo_jaundice: **34%** of healthy predominantly breastfed infants still looked jaundiced at 3 weeks (p. 4) — earlier visit means more testing.
- neo_jaundice / ref_norms: transcutaneous bilirubin and total bilirubin alone say nothing about direct/conjugated (p. 3).
- ref_norms: lab reference ranges for direct/conjugated bilirubin varied from **0.0–0.2 to 0.0–0.7 mg/dL** across hospitals in one multicenter study; borrowed (not derived) ranges can mislabel a high value as normal (p. 3). Labs should derive ranges from ~**120 newborns**, middle 95% (2.5th–97.5th centile) (Appendix p. 6).
- ref_norms / unconj_bili: direct assay = conjugated + delta + a little unconjugated, so it always reads slightly higher than conjugated; some labs report direct as "conjugated" (Appendix Table 1, p. 6).
- unconj_bili / neo_jaundice: ratio rule — direct/total ≥ 0.2 before 48 h was present in only **21%** of newborns later diagnosed with BA (Appendix p. 6). Ratios may be more useful later.
- chol_0_3m (epidemiology): BA 1 in 8,000–18,000 births; 60% of liver transplants under 1 year and 30% of all pediatric liver transplants (p. 1).
- chol_0_3m (screening, Future directions): two universal options — stool color card programs (Japan, Taiwan, Canadian provinces, Switzerland) and newborn direct/conjugated bilirubin screening before discharge (parts of the US); both linked to earlier Kasai. Universal screening is a **future goal**, not a current AAP recommendation (p. 5). Hispanic and non-Hispanic Black infants are referred later (p. 5).
- Newborn unit: order direct/conjugated bilirubin from the **same sample** as any total bilirubin (p. 5).

## Conflicts with what the app says now

- **c_conj_bili / neo_jaundice / unconj_bili (D-2026-11-11-2, D-2026-11-12-2):** the app turns a value **above lab range but < 1.0 mg/dL at ≥ 2 weeks** into a red "discuss with hepatology today (expert opinion)". AAP 2025 says the opposite for a value drawn **at 2–4 weeks**: **urgent GI consultation is not needed**, BA is unlikely, monitor clinically unless jaundice or pale stools appear (p. 4). The app's rule needs review — at least the label cannot claim support from AAP 2025. (Under 2 weeks the app's yellow "repeat at the 2-week visit" **matches** AAP.)
- **c_conj_bili:** the inclusive **≥ 1.0 mg/dL** threshold is confirmed (p. 3). AAP 2025 can now be cited in the calculator UI.
- **neo_jaundice / unconj_bili / ref_norms:** "≥ 1.0 mg/dL abnormal at any total" — AAP 2025 applies the fixed cut-off **only from 2 weeks**; before 2 weeks it uses the lab range. Not a contradiction of the app's "call at ≥ 1.0" in the first days (stricter), but AAP's own early-call rule is "≥ 1 **and rising**" (Table 1).
- **c_kasai_clock (D-2026-11-11-3):** AAP gives "ideally before 30–45 days" as background and "before 30 days" as the reason for a 2-week visit — not a graded target. The app's gradient framing (no "window closed") is consistent; "AAP 2025: Kasai before 30 days" as a formal target would overstate it.
- **neo_jaundice:** "do not order liver enzymes or ultrasound before calling GI" (p. 4) — check that the PCP-facing pathway does not tell PCPs to get an ultrasound first.
