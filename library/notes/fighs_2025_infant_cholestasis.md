## Answers to what was needed (positions for chol_0_3m guideline_compare)

1. **Target Kasai age (number of days):** no recommendation of its own. BA section states a recent meta-analysis confirmed the importance of diagnosis and surgery **before 30 days of life** for native-liver survival at 5, 10 and 20 years (p. 1372). Also: ESPGHAN survey — mean referral 55 days, Kasai 61 days (p. 1362). → background fact, not a target.
2. **Direct/conjugated threshold and the 20% rule:** cholestasis defined as direct/conjugated **> 1.0 mg/dL (17 µmol/L) with elevated total** (per NASPGHAN/ESPGHAN); "What is known" box says all neonates with direct **≥ 1 mg/dL** need evaluation (p. 1361–1362; Table 2 again "> 1 mg/dL"). **20% rule:** widely used but may confuse; **use only when the direct/conjugated level is equivocal** (p. 1361). Conjugated assay preferred when available; caution with diazo direct > 17 µmol/L cut-off → false positives with high unconjugated bilirubin (p. 1365). Rare cholestasis without raised direct bilirubin — look at AST/ALT/GGT and bile acids (normal < 10 µmol/L) (p. 1361).
3. **MMP-7:** listed only as an **"emerging diagnostic modality"** in Tier 2 (Table 2) — significantly higher in BA; **no cut-off**; cut-offs depend on modality and purpose; needs **prospective use and validation** before wider application (pp. 1367–1368). Not recommended as a standard test.
4. **HIDA:** not required. Scintigraphy is listed (with MRCP, ERCP, PTCC) as Tier 2 imaging with **variable sensitivity/specificity**; not universally adopted because of variable PPV for BA (p. 1368). No statement that HIDA is needed before cholangiography.
5. **Biopsy before cholangiography:** no explicit sequence. Percutaneous biopsy is a Tier 2 consultation option ("consider"); it "remains indispensable" for pathogenesis, severity and specific diagnoses; pathologists detect obstruction in clinically confirmed BA with accuracy 79–98% (PPV 91%); mimics: Alagille, A1AT, CF, PN cholestasis, some PFIC — especially with small samples (p. 1368). **IOC is the gold standard**; a concomitant liver biopsy is recommended at IOC; IOC must check patency both proximal and distal to the gallbladder. If genetically confirmed, histology is optional (p. 1368). Multiple modalities should be used **promptly** to decide on IOC; each center should build its own algorithm (p. 1369).
6. **Genetics (when, which test):** genetic causes ~25–50% of neonatal cholestasis; after BA is excluded, NGS gives a diagnosis in 10–30% (p. 1369). Exclusion of BA and genetic testing can run **concurrently or sequentially** (p. 1369). Choice of panel (60+ genes) / WES (singleton or trio) / WGS by indication, severity, urgency, resources; trio valuable. **Resource-limited priority:** low GGT, low glucose, abnormal metabolic profile, consanguinity or multiple affected family members; also progressive disease, cirrhosis or liver failure of unknown cause, and LT candidates once BA is excluded (p. 1371 box). Reanalysis/repeat testing if negative and disease progresses (p. 1370). Heterozygous ATP8B1/ABCB11/ABCB4 variants are linked to transient neonatal cholestasis — interpretation uncertain (p. 1370).
7. **Universal screening:** "What is new" box: universal screening with **stool color cards or direct bilirubin** testing **should be implemented based on local expertise and resources** (p. 1362). Both shown effective for earlier diagnosis/KPE; need health-authority commitment (pp. 1363–1365). Describes the Texas two-stage protocol: stage 1 within 60 h (site ULN = 97.5th centile for direct; 0.2 mg/dL for conjugated); stage 2 at 2 weeks negative if ≤ stage 1 and ≤ 1 mg/dL (p. 1363).
   - **Targeted testing (p. 1365):** fractionated bilirubin is mandatory for symptomatic or screen-positive infants, and for **all infants with jaundice beyond 2 weeks regardless of feeding**, pale/acholic stools at any age, or enlarged liver or spleen.

## Other facts useful for cards

- chol_0_3m — **Tier 1 (mandatory):** total + direct bilirubin, AST/ALT, GGT, albumin, glucose, CBC, coagulation, blood cultures, urine microscopy and culture, urine non-glucose reducing substances, TSH/T4 and cortisol, **fasting** abdominal ultrasound (Table 2). Tier 2 by suspicion: CMV and other serology, sweat chloride/IRT/CFTR, A1AT phenotype/genotype, serum bile acids (low in bile acid synthesis defects), urine bile acids by mass spectrometry, metabolic panel (ammonia, lactate, TMS, amino acids, succinylacetone, acylcarnitines, CK), ferritin (GALD), AFP, MRCP/HIDA/ERCP/PTCC, chest and spine X-ray, elastography, MMP-7, eye exam, biopsy, geneticist.
- chol_0_3m / ref_imaging — **ultrasound:** gallbladder after **3–4 h fasting** (longer fasting needs IV fluids to avoid hypoglycemia); BA signs: absent or small "ghost" gallbladder **< 1.5–2 cm**, abnormal wall/shape, no contraction after feed, triangular cord, enlarged hepatic artery; highly specific, moderately sensitive — a normal scan does not exclude BA (Table 2 footnote c; p. 1368).
- chol_0_3m — **GGT > ~200–300 U/L suggests BA**, though lower values occur (Table 2 footnote a). Low GGT → PFIC (except MDR3), bile acid synthesis defects, ARC.
- PTCC can show a patent tree in infants with a normal gallbladder and avoid an unnecessary IOC (p. 1368).
- Red flags box (p. 1369): jaundice beyond 2 weeks, dark urine, pale stools, abnormal clotting, poor growth, vomiting/lethargy, neurological signs.
- Table 1 (history/exam clues): maternal intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy → maternal heterozygous ABCB4/ABCB11; diarrhea → PFIC1, MYO5B, mitochondrial, galactosemia, HFI; delayed meconium → CF; cataracts → rubella, galactosemia; nystagmus/optic hypoplasia → septo-optic dysplasia; tyrosinemia "boiled cabbage" odor.
- BA epidemiology: 1–3/10,000 in East Asia and French Polynesia vs ~0.5/10,000 in Europe/North America; syndromic BA 10–15% in North America/Europe vs < 1–3% in Asia/South America; ~50% need LT by 2 years and ~75% by 20 years after Kasai (pp. 1361, 1372–1373). Only PKD1L1 implicated (subset of BASM); Mendelian genetics not thought to cause isolated BA.
- Post-Kasai: steroids, IVIG and rituximab "largely unsuccessful"; customized steroids and antibiotics "require validation"; NAC and IBAT inhibitors under study; management of steroids, antivirals, **antibiotic prophylaxis**, portal hypertension and LT timing varies by center (p. 1373). Suggests a formal RCT of antivirals in CMV-associated BA.
- Cholestasis frequency: ~1 in 2,500 term infants (p. 1361). PN-associated cholestasis can appear after ≥ 2 weeks of PN.

- **Target Kasai age:** FIGHS gives no target; "before 30 days" is cited from a meta-analysis — do not show FIGHS as recommending a day count.
