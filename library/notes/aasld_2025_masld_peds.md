## Answers to what was needed

1. **ALT thresholds for children < 10–12 years:**
   - **Statement 4 (p. 19):** validated, optimal screening cutoffs **for youth ≥ 10 years**: ALT **> 22 U/L girls, > 26 U/L boys** (sex-specific 95th percentile, NHANES; sensitivity 85% boys / 92% girls, specificity ~80%, p. 17).
   - **Statement 5 (p. 19):** children **< 10 years** *may be considered* for screening if evaluated/treated for overweight or obesity, with cardiometabolic risk factors, or family history of MASLD. **Optimal ALT thresholds are less validated under 10 years; ALT > 30 U/L "has been used in population-based studies".**
   - → "> 30 U/L under 10 years" is **not an AASLD recommended threshold**; it is reported as the cutoff used in studies (up to one third of young children in obesity programs had ALT > 30, p. 17; 8–35% in children ≤ 6 y, p. 8).
2. **"Under 10 years — look wider for other causes":** **not stated in these words.** The nearest text is **statement 9 (p. 26):** for persistently raised ALT do a full evaluation for the cause (Table 1: viral hepatitis, AIH, cardiometabolic tests; medication review — Table 2; substance/alcohol screen from age 12), and **consider inborn errors of metabolism when atypical**: early onset (**particularly before 3 years**), rapidly progressive or acute disease, **no overweight/obesity**, or other-organ (especially neurologic) involvement (Table 3). MASLD in a child is **presumptive until other causes of steatotic liver disease are excluded** (p. 26).
3. **Screening — who and when:**
   - **Statement 3:** screen children **≥ 10 years** with **obesity**, or with **overweight plus cardiometabolic risk factors or family history** (aligned with AAP 2023 obesity guideline).
   - **Statement 4:** use **ALT**, not imaging (ultrasound/CAP/MRI unsuitable for screening). Incidental steatosis on imaging → check aminotransferases.
   - **Statement 6:** no data on re-screening interval; **suggest annual ALT** for children ≥ 10 y who stay at risk.
   - **Statement 7:** single raised ALT in a well child → **repeat within 3 months**; refer if persistently raised or any sign of severe liver disease.

## Other facts useful for cards

- **Statement 8 (p. 25):** ALT > 26 boys / > 22 girls in a child with obesity, overweight or cardiometabolic risk → triggers MASLD evaluation; recheck within 3 months to confirm chronicity; if acute hepatitis is suspected, **recheck in 24–48 h and refer urgently**.
- **Statement 10:** imaging to support steatosis — **MRI-PDFF** when quantification/precision needed; **ultrasound** first when MRI-PDFF unavailable or anatomy matters; **CAP should not be used** to diagnose MASLD in children (no pediatric validation).
- **Statement 11:** full cardiometabolic evaluation — BMI percentile/z-score, fasting glucose or HbA1c, lipids, blood pressure.
- **Statement 12:** consider **biopsy** when uncertain, especially ALT persistently **≥ 2 × ULN (≥ 52 boys, ≥ 44 girls)**, concern for fibrosis/MASH, or positive autoantibodies (ANA, ASMA, high IgG) suggesting AIH (which can coexist).
- Diagnostic accuracy of ALT: > 35–40 U/L raises specificity to ≥ 90% but sensitivity falls to ~50%; ~2 × ULN (40 girls / 50 boys) specificity 92–96%, sensitivity 32–48%; up to 25% of children with NAFLD had ALT < 54 (p. 17).
- MASLD definition: steatosis (imaging or biopsy) + ≥ 1 cardiometabolic risk factor; monogenic inborn errors excluded; can overlap with AIH, Wilson, alcohol (statement 1). Periportal pattern = "zone 1 MASH" (statement 2).
- Prevalence: US children ~9.6%; adolescents ~16% by ALT > 22/26; up to 26% with obesity; 30% of adolescents with presumed NAFLD in NHANES had a healthy weight (p. 8, 10).
