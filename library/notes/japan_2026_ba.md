# japan_2026_ba — full-text note

- **Source:** Okubo R, Kuroda T, Abukawa D, Nio M, Suzuki T, Uchida H, Yamataka A, Yoshida M; Japanese Biliary Atresia Society. Clinical Practice Guidelines for Biliary Atresia in Japan, 2nd Edition. J Hepatobiliary Pancreat Sci 2026;33:433-441, doi:10.1002/jhbp.70101
- **Read:** 2026-11-24 from the full text (9 pages, pp. 433–441). CQs and recommendations pp. 434–438; Discussion pp. 438–439.
- **Status of document:** **society guideline** of the Japanese Biliary Atresia Society (103 hospitals), developed by MINDS 2020 method: systematic review to July 2022 (616 articles), modified Delphi (consensus = > 70% approval with ≥ 75% participation). 23 CQs, recommendations for 22. Strength 1 strong / 2 weak / 3 weakly against / 4 strongly against; evidence A–D. **Supersedes the 1st edition** (Ando 2021, J Hepatobiliary Pancreat Sci 28:55). English text gives **only the CQ section** — the Japanese book (Herusu Shuppan 2024) has the review section, doses and details. Valid up to 5 years. 7 of 23 CQs are strong despite weak (C) evidence (p. 438).

## Answers to what was needed (cholangitis after Kasai)

1. **Early postoperative antibiotics (CQ 9, p. 436):** to prevent early cholangitis, **IV antibiotics for about 2 weeks after Kasai, then long-term oral antibiotics**. **Strength 2 (weak), evidence C**, agreement 94%.
2. **Prophylaxis (CQ 13, p. 437):** prophylactic antimicrobials after surgery are proposed. **Strength 2 (weak), evidence C**, agreement 95%. Reason: reported reduction in cholangitis and better native-liver survival, no clear harm.
   - Discussion (p. 438): CQ 9 = immediate postoperative period; CQ 13 = longer-term prophylaxis. **Optimal duration of oral prophylaxis and the drug are not specified.**
3. **Treatment duration of cholangitis:** **not addressed** in any CQ.
4. **Steroids / choleretics for cholangitis (CQ 14, p. 437):** for **late** postoperative cholangitis, in addition to antibiotics, **steroids or other choleretics according to symptoms — weakly recommended (strength 2)**; **fasting — weakly not recommended (strength 3)**; evidence **D**, agreement 80%. Reason: limited evidence, no major adverse events reported; no evidence for fasting.
5. **Refractory cholangitis (CQ 22, p. 438):** **liver transplant recommended** for refractory cholangitis and treatment-resistant portal hypertension — strength 1, evidence C, agreement 76%.
6. **Intrahepatic bile duct dilatation / bile lakes (CQ 15, p. 437):** postoperative **biliary drainage** proposed — strength 2, evidence D (relieves cholangitis, no harm to later LT reported).
- **Is it the first society guideline on post-Kasai cholangitis?** It is a society guideline with graded positions on prophylaxis (CQ 9, 13) and adjuncts (CQ 14). The 1st edition (2021) already existed; this edition updates it.

## Other facts useful for cards

- **CQ 7 — Kasai within 30 days of birth: recommended, strength 1 (strong), evidence B**, agreement 77% (p. 436). Reason: higher native-liver survival for at least 15 years and better jaundice clearance. Discussion: KP as early as possible, **ideally before 30 days**; 1-month screening does not mean waiting until 1 month (pp. 438–439). Upgraded from strength 2 in the 1st edition.
- **CQ 8 — steroids after Kasai: recommended, strength 1, evidence B**, agreement 73% (p. 436); adverse events minor, better native-liver survival and clearance reported. **Upgraded from "none" in the 1st edition** (p. 439).
- CQ 10 — UDCA after Kasai: weakly recommended (2C).
- CQ 11 — redo Kasai: weakly recommended (2C) only after temporary clearance with recurrence, or good early flow that stopped suddenly; do not delay LT.
- CQ 12 — laparoscopic Kasai: no recommendation (D); 13.8% of registered cases since 2018.
- CQ 1 — screening at about 1 month: strongly recommended (1B). CQ 2 — examine infants with **pale-yellow** stools (many have pale-yellow, not acholic, stools before 1 month): 1C. CQ 3 — examine infants with prolonged jaundice and hepatomegaly: 1C.
- CQ 4 — intraoperative cholangiography to classify type: 1C. CQ 5 — liver biopsy for differential diagnosis: **weakly recommended, "limited" (2D)** — reversed from "not recommended" (strength 3) in the 1st edition, because of genetic testing advances (p. 439). CQ 6 — pathology of hilar tissue: 1C.
- Ultrasound: triangular cord sign is the key feature, but many neonatal cases do not reach the ≥ 4 mm criterion and some subtypes lack a hilar mass; if BA is not excluded, **direct cholangiography early** (p. 439).
- CQ 16 — check for varices from infancy by appropriate method (2D); CQ 20 — prophylactic variceal therapy (2D); CQ 21 — hypersplenism therapy (2D, downgraded from 1); CQ 19 — liver tumor screening in native-liver survivors (1C; cholangiocarcinoma has poor prognosis); CQ 18 — multidisciplinary pregnancy care (1C); CQ 17 — LT for growth failure (2C); CQ 23 — primary LT for advanced disease at diagnosis, limited (2D). LT eligibility now judged by varices/portal hypertension, not PELD (p. 439). Portopulmonary hypertension: needs a follow-up program (p. 439).
- Removed from 2nd edition: preoperative vitamin K, HPS and pulmonary hypertension CQs (moved to the review section) (p. 438).
