# emerick_1999_alagille — full-text note

- **Source:** Emerick KM, Rand EB, Goldmuntz E, Krantz ID, Spinner NB, Piccoli DA. Features of Alagille syndrome in 92 patients: frequency and relation to prognosis. Hepatology 1999;29:822-829, doi:10.1002/hep.510290331
- **Read:** 2026-11-24 from the full text (8 pages, pp. 822–829). Histology p. 826; scintigraphy and cholangiography p. 825–826; outcomes pp. 825–828.
- **Status of document:** retrospective case series, 92 patients (83 probands + 9 affected relatives), CHOP / Chicago / referrals from 20 US centers, records 1974–1997. Clinical diagnosis (≥ 3 of 5 major features), pre-genetic era. Authors note bias toward more severely affected individuals (p. 828).

## Answers to what was needed

1. **60% / 95% (paucity by age at biopsy)** — **confirmed.** Cross-sectional analysis of 88 biopsies from 57 patients: paucity in **29 of 48 (60%)** biopsies taken **before 6 months** vs **38 of 40 (95%)** after 6 months; P < .001 (p. 826). Longitudinal: in 27 patients with repeat biopsies (same pathologist), paucity (P = .002), fibrosis (P = .001) and fibrosis severity (P < .001) increased; but repeat biopsies were done for progressive disease (6 from explants) — a severe subset. On the later biopsy, bridging fibrosis 13/27 (48%), cirrhosis 7/27 (26%) (p. 826).
   - Paucity definition used here: interlobular duct : portal tract ratio **< 0.4**; ~10 portal tracts said to be needed, but diagnosis possible with fewer (p. 823).
   - Overall: 81 of 92 had ≥ 1 biopsy; **69/81 (85%)** showed paucity; the other 12 showed cholestasis, portal inflammation, fibrosis and sometimes ductular proliferation (p. 826).
2. **22 / 36 (scintigraphy)** — **confirmed.** Hepatobiliary scintigraphy in 36 infants: normal excretion into bowel by 4 h in **5/36 (14%)**; delayed but present by 24 h in **9/36 (25%)**; **no evident excretion after 24 h in 22/36 (61%)**. Uptake present in all (mildly abnormal in 6). Phenobarbital and UDCA were **not** routinely given before the scan (p. 825). Absent excretion did **not** predict transplant or death (abstract; p. 827).

## Other facts useful for cards

- alagille / chol_3_12m — feature frequency (Table 1, p. 823; denominators differ): paucity 85% (69/81), chronic cholestasis 96% (88/92), murmur 97% (90/92), butterfly vertebrae 51% (37/71), characteristic facies 96% (86/92), posterior embryotoxon 78% (65/83; Axenfeld 13%), renal disease 40% (28/69), growth retardation 87% (27/31), developmental (gross motor) delay 16%, intellectual disability 2%. Pancreatic insufficiency 7/17 (separate study). Five major features in 25%, four in 53%, three in 22%.
- Chronic cholestasis defined as direct bilirubin > 1.0 mg/dL beyond 6 months (p. 822). All tested infants had high GGT, ALP and bile acids with normal or mildly raised aminotransferases. Hepatomegaly 93%, splenomegaly 70% at some point; xanthomas 42%; severe pruritus ~45% in the first decade, improving later; fractures/rickets 12/92 (p. 823).
- Heart: formal cardiology in 73 — pulmonary-tree stenosis 49/73 (67%), mostly peripheral pulmonic stenosis; **structural intracardiac disease 22/92 (24%)** (TOF 10, 4 with pulmonary atresia) (p. 823).
- Renal (Table 2): renal tubular acidosis commonest (10); small hyperechoic kidneys 14; infantile renal insufficiency 5 (resolved by 6 months) (p. 824).
- Intracranial bleeding 13/92 (14%), 4 of 13 died; 6 linked to head trauma that was minor relative to the bleed; only 2 had coagulopathy; no risk factor or location pattern; MRI in 21 showed nothing predictive (p. 824, 827).
- **Kasai in Alagille:** 7 infants had a Kasai for presumed BA (non-excreting scan + hepatitis/ductular proliferation on biopsy); every intraoperative cholangiogram showed gallbladder-to-duodenum drainage with hypoplastic/non-visualized proximal ducts; 3/7 (43%) later transplanted vs 16/85 (19%) without Kasai. Authors: Kasai should be avoided in AGS (p. 827). Operative cholangiograms (n = 19): intrahepatic ducts not visualized in 14/19 (74%) (p. 825–826).
- Outcomes: LT in 19/92 (21%), median age 72 months; 79% 1-year post-LT survival; 5 retransplants (3 for hepatic artery thrombosis). Mortality 16/92 (17%): hepatic/LT 4 (25%), intracranial bleeds 4 (25%), cardiac/multisystem 3 (Table 4). Predicted survival to 20 years: 75% all, 80% without LT, 60% with LT; **40%** with vs **80%** without structural intracardiac disease (Fig. 1–2).
- Prognosis: only major intracardiac disease predicted mortality (P < .001). In infants, conjugated bilirubin > 5 mg/dL at presentation was linked to death (36% vs 0%), but this disappeared after excluding complex heart disease (P = .375) (p. 827).
- No hepatocellular carcinoma in the series (p. 825).

## Conflicts with what the app says now

- **ref_biopsy:** "Paucity in 60% of biopsies before 6 months and 95% after" — **correct** (29/48 vs 38/40). Could add that the later biopsies were a cross-sectional, partly severe sample.
- **ref_imaging:** "22 of 36 infants did not excrete by 24 hours" — **correct** (61%). The paper's wording is "no evident excretion after 24 hours".
- **drafts/chol_3_12m.md (paucity definition):** the draft uses "ducts/portal tracts < 0.9 on ≥ 10 tracts". Emerick 1999 used **< 0.4**. Not an error in the app, but if a ratio is shown, name its source — the threshold differs between studies.
- No other conflicts: the drafts' feature frequencies, 21% LT, 17% mortality, 75/80/60% and 40% vs 80% match the full text.
