## Answers to what was needed

### 1. All pediatric recommendations

**Panel 1 — diagnosis / non-invasive tests (p. 995):**
- **1.24** In children, **extrahepatic portal vein obstruction (EHPVO)** and **ACLD from biliary atresia** are the two leading causes of portal hypertension (LoE 1; 95%).
- **1.25** Liver stiffness (LSM) helps identify children at risk of portal hypertension from ACLD non-invasively and to monitor progression (LoE 3; **strong**; 97%).
- **1.26** In children, portal hypertension management **cannot rely solely on LSM and/or spleen stiffness**, because cutoffs for presence/absence of varices are not validated (LoE 3; **strong**; 100%).
- **1.27** Combining several NITs — e.g. the **clinical prediction rule (age-adjusted spleen-length Z-score, platelets, albumin)** — may help predict high-risk varices and set endoscopy intervals (LoE 3; 100%; no strength given).
- Research agenda RA1.9–1.11: NIT criteria to rule out varices in children, NIT models to predict bleeding — **not yet available**.

**Panel 9 — chronic PVT (EHPVO) in children (p. 1011–1012):**
- **9.29** Chronic PVT in children = complete obstruction of normal portal flow to the liver; congenital or acquired; diagnosed on imaging (LoE 3; 96%).
- **9.30** Work-up: standard evaluation for underlying chronic liver disease; **liver biopsy may be considered** to exclude intrinsic liver disease; **thrombophilia work-up recommended** (LoE 3; strong; 98%).
- **9.31** With features of portal hypertension, **Meso-Rex bypass should be considered for pre-primary (no varices yet) and primary prophylaxis**; > 90% success requires established criteria (patent intrahepatic portal system, surgical expertise) (LoE 3; strong; 100%).
- **9.32** Other primary prophylaxis (drugs, endoscopy, interventional radiology) — **role not well established** (LoE 4; 95%).
- **9.33** Variceal management may need to be individualized for children without ready access to care (LoE 4; weak; 90%).
- **9.34** **Secondary prophylaxis: Meso-Rex preferred**; if not feasible → portal vein recanalization, endoscopic therapy ± NSBB, or distal splenorenal shunt (LoE 3; strong; 100%).
- **9.35** Distal splenorenal shunt for recurrent variceal bleeding **if no HPS, PoPH or hepatic encephalopathy** (LoE 3; strong; 93%).
- **9.36** With portal hypertension and/or portosystemic shunts (spontaneous or created): **annual surveillance** for shunt complications, **especially portopulmonary hypertension** (LoE 4; strong; 93%).
- **9.37** Consider **liver transplantation** for life-threatening, otherwise untreatable portal-hypertensive complications (LoE 3; strong; 100%).
- **9.38** Recent PVT in **neonates with umbilical catheters**: may resolve spontaneously; anticoagulation role uncertain; **monitor for clot extension**; consider anticoagulation for extensive thrombus (LoE 4; weak; 100%).
- **9.39** Cirrhosis-associated PVT is uncommon in children; evidence to apply adult recommendations is insufficient (LoE 5; 93%).

### 2. Prevention and treatment of variceal bleeding in children
- Pediatric statements cover **EHPVO** (Meso-Rex for pre-primary/primary/secondary prophylaxis; other primary prophylaxis not established). There are **no pediatric-specific statements on acute variceal bleeding management** (vasoactive drugs, antibiotics, timing of endoscopy) or on primary prophylaxis in **cirrhotic** children (e.g. biliary atresia) — the document gives none; adult statements are not declared applicable to children.

### 3. Non-invasive CSPH criteria and applicability to children
- Adult rules (cACLD): LSM 10–15 kPa suggestive and > 15 kPa highly suggestive of cACLD; **LSM ≤ 15 kPa and platelets ≥ 150 × 10⁹/L rule out CSPH** (NPV > 90%) and endoscopy can be avoided (1.12–1.15, Fig. "NIT algorithm"); spleen stiffness may refine classification (1.13); NSBBs, preferably carvedilol, when CSPH is present (adult).
- **Children: not applicable as stand-alone** — statement 1.26 (strong): pediatric LSM/SSM cutoffs for varices are not validated; use combinations (1.27).

## Other facts useful for cards

- splenomegaly_ph: EHPVO and BA-related ACLD are the two main causes in children (1.24, LoE 1); look for thrombophilia and consider biopsy in EHPVO (9.30); **refer early for Meso-Rex evaluation** (9.31, 9.34).
- hps_poph: annual screening for PoPH in any child with portal hypertension or portosystemic shunt (9.36); distal splenorenal shunt contraindicated by HPS/PoPH/HE (9.35).
- ref_elasto: LSM useful for trend and risk in pediatric ACLD (1.25) but not for deciding about varices (1.26).
- varix_bleed: no pediatric acute-management statements — keep adult-derived steps labeled as such.

## Conflicts with what the app says now

- **ref_elasto:** any line implying adult Baveno VII rule-out (LSM < 15 + PLT > 150) can be applied to children should carry "adult rule; not validated in children (Baveno VIII 1.26)".
- **splenomegaly_ph / varix_bleed (future cards):** use Baveno VIII instead of Baveno VII for pediatric rows (Baveno VI pediatric 2016 — `baveno_vi_peds_2016` — remains the more detailed pediatric source for acute bleeding; Baveno VIII does not replace it on that topic).
- **hps_poph:** Baveno VIII supports annual PoPH surveillance with shunts; it gives no SpO2 threshold.
