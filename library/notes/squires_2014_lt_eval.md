## Answers to what was needed

- **Cholangitis prophylaxis after Kasai — exact position:** a **narrative sentence in the background text, not a numbered recommendation and without a grade** (p. 371). It says prophylactic antibiotic regimens with **trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole or neomycin** reduce recurrent cholangitis and **improve survival**, citing two references: **Bu LN et al., J Pediatr Surg 2003;38:590-593** (ref. 137) and **Luo Y, Zheng S., World J Pediatr 2008;4:14-19** (ref. 138, a narrative review). → Ravanbakhsh 2024's reading ("cite one study") is essentially right: one small primary study (Bu 2003) plus one review. No evidence level is attached; recommendations 32–35 for BA do not mention prophylaxis.
- **Is it in the 2026 update?** Per the existing note `aasld_ast_naspghan_2026_lt_eval`, the 2026 update does **not** address prophylaxis (recurrent cholangitis only as a referral trigger). So this 2014 sentence is the only North American guideline-document mention, and it was dropped in 2026.

## Other facts useful for cards (BA section, p. 370–371)

- Kasai "by 8 to 10 weeks of age" called optimal for transplant-free survival beyond early childhood (background text).
- Poor outcome predictors before Kasai: vitamin K-unresponsive coagulopathy, hypoalbuminemia, advanced cirrhosis on histology, ascites, portal hypertension, poor nutrition.
- Up to 70% may have prolonged transplant-free survival if total bilirubin falls **< 2 mg/dL within 3 months** after Kasai.
- UDCA/choleretic regimens after Kasai "not standardized".
- Beta-blockers for primary variceal prophylaxis in children called controversial.
- Recurrent cholangitis, with or without decompensation, "can be an indication" for LT (ref. Inoue 2008, bile lakes).
- ≥ 80% of BA patients transplanted by 20 years, most under 4 years; US 10-year graft / patient survival after LT for BA 73% / 86%.
- **Rec 32:** Kasai is preferred initial management (1-B); consider LT evaluation if decompensated before Kasai (2-B).
- **Rec 33:** aggressive nutritional support before LT (1-B).
- **Rec 34:** refer promptly for LT evaluation if total bilirubin **> 6 mg/dL beyond 3 months** after Kasai (1-B); consider evaluation at **2–6 mg/dL** (1-B), and at lower values with unmanageable cirrhosis/portal hypertension consequences (2-B).
- **Rec 35:** **high-dose corticosteroids started within 72 h of Kasai are not recommended (1-B)** — based on START (Bezerra, abstract 2013).
- PALF entry criteria (p. 372): no known chronic liver disease; liver-based coagulopathy not corrected by parenteral vitamin K; INR 1.5–1.9 with encephalopathy or ≥ 2.0 regardless.
