## Answers to what was needed

1. **HPS screening — SpO2 threshold and posture:**
   - **Statement 9 (Strong, Level 2), p. 8:** all children undergoing LT evaluation → screen for HPS with **pulse oximetry upright and supine**. Text (p. 9): **all children with chronic liver disease** should be screened this way.
   - **No SpO2 number** is given anywhere in the document (neither < 96% nor < 97%). The text notes pulse oximetry is **insensitive in children**.
   - Positive screen (symptoms or hypoxemia) → **contrast-enhanced TTE** with agitated saline (bubbles in the left atrium within **3–6 cardiac cycles**); **99mTc-MAA** lung perfusion scan quantifies shunting and may be more sensitive (p. 9).
   - HPS in 4–47% and PoPH in 1–5% of children listed for LT (small series).
2. **Orthodeoxia ≥ 4%:** **not in the text.** Orthodeoxia and platypnea are listed only as clinical features of HPS.
3. **PoPH — mPAP threshold as contraindication:** **no number.** Diagnosis: right-heart catheterization (gold standard) for suspected moderate–severe pulmonary hypertension — **statement 11** (raised mPAP from raised PVR with normal wedge pressure). **Statement 10 (Strong, Level 3):** 2D echo with Doppler + ECG in **all** candidates. **Table 5:** "severe PoPH unresponsive to medical therapy" = **absolute contraindication**, without an mPAP value.
4. **Referral criteria (replacing Squires 2014):**
   - **Statement 31 (Strong, Level 3):** children with chronic liver disease who have a **decompensating event** → consider LT evaluation.
   - **Statement 32 (Strong, Level 3):** also refer for **uncorrectable coagulopathy, growth failure, significant pruritus, or recurrent cholangitis despite maximal medical therapy**.
   - Text (p. 13): refer when complications become refractory **but before** disease is unmanageable; triggers include recurrent cholangitis, portal-hypertensive GI bleeding, recurrent/refractory encephalopathy, growth failure, intractable pruritus, poor quality of life.
   - **Biliary atresia — statement 54 (Strong, Level 3):** HPE first; **primary LT evaluation** if decompensated liver disease at presentation (HPE unlikely to succeed and may worsen outcome). **Statement 55 (Strong, Level 3):** start LT evaluation after HPE when there are significant liver-related complications, **regardless of bilirubin**.
   - Text (p. 21): better transplant-free survival when HPE is done **before 30 days** and drains bile (**total bilirubin < 2 mg/dL at 3 months**); **bilirubin > 6 mg/dL at 3 months** → high likelihood of LT within 2 years. (No explicit "refer at TB ≥ 2 / > 6" rule as a statement.)
   - **Statement 56:** aggressive nutritional support before LT in cholestatic disease. **Statement 57:** multidisciplinary multisystem evaluation in genetic cholestasis (Alagille: ~50% eventually need LT; check heart, kidneys, cerebral vessels — intracranial bleeding risk).
5. **Cholangitis after Kasai — antibiotic prophylaxis:** **not addressed.** "Prophylaxis" appears only for peri-transplant infections (MDRO screening, statement 14). Recurrent cholangitis appears only as a **referral trigger** (statement 32; p. 21 for BA).
6. **Recurrent cholangitis as a reason to refer:** yes — statement 32 (all CLD), p. 21 (BA, even with intermediate bilirubin), statement 65 (PSC: recurrent cholangitis, intractable pruritus, suspected cholangiocarcinoma; Strong, Level 4).

## Other facts useful for cards

- **Statement 5 (Strong, Level 5):** abdominal **Doppler ultrasound** is first-line imaging; **statement 6:** cross-sectional imaging selectively.
- **Statement 13 (Strong, Level 5):** infants < 12 months — positive CMV IgG may be maternal; with a CMV-positive donor treat as high risk (D+/R−).
- **Statements 15–21:** full vaccination before LT; live vaccines (MMR, varicella — 2–3 doses at 1-month intervals, Strong, Level 5) when feasible; **inactivated** polio only; influenza for listed children > 6 months and all household members (Strong, Level 2).
- Table 5 absolute contraindications include HCC with extrahepatic metastases, severe PoPH unresponsive to therapy, primary HLH, valproate-induced ALF in > 10 y (mitochondrial), uncorrectable multi-organ disease (see table).
- **Statements 33–34:** all children with ALF → transfer to/consult an LT center early; LT centers must respond promptly to transfer requests. CHALF model C-statistic 0.83 vs PELD 0.76 (not prospectively validated).
- **Statement 64:** LT for AIH not responding to therapy or with ESLD complications.
