# aap_2022_hyperbili — full-text note

- **Source:** Kemper AR et al. Clinical Practice Guideline Revision: Management of Hyperbilirubinemia in the Newborn Infant 35 or More Weeks of Gestation. Pediatrics 2022;150:e2022058859, doi:10.1542/peds.2022-058859
- **Read:** 2026-11-21 from the full text (PDF pp. 1–28: guideline, figures, key-action-statement tables). Cholestasis content: section II.E "Evaluating Elevated Direct-Reacting or Conjugated Bilirubin Concentrations" (p. 6–7) and KAS 9 (p. 7; evidence profile p. 26).
- **Status of document:** AAP clinical practice guideline (key action statements, KAS 1–21, with AAP evidence grades). Infants ≥ 35 weeks. Focus is unconjugated hyperbilirubinemia; cholestasis is covered only as a screening/referral prompt.

## Answers to what was needed

1. **"Conjugated > 0.3 mg/dL" and "direct > 1.0 mg/dL" — are they in the text?** Yes, both, but as **background, not as an AAP threshold** (p. 7, section II.E):
   - Direct > 1.0 mg/dL is attributed to the joint NASPGHAN/ESPGHAN recommendation (ref. 78 = Fawaz 2017).
   - A cutoff of ≥ 0.3 mg/dL "has been used" for **conjugated** bilirubin (ref. 76 = Harpavat), described as only about the **95th percentile**.
   - AAP adds: because biliary atresia is rare (1 in 14,000), > 99% of infants with a **single** raised direct/conjugated value do **not** have biliary atresia; positive predictive value improves a lot with a **repeat measurement within a few days to 2 weeks**; a **rising** value suggests pathologic cholestasis that needs evaluation.
   - The old rule "direct > 20% of total" is **no longer regarded as necessary** for diagnosing cholestasis (p. 7, citing ref. 78).
   - Direct and conjugated bilirubin are **different tests**: direct is higher and more variable than conjugated and rises with total bilirubin; reference ranges for direct vary by laboratory (p. 6).
   - → AAP itself sets **no numeric cholestasis threshold**. Citing "AAP 2022" for "conjugated > 0.3 mg/dL" as a cutoff is inaccurate; at most: "AAP 2022 notes 0.3 mg/dL (≈ 95th percentile) has been used for conjugated bilirubin; NASPGHAN/ESPGHAN: direct > 1.0 mg/dL".
2. **When to fractionate (KAS 9, p. 7 and p. 26):** measure total **and** direct-reacting (or conjugated) bilirubin in **breastfed infants still jaundiced at 3–4 weeks** and **formula-fed infants still jaundiced at 2 weeks**. Evidence grade **X, Recommendation** (evidence profile: "exceptional situation"; parents may prefer testing at the 2-week visit rather than returning at 4 weeks).
3. **Who to call:** formula-fed infants with any prolonged jaundice, or breastfed infants with direct/conjugated hyperbilirubinemia → **consult a gastroenterologist or other expert** (p. 7).

## Other facts useful for cards

- unconj_bili / neo_jaundice: breast-milk jaundice (prolonged unconjugated) can last **up to 3 months**, is almost always nonpathologic and **not associated with direct/conjugated hyperbilirubinemia** (p. 3).
- unconj_bili: do **not subtract** direct/conjugated bilirubin from TSB when using phototherapy thresholds; if direct/conjugated **> 50% of TSB** in severe hyperbilirubinemia → consult an expert (p. 8; Fig 2–3 legends).
- unconj_bili: rapid TSB rise (≥ 0.3 mg/dL per hour in the first 24 h or ≥ 0.2 mg/dL per hour after) suggests hemolysis → DAT if not done (KAS 7, p. 6; Grade D, Option).
- unconj_bili: neurotoxicity risk factors = GA < 38 weeks, albumin < 3.0 g/dL, isoimmune hemolysis, G6PD deficiency or other hemolysis, sepsis, significant clinical instability in the past 24 h (Fig 2–3 legends, p. 8–9).
- Infants < 24 h old at/above phototherapy threshold likely have hemolysis (Fig 2 legend, p. 8).
- Prolonged indirect hyperbilirubinemia after phototherapy can be confirmed with a fractionated bilirubin; indirect = total − direct/conjugated (p. 10).

## Conflicts with what the app says now

- **c_conj_bili / neo_jaundice (D-2026-11-11-2):** threshold "direct or conjugated ≥ 1.0 mg/dL" — consistent with the NASPGHAN/ESPGHAN figure that AAP quotes. AAP's own message adds "repeat within days to 2 weeks; a rise matters" — consider a pearl.
- **unconj_bili (D-2026-11-18-1):** the earlier "conjugated > 0.3 mg/dL (AAP 2022)" line was removed and replaced by "above lab normal" — the full text supports that decision: 0.3 is a cited research cutoff (95th percentile), not an AAP recommendation.
- **neo_jaundice:** fractionation timing "2 weeks; 3 weeks if breastfed" — AAP says **3–4 weeks** for breastfed (2 weeks formula-fed). NASPGHAN/ESPGHAN (Fawaz 2017) says 2 weeks for all, with 3 weeks for breastfed if well — see `naspghan_espghan_2017_nc` note. Show both if they differ in the card.
